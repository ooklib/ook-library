Extensive US intelligence/defense career; Gerb calls her online footprint "largely a ghost," with
sparse public information beyond an NRO "Leaders of the NRO" volume 3 celebration and old Senate
Select Committee on Intelligence (SSCI) documents. *([nro](../raw/GM1Qx9vuo2c_nro.md)
2:47:12–2:47:45)*

## Claims

- Worked at [[bdm-international|BDM]] (Braddock, Dunn & McDonald) on defense/arms-control
  research and technology transfer before government service.
  *([nro](../raw/GM1Qx9vuo2c_nro.md) 2:47:45–2:48:15)*
- Began CIA career in 1985 at the Directorate of Intelligence's Technology Transfer Assessment
  Center, focused on Soviet acquisition of Western dual-use technology; later worked in the CIA
  Counter Intelligence Center, then 8 years at SSCI (evaluating tactical military programs,
  advising on CIA programs, serving as budget director), then back to CIA as agency comptroller.
  *([nro](../raw/GM1Qx9vuo2c_nro.md) 2:48:19–2:49:13)*
- Appointed in 2001 as special assistant to the president and senior director for intelligence
  programs, National Security Council, under George W. Bush; then NRO deputy director for
  national support, 2004–2006. *([nro](../raw/GM1Qx9vuo2c_nro.md) 2:49:13–2:49:31)*
- Served as Lockheed Martin VP of government affairs (2006–2011) then VP of intelligence, joint,
  and science and technology programs (2011–2015) — active during the 2010–2011 "Kona Blue"
  technology-transfer attempt. Gerb wagers her NSC/CIA-DS&T allegiances would have commanded her
  to aid [[glenn-gaffney|Glenn Gaffney]] in blocking the transfer.
  *([nro](../raw/GM1Qx9vuo2c_nro.md) 2:49:39–2:50:18)*
- Gerb notes she and [[christopher-mellon|Christopher Mellon]] worked closely together at SSCI
  for years, but Mellon reportedly declined Gerb's request for help questioning her.
  *([nro](../raw/GM1Qx9vuo2c_nro.md) 2:50:18–2:50:35)*
