Walter Haut was the public relations officer of the 509th Bomb Group at Roswell Army Air Field in 1947 and wrote the 8 July 1947 press release announcing a captured flying disc. He signed two affidavits, one in 1993 and a sealed one in 2002 released after his 2005 death. [[uap-gerb]] calls him one of the best Roswell whistleblowers. All below are Haut's claims as relayed by Gerb.

## Claims (gerb/whistleblowers-1)

- Credentials per Gerb: WWII bombardier with 35 missions over Japan, took part in Bikini atomic tests, served in the 509th, the wing that dropped the atomic bombs. He stayed silent until the early 1990s and signed his first affidavit on 14 May 1993. *([gerb/whistleblowers-1](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-05-05 - UFO Whistleblowers [Vol.1].md>) 5:29-6:16)*
- In the 1993 affidavit Haut says Col. William Blanchard, the base commander, dictated the press release almost verbatim (a flying saucer found on a ranch north of Roswell, to be flown to Gen. Ramey's office) and told him to deliver it to the two Roswell newspapers and two radio stations "post haste"; Haut says the response to Blanchard was always "yes sir." Material was loaded onto a C-54, and Haut says the aircraft flew but the material did not. *([gerb/whistleblowers-1](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-05-05 - UFO Whistleblowers [Vol.1].md>) 6:16-8:30)*
- Haut was stunned by the retraction to a weather balloon by [[roger-ramey]] and believed neither Blanchard nor [[jesse-marcel]] could have mistaken the wreckage for a balloon. Marcel admitted his involvement in a 1978 interview and privately to Haut in 1980. *([gerb/whistleblowers-1](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-05-05 - UFO Whistleblowers [Vol.1].md>) 8:39-9:03)*
- Corroboration cited by Gerb: the affidavit of Arthur McQuiddy, managing editor of the Roswell Morning Dispatch, confirming the press release and the later call from the base saying it was wrong, and quoting Blanchard months later: "the stuff I saw I've never seen any place else in my life." Also the affidavit of Fort Worth Army Air Field Col. Thomas J. DuBose, saying the material shown in Ramey's office was a weather balloon used as a cover story and that he arranged for the real material to go to Gen. Chidlaw at Wright Field. *([gerb/whistleblowers-1](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-05-05 - UFO Whistleblowers [Vol.1].md>) 9:03-10:08)*
- The 2002 affidavit (released 2007): at a 8 July staff meeting with Blanchard, Ramey and DuBose, samples of a lightweight, shape-retaining alloy with strange markings were passed around, and Ramey proposed drawing attention to the debris field to divert it from the more important craft site north of town. *([gerb/whistleblowers-1](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-05-05 - UFO Whistleblowers [Vol.1].md>) 10:15-11:25)*
- Haut says Blanchard took him to Building 84, a heavily guarded B-29 hangar, where he saw an egg-shaped craft 12 to 15 ft long and 6 ft in diameter with a metallic surface and no windows, portholes, wings, landing gear or tail, and under a canvas tarp a couple of bodies about 4 ft tall (child-sized) with larger-than-normal heads, in a temporary morgue. *([gerb/whistleblowers-1](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-05-05 - UFO Whistleblowers [Vol.1].md>) 11:25-12:30)*
- Haut says he never told earlier because he swore to Blanchard he would not reveal it in his lifetime. Gerb notes the egg-shaped craft resembles the one [[eric-taber]] described at Area 51. *([gerb/whistleblowers-1](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-05-05 - UFO Whistleblowers [Vol.1].md>) 12:06-12:56)*
