Lieutenant Colonel Philip J. Corso, author of *The Day After Roswell* and (per Gerb, the more
credible source) the manuscript *Dawn of a New Age*, claimed the Army's Foreign Technology
Division under Gen. [[arthur-trudeau]] was mandated to recruit trusted defense-industry partners
to adapt technologies discovered in the Roswell wreckage. *([gerb/mitre](../raw/gerb/mitre.md) 1:03:56–1:04:16)*

Gerb explicitly distinguishes Corso's own manuscript (which he finds "intriguing, worthy of
study, and likely accurate") from *The Day After Roswell*, which he says has "an incredible
amount of fluff inserted by the book's co-writer." *([gerb/mitre](../raw/gerb/mitre.md) 1:04:04–1:04:24)*

## Claims

- Technologies aided/inspired by Roswell materials per Corso: electromagnetic/anti-gravity
  propulsion, fiber optics, super-tenacity fibers, brain-machine interfaces, night vision, and
  guiding ballistic missiles by brain waves. *([gerb/mitre](../raw/gerb/mitre.md) 1:04:54–1:05:14, 2:08:01–2:08:18)*
- In an embedded interview clip (presumably archival Corso testimony), describes being given
  the job by "General Trudeau" of finding industry/scientists to feed Roswell-derived artifacts
  into normal R&D projects, with items marked in files as coming from New Mexico/Roswell; notes
  provisions allowed companies to patent resulting technology but required feeding some benefit
  back to the Army as competitive edge, and to the American people/world "if necessary."
  *([gerb/mitre](../raw/gerb/mitre.md) 1:05:47–1:06:56)*
- States Army R&D "began to flourish and enter a golden age" in 1958 — the same year
  [[mitre-corporation|MITRE]] was founded. *([gerb/mitre](../raw/gerb/mitre.md) 1:05:14–1:05:22)*
