Operation PAPER was, per Eyes Wide Open, a little-known CIA operation run mainly from Bangkok
between roughly 1951 and 1952 that secretly airlifted arms to General [[li-mi]]'s breakaway
Kuomintang (KMT) forces in Burma, ostensibly to let them re-invade China's Yunnan province. The
host argues it failed at that goal but "succeeded" in a more insidious way: the KMT remnant
became a self-sustaining opium-funded army, which he calls the blueprint for
[[narco-counterinsurgency]].

## Claims

- Authorization: NSC 342 (approved by [[harry-truman]] 3 March 1949) said the principal reliance
  against Kremlin influence in China should be "indigenous Chinese elements"; a Joint Chiefs memo of
  April 1950 called for covert operations in Southeast Asia; the [[office-of-policy-coordination]]
  draft was forwarded by the Joint Chiefs in November 1950 and Truman approved it. *([ewa/operation-paper](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2023-10-04 - THE HISTORY OF THE CIA - Operation PAPER and Trouble in the Golden Triangle [pt. 6].md) 13:51-14:35, 20:09-22:17)*
- Aim: per [[claire-chennault]], to trap China in "giant pincers" between the Korean front and a
  Burma front. *([ewa/operation-paper](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2023-10-04 - THE HISTORY OF THE CIA - Operation PAPER and Trouble in the Golden Triangle [pt. 6].md) 22:17-22:36)*
- Rare case of a White House-backed operation opposed by the DCI, [[walter-bedell-smith]], who
  the host says objected for bureaucratic reasons (he was folding OPC into the CIA hierarchy) and
  because he saw Li Mi's force as too weak and a diplomatic liability. Per [[peter-dale-scott]],
  Secretary of State Dean Acheson and the US embassies in Thailand and Burma were not told until
  at least 1951. *([ewa/operation-paper](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2023-10-04 - THE HISTORY OF THE CIA - Operation PAPER and Trouble in the Golden Triangle [pt. 6].md) 21:35-25:26)*
- Run out of Bangkok: [[paul-helliwell]] set up [[sea-supply-corporation]]; [[alfred-cox]] and
  Sherman Joost ("Sherman ju" in the ASR) ran it; [[willis-bird]] was the local point man. Thai
  police under [[phao-sriyanon]] handled deliveries. *([ewa/operation-paper](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2023-10-04 - THE HISTORY OF THE CIA - Operation PAPER and Trouble in the Golden Triangle [pt. 6].md) 25:41-35:38)*
- Burmese intelligence reportedly saw unmarked C-46/C-47s making at least five parachute drops a
  week in early 1951; per Li Mi's later testimony, weapons were traded for poppies that went to
  Bangkok refineries and dens. *([ewa/operation-paper](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2023-10-04 - THE HISTORY OF THE CIA - Operation PAPER and Trouble in the Golden Triangle [pt. 6].md) 36:03-36:32)*
- Unverified claim (host's inference): the operation likely supported Li Mi's poppy cultivation
  and opium exports so his force could operate off the books after US funding ended. *([ewa/operation-paper](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2023-10-04 - THE HISTORY OF THE CIA - Operation PAPER and Trouble in the Golden Triangle [pt. 6].md) 30:14-31:11)*
- Timeline: June 1951 incursion into Yunnan (about 4,000 men; captured an airbase roughly 60
  miles in, then repulsed); a CAT-supported Burmese bush airstrip built late 1951-early 1952; 700
  Taiwanese irregulars airlifted in November 1952 per McCoy; August 1952 second invasion of 2,100
  troops repulsed; official US aid ended July 1952 per Li Mi's 1953 interview. *([ewa/operation-paper](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2023-10-04 - THE HISTORY OF THE CIA - Operation PAPER and Trouble in the Golden Triangle [pt. 6].md) 36:44-39:38)*
- Diplomatic fallout: US Ambassador to Burma David Key's 15 Aug 1951 memo to Dean Rusk described
  unruly KMT looting and arms sales; Ambassador (William) Sebald's 2 Sep 1952 memo (ASR "William J
  seal") described the opium-for-supplies route through Thailand with "apparent tacit approval" of
  Thai authorities; Burma took the matter to the UN (March 1953, resolution April 1953); a
  four-nation Bangkok commission stalled; about 2,000 men were airlifted to Taiwan in Sept 1953
  and about 5,700 more plus Li Mi over the next 11 months by CAT; the rest stayed in Shan State.
  *([ewa/operation-paper](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2023-10-04 - THE HISTORY OF THE CIA - Operation PAPER and Trouble in the Golden Triangle [pt. 6].md) 37:15-38:33, 41:39-44:43)*
- Lessons the host draws: (1) opium as a self-sustaining off-the-books funding model; (2) a
  client state (Thailand) as stepping stone to the target state (echoed later in Honduras/Costa
  Rica for Nicaragua and Pakistan for Afghanistan); (3) proprietary firms ([[civil-air-transport]],
  [[sea-supply-corporation]]) that keep operating after official support ends. *([ewa/operation-paper](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2023-10-04 - THE HISTORY OF THE CIA - Operation PAPER and Trouble in the Golden Triangle [pt. 6].md) 47:54-49:51)*

- War Conspiracy 1: host frames it as the CIA's first rollback attempt through [[civil-air-transport]]: planning began in late 1950; Helliwell's Miami/Bangkok "C Supply Company" ([[sea-supply-corporation]]) fed US Army depot weapons to [[li-mi]]'s KMT troops in Shan State; opium was brought to market by the Thai Border Police; Li's forces made at least three cross-border raids into China in 1951-52, failing; the operation largely ended by 1953 but the KMT remained a CIA proxy in Burma through Vietnam; Scott and McCoy are the cited sources. *([ewa/war-conspiracy-1](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-29 - The Origins of the Military-Industrial Complex - War Conspiracy 1.md) 45:36-48:39)*
