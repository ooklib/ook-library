Gerb highlights Australia as the country with an abundance of declassified UFO and USO government documentation, including reports, papers and newspaper clippings on water-linked sightings. Cases are attributed to the files and press accounts as he relays them. *([gerb/uso-casebook](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-04-26 - USO Case Book_ Unidentified Submerged Objects Throughout History.md>) 11:03-11:25)*

## Claims

- The Royal Australian Air Force's first official "unknown" UAS (unusual aerial sighting), 23 January 1964, described large lights in the water off the northeast point of Groote Eylandt (transcribed "Groot Island"), Western Australia (as transcribed), with the compass going haywire and a rotating shadow making the lights pulsate; a report was drafted on magnetic disturbance and compass activity. Gerb says instrument tampering recurs across USO/UFO encounters. *([gerb/uso-casebook](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-04-26 - USO Case Book_ Unidentified Submerged Objects Throughout History.md>) 11:25-11:59)*
- Authorities began taking such sightings seriously in the 1950s-60s: newspaper clippings of civilian water encounters (a 1963 fisherman seeing a light shoot from the water, hover and vanish) were filed with government and military reports, and Australian intelligence tracked similar sightings. A 1967 incident had Department of Works staff and locals see lights rise from the sea to a great height, curve and return, many accompanied by three flashes (compare the City of Almaco case in [[blue-book-uso-cases]]). Lieutenant Commander A. F. Perry of Australian intelligence hypothesized oil-search experiments, which Gerb finds inconsistent since Australians, Soviets and Americans all report the same. *([gerb/uso-casebook](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-04-26 - USO Case Book_ Unidentified Submerged Objects Throughout History.md>) 11:59-13:17)*
- 1947, Port Augusta, South Australia: Frederick Walter and Emma Flav (spelling as transcribed) saw five gray oblong objects rise from the sea and move northwest to southeast, also seen by two railroad workers who described five white or light pink egg-shaped objects that quivered (compare the MV Marcala case). *([gerb/uso-casebook](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-04-26 - USO Case Book_ Unidentified Submerged Objects Throughout History.md>) 13:17-13:51)*
- Flying Saucer Review (July-August, 1966-67): private pilot C. Adams and television cameraman Les Hendy (as transcribed) reported four or five objects east of Fraser Island, two dark cigars up to 100 ft long with three smaller objects, which began to submerge as the aircraft approached within 2 miles; independent witnesses at a nearby hotel said the surface was not disturbed, which Gerb compares to the HMNZS Southland case discussed by [[kevin-knuth]]. *([gerb/uso-casebook](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-04-26 - USO Case Book_ Unidentified Submerged Objects Throughout History.md>) 13:51-15:05)*
