Plan Bleu ('Blue Plan') was, per Eyes Wide Open, the first confirmed Gladio operation in France: a 1946-47 secret far-right army of covert cells, organized by the [[sdece]] with CIA and British collaborators, which was revealed and foiled on 30 June 1947. *([ewa/pan-europa](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2023-07-28 - THE HISTORY OF THE CIA - France’s Secret Armies and the Pan-Europa Project [pt. 5.3].md) 14:54-16:02)*

## Claims

- Background: post-war France was polarized among the MRP (Christian Democrats), the French Communist Party (PCF, the second-largest Communist party in Western Europe per [[daniele-ganser]]) and the Socialists; the Communists won 26% nationally in October 1945 but were denied key cabinet posts. US ambassador Jefferson Caffery and [[hoyt-vandenberg]] warned Truman in 1946 that the Communists had 'sufficient strength to seize power in France', though Vandenberg noted they preferred legal means. *([ewa/pan-europa](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2023-07-28 - THE HISTORY OF THE CIA - France’s Secret Armies and the Pan-Europa Project [pt. 5.3].md) 9:06-14:50)*
- The French government abolished the tripartite Direction Générale and created the [[sdece]], 'filled with hardline cold warriors'; the SDECE, with CIA and SAS collaborators, allegedly organized Plan Bleu. *([ewa/pan-europa](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2023-07-28 - THE HISTORY OF THE CIA - France’s Secret Armies and the Pan-Europa Project [pt. 5.3].md) 15:22-15:55)*
- On 30 June 1947 Socialist Interior Minister [[edouard-depreux]] told Parliament that a secret far-right army was planning a coup, alleging CIA and British intelligence support. A French intelligence-led investigation concluded the arms 'had been paid in part by London and Washington' but were meant to resist Communists, not stage a coup. *([ewa/pan-europa](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2023-07-28 - THE HISTORY OF THE CIA - France’s Secret Armies and the Pan-Europa Project [pt. 5.3].md) 15:55-16:47)*
- Arrests included a minor Breton noble (ASR: 'Earl Edme de Vill Pian'), in whose castle police found heavy weaponry, battle orders and attack plans that reportedly contained an early mention of a 'strategy of tension'; the military police chief Maurice Guillaudot (ASR 'Guillando'); and [[georges-loustaunau-lacau]]. Both leaders were released after six months. *([ewa/pan-europa](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2023-07-28 - THE HISTORY OF THE CIA - France’s Secret Armies and the Pan-Europa Project [pt. 5.3].md) 16:47-19:34)*
- The failure did not deter further intelligence-agency work; a new stay-behind, [[rose-des-vents]], followed. *([ewa/pan-europa](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2023-07-28 - THE HISTORY OF THE CIA - France’s Secret Armies and the Pan-Europa Project [pt. 5.3].md) 19:34-19:41, 39:48-40:26)*
