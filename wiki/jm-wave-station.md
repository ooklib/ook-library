JM/WAVE is the CIA Miami station opened in the early 1960s, per [[eyes-wide-open]] "their largest spy station on domestic soil and at the time in the entire world." *([ewa/war-conspiracy-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-12-06 - Eisenhower, Bananas, and the CIA Imperium - War Conspiracy 2.md) 51:51-52:15)*

## Claims

- The Miami anti-Castro operations trained future paramilitary experts (host names Ted Shackley, Thomas Clines, Felix Rodriguez, David Morales; no wiki pages) who later did counterinsurgency in Laos. *([ewa/war-conspiracy-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-12-06 - Eisenhower, Bananas, and the CIA Imperium - War Conspiracy 2.md) 47:14-47:47)*
- [[paul-helliwell]] was already the CIA's Miami paymaster/banker from the mid-1950s. *([ewa/war-conspiracy-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-12-06 - Eisenhower, Bananas, and the CIA Imperium - War Conspiracy 2.md) 50:39-51:04)*
- Per the host, the Cuba program eventually housed and trained the cadre for the later Southeast Asian secret wars and helped usher in the narco-funding of the agency. *([ewa/war-conspiracy-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-12-06 - Eisenhower, Bananas, and the CIA Imperium - War Conspiracy 2.md) 53:32-53:58)*

## Additional claims (The CIA's Miami Station - JMWAVE, ewa/jmwave)

The dedicated Season 3 episode on the station. Everything below is [[eyes-wide-open]]'s account or sources it cites, not verified fact.

### Site, cover and scale
- The station moved in early 1962 from a block of houses in Coral Gables to an abandoned naval air base in Richmond, south of Miami, leased by the Navy to the [[university-of-miami]]; the CIA tenant was the cutout [[zenith-technical-enterprises]]. *([ewa/jmwave](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-07 - The CIA's Miami Station - JMWAVE.md) 0:00-0:43)*
- February 1962 marked two launches per the host: Kennedy's White House-directed [[operation-mongoose]] (under [[robert-f-kennedy]] and [[edward-lansdale]]) and the arrival of [[ted-shackley]], ordered to Miami by [[richard-helms]] as covert operations chief, who "would quickly assume the role of full station chief." *([ewa/jmwave](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-07 - The CIA's Miami Station - JMWAVE.md) 0:43-1:52)*
- Scale (host citing Douglas Valentine and Hinckle and Turner): some 400 to 600 CIA case officers at any time managing an estimated 3,000 Cuban exile assets; the base was a "small spy city" with many paramilitary experts on loan from the Army or Marine Corps; the CIA itself "had little experience in paramilitary operations" and was at the same time building guerrilla capability in Laos and Thailand with loaned military instructors. *([ewa/jmwave](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-07 - The CIA's Miami Station - JMWAVE.md) 6:54-8:25)* Flagged difference: `ewa/shackley` gave Hinckle and Turner's figure as 600-700 American personnel and a budget of "well over $500 million a year"; this episode gives 400-600 officers and does not repeat the budget. *([ewa/jmwave](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-07 - The CIA's Miami Station - JMWAVE.md) 8:02-8:13)*
- Hinckle and Turner: Eastern Airlines' midday Washington-Miami flight was known in the CIA as the "Miami Milk Run" because up to half the passengers might be on the agency payroll; roughly 1,000 new Cuban exiles arrived in Miami every month, a constant recruiting pool. *([ewa/jmwave](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-07 - The CIA's Miami Station - JMWAVE.md) 2:24-2:44, 4:02-4:21)* Army paratrooper Bradley Ayers, training exiles there, called it "a sprawling bureaucratic monster." *([ewa/jmwave](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-07 - The CIA's Miami Station - JMWAVE.md) 7:43-8:08)*
- Host: Miami and the Gulf Coast became a "swirling nexus" of CIA agents, exiles, military intelligence officers, organized crime figures and soldiers of fortune; empowering such "militant and uncontrollable forces" meant the CIA and for a time the White House were "arming the very forces that have been speculated of taking down the president himself." *([ewa/jmwave](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-07 - The CIA's Miami Station - JMWAVE.md) 2:04-3:25)*

### Chiefs and authority
- Predecessor Al Cox: Shackley's memoir says [[william-harvey]] visited about a week after Shackley arrived, had Shackley drive him to sick Cox's house, then fired Cox and promoted Shackley. The host calls this story "dubious" and speculates Cox was reshuffled to Southeast Asia; see [[alfred-cox]]. *([ewa/jmwave](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-07 - The CIA's Miami Station - JMWAVE.md) 8:31-13:57)*
- Host: Shackley was originally meant to be chief of operations one rung below Cox; the new covert operations chief, Rocky Farnsworth, quarrelled with Shackley over interference ("If you don't quit interfering, I'm out of here." "You're out now."), per David Corn, and Shackley replaced him with [[david-sanchez-morales]]. *([ewa/jmwave](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-07 - The CIA's Miami Station - JMWAVE.md) 34:05-34:56)*
- Chain of command was confusing: Shackley reported to Harvey (Task Force W, the Cuban Division), whose authority was challenged by the Special Group Augmented, the White House Cuba task force of [[robert-f-kennedy]] and Lansdale. RFK dropped by unannounced, met exile leaders privately, and Harvey complained to DCI [[john-mccone]] about the detail required for approval of "major operations." *([ewa/jmwave](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-07 - The CIA's Miami Station - JMWAVE.md) 38:06-40:26, 41:37-42:13)* See [[robert-f-kennedy]].
- Shackley's memoir claims the "W" in Task Force W honoured filibuster William Walker, executed after invading Nicaragua; Shackley notes the irony that Harvey himself was shuffled out of Miami by early 1963 ("executed" in the ASR, likely a figure of speech), replaced by [[desmond-fitzgerald]]. *([ewa/jmwave](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-07 - The CIA's Miami Station - JMWAVE.md) 38:27-39:18)*
- Shackley ran it "1962 to 65" per the host at 28:08. Flagged discrepancy: `ewa/shackley` and the [[ted-shackley]] page say 1962-64. *([ewa/jmwave](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-07 - The CIA's Miami Station - JMWAVE.md) 28:08-28:16)*
- Shackley ordered the "station's cowboys" (paramilitary staff) to submit detailed operational plans; case officers "dreaded" briefing him (Corn); see [[grayston-lynch]]. The team he assembled, including [[thomas-clines]] and Morales, followed him through his career. *([ewa/jmwave](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-07 - The CIA's Miami Station - JMWAVE.md) 29:43-32:10)*

### Operations
- Through mid-1962 it funded a dizzying array of exile groups, ran infiltrations and hit-and-run sabotage under Lansdale's "10,000 pinpricks" concept; the failed plot against the Matahambre copper mine (Pinar del Rio) is the example; the host says most such sabotage was counterproductive. *([ewa/jmwave](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-07 - The CIA's Miami Station - JMWAVE.md) 42:13-44:49)*
- Church Committee timeline: in late April 1962 Harvey, "with the help of Ted Shackley," loaded arms and equipment into a U-Haul truck left in a parking lot for [[johnny-roselli]], who delivered it to a Cuban contact, part of the [[zr-rifle]] mafia plots that "largely went nowhere." *([ewa/jmwave](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-07 - The CIA's Miami Station - JMWAVE.md) 42:59-43:39)* See [[cia-mafia-assassination-plots]].
- Host thesis: Mongoose failed at deposing Castro but "did succeed in creating a secret army for the CIA in the Western Hemisphere," an ecosystem that outlasted Cuba, "just in time for its future work in Southeast Asia"; details deferred to the next episode. *([ewa/jmwave](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-07 - The CIA's Miami Station - JMWAVE.md) 44:56-45:28)*
- Host: Cox, Helliwell, Shackley's later Laos posting and Fitzgerald show a Southeast Asia link; "there was a significant link between the AY's operations in Indochina and their operations in South Florida and Louisiana." *([ewa/jmwave](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-07 - The CIA's Miami Station - JMWAVE.md) 14:14-14:25, 22:33-23:18)*

### Funding
- Host: Mongoose funds flowed early in Shackley's tenure but dwindled after the missile crisis and the 1963 push for detente, especially for assassination programs; the station relied on a clandestine financial network built over a decade. *([ewa/jmwave](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-07 - The CIA's Miami Station - JMWAVE.md) 14:26-15:40)*
- Back-channel financing: [[paul-helliwell]] (paymaster, and per Trento a mentor and fronts adviser), private benefactors [[william-pawley]] and later [[h-l-hunt]], and [[george-h-w-bush]]'s [[zapata-offshore]] in Marathon, Florida (host speculation). *([ewa/jmwave](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-07 - The CIA's Miami Station - JMWAVE.md) 15:53-29:43)*

### Aftermath
- By 1963 the White House shifted to shutting down exile activity; the host argues the CIA, "rogue military intelligence elements" and private interests (Texas oil, the old [[china-lobby]] crowd) filled the void and gave the anti-Kennedy coalition its exile foot soldiers. *([ewa/jmwave](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-07 - The CIA's Miami Station - JMWAVE.md) 55:41-57:03)*
