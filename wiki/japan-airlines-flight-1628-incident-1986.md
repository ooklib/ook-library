[[uap-gerb]] calls the 17 November 1986 Japan Airlines cargo flight 1628 sighting one of his all-time favorite and most underrated UFO cases. *([gerb/iceberg-2](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-01-16 - The Alien and UFO Obscure Oddities Iceberg (Level 2).md>) 1:03-1:35)*

## Claims

- Gerb recounts that Captain Kenji Terauchi (ASR "Kenju terauchi/terachi") and two crew, flying a Boeing 747-200F from France toward Tokyo on the Reykjavik to Anchorage leg at about 5 p.m., reported a huge craft the size of about four 747s, accompanied by two smaller lights, following them at 35,000 ft for 31 minutes. *([gerb/iceberg-2](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-01-16 - The Alien and UFO Obscure Oddities Iceberg (Level 2).md>) 1:35-2:30)*
- He says the craft orbited the aircraft at roughly 7.5 miles, sometimes at fixed speed and sometimes darting, and that FAA long-range 3D radar tracked it at that distance, occasionally switching sides between 12-second sweeps. *([gerb/iceberg-2](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-01-16 - The Alien and UFO Obscure Oddities Iceberg (Level 2).md>) 2:30-3:14)*
- Gerb notes that Aviation Week introduced the explanation that the crew saw Mars and Jupiter, which caught on, and says he disagrees. *([gerb/iceberg-2](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-01-16 - The Alien and UFO Obscure Oddities Iceberg (Level 2).md>) 1:19-1:35)*
- He cites NYU physicist [[kevin-knuth]]'s paper on flight characteristics of anomalous aerial vehicles, which modeled 68 plus or minus 7 g across the circle's diameter or 84 plus or minus 8 g of centripetal acceleration for 31 minutes, versus a trained pilot's roughly 9 g limit for about a minute. *([gerb/iceberg-2](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-01-16 - The Alien and UFO Obscure Oddities Iceberg (Level 2).md>) 3:14-4:43)*
- Gerb later lists the case among those the Air Force debunked, in the [[j-allen-hynek]] discussion. *([gerb/iceberg-2](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-01-16 - The Alien and UFO Obscure Oddities Iceberg (Level 2).md>) 13:54-14:02)*
