Chatham House — formally the Royal Institute of International Affairs (RIIA), registered UK
charity number 208223 — is a London foreign-policy think tank headquartered on St. James's
Square, 650 m from Downing Street. Green & Pleasant presents it as the same "hiding in plain
sight" institutional model as the [[fabian-society]] ("the same trick played at a higher level"),
tracing its founding to the same 1919 Paris Peace Conference network of Oxford-educated
imperialists that produced the [[round-table-movement]]. As with the wiki's other Green &
Pleasant sources, all figures and characterizations below are the source's claims (it presents
itself as fully sourced from primary/public records), not independently verified by this wiki.

## Claims

### The Chatham House Rule

- The rule, as posted on Chatham House's own site: "When a meeting or part thereof is held under
  the Chatham House Rule, participants are free to use the information received, but neither the
  identity nor the affiliation of the speaker or speakers, nor that of any other participant, may
  be revealed." First written June 1927, refined in 1992 and 2002; it can be invoked at any
  meeting anywhere in the world, not just at Chatham House's own building
  *([gp/chatham-house](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-04-10 - Britain's Most Powerful Secret Organisation - Chatham House Exposed.md) 2:08, 2:24, 3:15)*.
- Enforcement is informal: Chatham House's own FAQ says breaking the rule "is likely to mean
  future exclusion from all events" — social/professional pressure rather than any legal
  penalty *([gp/chatham-house](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-04-10 - Britain's Most Powerful Secret Organisation - Chatham House Exposed.md) 3:39)*.
- Cited market-moving example: a May 2015 speech by ECB executive-board member Benoît Cœuré
  (transcribed as "Benois Kuay") at a private conference under the Rule was not released publicly
  until the next morning; when it went public the euro dropped over 1.5% and bond/stock markets
  moved — informational advantage the closed-door attendees held for 24 hours
  *([gp/chatham-house](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-04-10 - Britain's Most Powerful Secret Organisation - Chatham House Exposed.md) 4:12)*.
- The Bilderberg Group (~130 politicians, CEOs and security officials meeting annually) has used
  the Chatham House Rule for every session over 70 years, with zero published minutes or
  transcripts *([gp/chatham-house](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-04-10 - Britain's Most Powerful Secret Organisation - Chatham House Exposed.md) 4:41)*.
- Funding-transparency watchdog "Who Funds You?" gave Chatham House a B grade in 2024
  *([gp/chatham-house](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-04-10 - Britain's Most Powerful Secret Organisation - Chatham House Exposed.md) 5:06)*.

### Historical origins

- Founded via a meeting convened by [[lionel-curtis]] at the Hotel Majestic, Paris, 30 May 1919,
  during the Paris Peace Conference, proposing "a permanent Anglo-American Institute of
  International Affairs" with London and New York offices. The British and American delegates
  subsequently split into two organizations: the British Institute of International Affairs
  (inaugural meeting 5 July 1920; became Chatham House) and the American group, which became the
  [[council-on-foreign-relations]] — still referred to by Chatham House as its "US Sister
  Institute" *([gp/chatham-house](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-04-10 - Britain's Most Powerful Secret Organisation - Chatham House Exposed.md) 13:18, 13:43, 15:52)*.
- First presidents included Arthur Balfour, former Prime Minister and author of the Balfour
  Declaration; initial funding included a $2,000 gift from Thomas W. Lamont of J.P. Morgan and
  Company. The Royal Charter was granted 16 July 1926 by King George V; petitioners included the
  future Edward VIII; the current patron is King Charles III, who confirmed the charter in May
  2024 *([gp/chatham-house](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-04-10 - Britain's Most Powerful Secret Organisation - Chatham House Exposed.md) 16:00, 16:15, 16:32)*.
- Georgetown historian Carroll Quigley, who studied the network for 20 years with access to its
  private papers, wrote: "The Royal Institute of International Affairs is nothing but the Milner
  group writ large." Peer-reviewed research by Inderjeet Parmar (City University of London) is
  cited as confirming Chatham House "was deeply embedded in political, economic and military
  elites and functioned as a mechanism for elite consensus building"
  *([gp/chatham-house](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-04-10 - Britain's Most Powerful Secret Organisation - Chatham House Exposed.md) 16:39, 16:57)*.
- See [[round-table-movement]] and [[alfred-milner]] for the founding network's imperial-era
  origins.
