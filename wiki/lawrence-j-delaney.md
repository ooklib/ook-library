Lawrence J. Delaney served as Assistant Secretary of the Air Force for Acquisition, Technology
and Logistics ([[saf-aq|SAF/AQ, "SECAF ATNL"]]) from 1991 to 1992. Gerb calls his pre-government
career history "certainly rather engaging," comparable to other gatekeeper figures he has
covered, such as [[mary-sturtevant|Mary Sturtevant]] and [[terry-phillips|Terry Phillips]].
*([gerb/hidden_wing](../raw/gerb/hidden_wing.md) 39:29–39:51)*

## Claims

- After earning a PhD in chemical engineering, Delaney worked at the
  [[institute-for-defense-analyses|Institute for Defense Analyses (IDA)]] FFRDC from 1961–1962 —
  which also housed former presidential adviser Harold Malmgren, whose IDA position Gerb
  believes was a non-official-cover (NOC) role for CIA work. *([gerb/hidden_wing](../raw/gerb/hidden_wing.md) 39:51–40:19)*
- Moved to the [[aerospace-corporation|Aerospace Corporation]] as director of its propulsion
  technology and systems study group, then to [[martin-marietta|Martin Marietta]] as director of
  advanced programs and director of its Navy systems division — Gerb notes Martin Marietta
  became half of Lockheed Martin and that he believes even pre-merger Martin Marietta engaged in
  UFO programs. *([gerb/hidden_wing](../raw/gerb/hidden_wing.md) 40:19–40:51)*
- Joined [[science-applications-international-corporation|SAIC]] as deputy sector manager for
  military science and information systems, later manager of its information systems group, and
  worked for [[bdm-international|BDM (Braddock, Dunn & McDonald)]] — later acquired by Northrop
  Grumman — shortly before his SAF/AQ appointment. *([gerb/hidden_wing](../raw/gerb/hidden_wing.md) 40:51–41:39)*
- Gerb concludes Delaney "had exposure to UFO programs even before his tenure as head of SAF/AQ,"
  given his path through IDA, Aerospace Corp, Martin Marietta, SAIC, and BDM — all contractors
  and FFRDCs he separately accuses of legacy-program involvement.
  *([gerb/hidden_wing](../raw/gerb/hidden_wing.md) 41:48–41:58)*
