A Federally Funded Research and Development Center is a US government-sponsored,
independent, not-for-profit R&D institution — "government-owned, contractor-operated" (GOCO) —
that performs sensitive long-term research for a federal sponsor while remaining legally
outside the government itself.

## Claims

- FFRDCs are "small in size, but have had enduring impact," developing technologies,
  performing analyses, and helping government make cost-effective choices. *([mitre](../raw/107BtDYhTkA_mitre.md) 17:59–18:26)*
- Former Under Secretary of Defense for Acquisition, Technology and Logistics
  [[frank-kendall]] is quoted describing FFRDCs as giving government "intelligence firepower
  and capital." *([mitre](../raw/107BtDYhTkA_mitre.md) 18:35–18:50)*
- Roots trace to WWII-era "federal contract research centers"; many DOE/NSA National Lab
  FFRDCs (e.g. [[los-alamos-national-laboratory|Los Alamos]], Oak Ridge, soon-to-be
  [[sandia-national-laboratories|Sandia]]) were founded during the Manhattan Project.
  *([mitre](../raw/107BtDYhTkA_mitre.md) 18:59–19:37)*
- Gerb alleges 1948 classified presidential executive orders designated Atomic Energy
  Commission national labs as custodians for recovered nonhuman technical vehicles, under
  [[vannevar-bush]]'s lead — i.e., FFRDCs have allegedly "been the holders of the keys ... since
  the days of the Roswell crash." *([mitre](../raw/107BtDYhTkA_mitre.md) 19:45–20:13)*
- Over 70 FFRDCs established since WWII, ~41–42 active today, 12 federal sponsor agencies,
  receiving over $31 billion in R&D contracts in FY2024. *([mitre](../raw/107BtDYhTkA_mitre.md) 21:16–21:34)*
- Three FFRDC categories: (1) research & development labs (DOE/NSA — Los Alamos, Oak Ridge,
  Sandia, Lawrence Livermore); (2) study & analysis centers (RAND, Center for Naval Analyses);
  (3) systems & integration centers (Aerospace Corporation, [[mitre-corporation]]'s National
  Security Engineering Center / NSEC). *([mitre](../raw/107BtDYhTkA_mitre.md) 21:42–22:14)*
- FFRDCs are barred from competing for contracts, receive extreme access to government/supplier
  data and facilities beyond normal contractors, and per MITRE's own FFRDC primer "assist in
  transferring technology between the government and the private sector ... a repository for
  knowledge ... unencumbered with conflicts concerning for-profit institutions." *([mitre](../raw/107BtDYhTkA_mitre.md) 22:39–25:41)*
- Not subject to FOIA; described as easy to hide funding footprints and paper trails in because
  they are only semi-government-operated. *([mitre](../raw/107BtDYhTkA_mitre.md) 27:56–28:13)*
- Congress.gov has historically flagged "mission creep" as a concern for FFRDCs — internal
  diversification beyond initially defined purpose — which Gerb argues is visible "from the
  very onset of MITRE." *([mitre](../raw/107BtDYhTkA_mitre.md) 28:56–29:24)*
- Illustrative hypothetical: if ONI/ONR recovered a "lenticular disc" from the seafloor via
  the Supervisor of Salvage and Diving and deemed its systems exploitable, custody would go to
  a DOE/NSA National Lab FFRDC (protected by 1954 Atomic Energy Act authority the president
  cannot unilaterally declassify) rather than directly to contractors like Lockheed or
  Northrop, specifically to avoid giving contractors direct custody of the technology.
  *([mitre](../raw/107BtDYhTkA_mitre.md) 25:41–27:34)*
