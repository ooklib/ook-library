San Francisco Chronicle writer whose September 1952 article and 1952 book *Flying Saucers and the Mysterious Little Men* framed the Aztec crash as a hoax by oil conmen and led to the Newton-Gebauer prosecution. ASR renders the name "Khan". [[uap-gerb]] portrays him as obsessed and vindictive, an allegation on Gerb's part rather than settled fact.

## Claims (gerb/aztec)

- In 1949 Cahn tried to buy the saucer story from [[frank-scully]] at a lowball price, then told the Chronicle they had agreed; he turned to [[silas-newton]], offering $25,000 and 50% royalties for the roster of Dr. G, and admitted to Scully he stole a Newton "gear" and replaced it with a slug (the item was a replica). *([gerb/aztec](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2025-07-19 - The 1948 Aztec, New Mexico UFO Crash Retrieval.md>) 39:14-41:27, 44:42-45:37)*
- He labeled [[leo-gebauer]] the sole Dr. G without basis and, per a 15 June 1952 Scully letter to Newton's attorney, threatened to accuse Scully of being in the hoax unless it was exposed under Scully's name. *([gerb/aztec](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2025-07-19 - The 1948 Aztec, New Mexico UFO Crash Retrieval.md>) 41:43-41:52, 45:07-45:52)*
- He wrote J. Edgar Hoover on 25 April 1951 that Newton had leaked the case and was preparing a swindle "of large proportions", and Hoover replied within weeks; he located investor Herman Flater, told him he was a victim, and helped the prosecution throughout the 1953 trial. *([gerb/aztec](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2025-07-19 - The 1948 Aztec, New Mexico UFO Crash Retrieval.md>) 45:52-46:56, 49:36-50:28, 55:17-55:24)*
- Gerb notes Cahn's estate manager later produced cover art for Cahn's own flying saucer pieces in the 1960s. *([gerb/aztec](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2025-07-19 - The 1948 Aztec, New Mexico UFO Crash Retrieval.md>) 56:20-56:36)*
