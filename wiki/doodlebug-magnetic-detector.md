"Doodlebug" was slang for electronic or geophysical sensing devices used to find oil, gas or water; the one [[leo-gebauer]] and [[silas-newton]] sold was the basis for the 1953 fraud case. [[uap-gerb]] argues it may have had real basis in the magnetic-anomaly detection (MAD) technology developed for anti-submarine warfare, linking the Aztec sources to [[vannevar-bush]]'s wartime scientists. This is Gerb's speculative reconstruction.

## Claims (gerb/aztec)

- Gerb says the doodlebug is not shown to have run on Aztec technology, and that its operation had witness-attested successes, unlike the surplus-store device offered as proof at trial. *([gerb/aztec](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2025-07-19 - The 1948 Aztec, New Mexico UFO Crash Retrieval.md>) 42:18-42:32, 54:30-55:17)*
- Steinman ties the devices to World War II anti-submarine magnetic detection by [[carl-a-highland]] and Gebauer; Scully says some Dr. G scientists worked out how Japanese submarines were detected magnetically. *([gerb/aztec](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2025-07-19 - The 1948 Aztec, New Mexico UFO Crash Retrieval.md>) 1:07:35-1:08:16, 1:11:20-1:11:38)*
- [[geophysical-service-inc]] (later [[texas-instruments]]) built oil-detection gear, was recruited into OSRD anti-submarine work with airborne magnetic detectors for the Navy, and Texas Instruments calls its old survey staff "doodlebuggers"; Schmidt and [[john-torrance-tate]] retrofitted PBY Catalinas with MAD gear. *([gerb/aztec](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2025-07-19 - The 1948 Aztec, New Mexico UFO Crash Retrieval.md>) 1:11:38-1:15:49, 1:17:30-1:18:33)*
- Gerb calls saying "doodlebug" with a straight face the hardest part of the script. *([gerb/aztec](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2025-07-19 - The 1948 Aztec, New Mexico UFO Crash Retrieval.md>) 2:17:42-2:18:12)*
