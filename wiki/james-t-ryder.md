Dr. James T. Ryder served nearly 40 years at [[lockheed-martin|Lockheed Martin]], culminating as
VP of Lockheed Martin Space Systems Company and head of the Advanced Technology Center (now
called STAR), 2004–2011 — representing roughly $9B in annual program infrastructure. He is named
by David Grush and Chris Sharp reporting as the Lockheed executive who proposed divesting
recovered UFO materials via the [[operation-kona-blue|Kona Blue]] PAP. Ryder died suddenly in
2018. *([gerb/lockheed](../raw/gerb/lockheed.md) 2:10:25–2:12:15, 1:47:05, 2:04:31–2:04:47)*

## Claims

- PhD, master's, and bachelor's in (theoretical and applied) engineering mechanics, University
  of Illinois. Early DTIC-published work (1981–82) on fatigue life and composite laminate
  stiffness/strength for Air Force Systems Command's Wright Aeronautical Laboratory.
  *([gerb/lockheed](../raw/gerb/lockheed.md) 2:11:05–2:13:37)*
- Proposed a "UAP material divestment plan" to OAP leadership, describing a specific secure
  Lockheed facility holding crash-retrieval materials from the 1950s, and sought a technology
  transfer agreement into the waived PAP ([[operation-kona-blue|Kona Blue]]) that Sen. Harry Reid
  was championing. *([gerb/lockheed](../raw/gerb/lockheed.md) 1:58:51–1:59:43)*
- Gerb speculates Ryder may have wanted to offload only a small, particularly intractable batch
  of materials (possibly part of the 1953 [[kingman-arizona-crash-1953|Kingman]] wreckage) that
  Lockheed had been unable to exploit since the 1950s — bringing in fresh teams rather than
  divesting Lockheed's entire portfolio. *([gerb/lockheed](../raw/gerb/lockheed.md)
  2:03:33–2:05:06, 2:38:34–2:39:03)*
- [[eric-davis|Eric Davis]] acknowledged in a 2019 interview that Ryder recruited him into the
  OAP project's informal "read-in" network; Davis, [[hal-puthoff|Hal Puthoff]], and Ryder all
  served on the Starfire Project science review team (studying the "electric sun" hypothesis)
  following Ryder's Lockheed career. *([gerb/lockheed](../raw/gerb/lockheed.md)
  2:06:55–2:10:25)*
- Gave three talks to the Lucius Trust's Arcane School (London) shortly before his death — "The
  Rendering of the Veils, Part One: Fairies, Devas and Unidentified Aerial Phenomena" (2017,
  discussing cave art, crop circles, and Rendlesham Forest), "The Soul is Light. What Then Is
  Light?" (2015, thanking Hal Puthoff), and "The Garment of God" (2018) — none directly
  referencing his own Lockheed UFO work, but touching on ESP and consciousness in ways Gerb
  compares to Ben Rich's and Dr. Eric A. Walker's alleged statements.
  *([gerb/lockheed](../raw/gerb/lockheed.md) 2:13:47–2:19:12)*
- Gerb does not suspect foul play in Ryder's death, describing him as "a genuinely incredible
  man with insatiable curiosity." *([gerb/lockheed](../raw/gerb/lockheed.md) 2:19:00–2:19:12)*
