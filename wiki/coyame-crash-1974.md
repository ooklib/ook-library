The alleged 1974 crash retrieval near Coyame, Chihuahua, Mexico, called "Mexico's Roswell". Per the [[deneb-report]], a disc-shaped object tracked by US radar collided with a civilian light plane on 25 August 1974, the Mexican military recovered both wrecks, its convoy team all died, and a CIA-organized US recovery team from Fort Bliss covertly crossed the border by helicopter, took the disc and destroyed the rest. [[uap-gerb]] treats it as a severely underexplored case that may give insight into US craft-recovery protocol, and notes that primary sources are few. All details below are attributed claims. *([gerb/coyame](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-08-09 - The 1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash.md>) 0:31-1:19)*

## Radar track and collision (per the Deneb report)

- At 2207 hours on 25 August 1974 US air defense radar allegedly detected an unknown object over the Gulf of Mexico at 2,200 knots, bearing 325 degrees, altitude 75,000 ft. It slowed to about 1,700 knots, turned to 290 degrees and descended, entered Mexican airspace near Brownsville, and was tracked about 500 miles by two military radar sites in steps (45,000 ft then 20,000 ft, about five minutes per level) until vanishing near Coyame. The report rules out a meteor because of the stepped descent and a 35 degree course change. *([gerb/coyame](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-08-09 - The 1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash.md>) 3:45-5:44)*
- About 52 minutes later civilian radio traffic indicated a civilian craft down in the same area; it had left El Paso for Mexico City. Next day (about 10:35) Mexican searchers reported the wreck and a second, circular craft on the ground, apparently in one piece, and the Mexican military imposed radio silence. *([gerb/coyame](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-08-09 - The 1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash.md>) 5:44-6:50)*
- The report says the object was damaged in the collision, and that the hole of about 12 inches and the two-foot dent may result from the collision (at about 1,700 knots) and the ground impact. It notes nothing is known of the plane's occupants. *([gerb/coyame](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-08-09 - The 1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash.md>) 11:34-13:07)*

## Recovery (per the Deneb report)

- Radio interceptions were reported to the [[central-intelligence-agency]], and possibly two other agencies; the CIA formed a recovery team so fast the report infers a rehearsed exercise. US requests to enter Mexico were refused. By 2100 hours on 26 August the team was staged at Fort Bliss with sand-colored unmarked helicopters (probably three UH-1 Hueys and a larger Sea Stallion-type helicopter). *([gerb/coyame](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-08-09 - The 1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash.md>) 6:50-8:00)*
- Satellite and reconnaissance overflights showed the disc and plane loaded on flatbed trucks heading south. Later the convoy stopped, no radio contact occurred, and a low-altitude fast overflight photographed stopped trucks and jeeps with doors open and two human bodies on the ground. *([gerb/coyame](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-08-09 - The 1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash.md>) 8:00-9:02)*
- The four helicopters left Fort Bliss at 1438 hours, followed the border to Presidio, entered Mexico north of Candelaria and reached the convoy at 1653 hours: all Mexican personnel were dead. Team members in bio-protection suits re-strapped the object to a cable from the Sea Stallion and by 1740 hours it was en route to US territory; the Mexican vehicles, bodies and the civilian plane pieces were then destroyed with high explosives. *([gerb/coyame](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-08-09 - The 1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash.md>) 9:02-10:03)*
- The team waited in the Davis Mountains near Valentine, met a convoy near Van Horn, transferred the disc to a truck, and after helicopter decontamination the convoy drove non-stop by back roads toward Atlanta, Georgia. The report says hard evidence thins out there: one unconfirmed report says Wright-Patterson, another an unnamed base. *([gerb/coyame](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-08-09 - The 1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash.md>) 10:03-11:17)*
- Disc description: 16 ft 5 in diameter, convex top and bottom, silver like polished steel, under 5 ft thick, no visible doors, windows, lights, propulsion or markings, about 1,500 lb (estimated from the helicopter load). *([gerb/coyame](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-08-09 - The 1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash.md>) 11:17-12:00)*

## Gerb's analysis

- The civilian plane: FAA inquiries found no records, and Embry-Riddle accident reports and the Aviation Safety Network list no 1974 Mexican crash. Torres and Uriarte guess a Cessna 180 (service ceiling 17,700 ft, fits the 20,000 ft or lower last radar detection) doing drug smuggling, citing a 21 October 1974 Ford and Mexican president discussion of small planes crossing the border, and a Mexican pilot, Enrique Riviera Gutierrez, who died 30 August 1974 per El Fronterizo of Ciudad Juarez; Gerb says he could not trace the paper and finds the link unlikely. *([gerb/coyame](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-08-09 - The 1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash.md>) 18:54-21:29)*
- The CIA and the recovery team: Gerb compares the report to [[chris-sharp]]'s article on the [[cia-office-of-global-access]] (the CIA as a facilitator, with the physical retrieval done by military, then moved to private hands quickly), which he says suggests CIA crash-retrieval work predates the OGA's 2003 creation. His CIA FOIA request (F-2007-00637) for the alleged midair collision returned no results. *([gerb/coyame](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-08-09 - The 1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash.md>) 21:29-22:39)*
- Atlanta: he notes the Lockheed Martin complex at Air Force Plant 6 in Marietta, Georgia (Skunk Works work, C-130 work since the 1950s) and the closed Lockheed Georgia Nuclear Aircraft Laboratory (AFP 67), calling it an "interesting connection" only. See [[lockheed-martin]]. *([gerb/coyame](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-08-09 - The 1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash.md>) 23:26-24:19)*
- Fort Bliss: [[philip-j-corso]] said Roswell debris and bodies went to Fort Bliss; the El Paso Intelligence Center (EPIC, founded there in 1974) and 1971 Keyhole spy satellites (NRO) may have supplied the intelligence, possibly the sort of imagery [[david-grush]] referred to. *([gerb/coyame](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-08-09 - The 1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash.md>) 25:47-26:58)*
- The hazmat-suited team and the delay for additional equipment and two more personnel remind Gerb of [[jonathan-weygandt]]'s Peru account and lead him to suggest the additional personnel were munitions experts sent to sanitize contaminated sites. *([gerb/coyame](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-08-09 - The 1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash.md>) 24:26-25:18)* *([gerb/coyame](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-08-09 - The 1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash.md>) 26:58-27:39)*
- Noe Torres wonders whether the explosive used was a Mk-54 SADM ("suitcase nuke", smallest yield 10 tons), and Gerb cites Hans Kruger's 2000 paper on neutralizing stored biological agents with low-yield nuclear warheads. *([gerb/coyame](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-08-09 - The 1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash.md>) 27:39-28:20)*

## What killed the Mexican soldiers

- The Deneb report says the cause is unknown and speculates a chemical released from the damaged disc or a microbiological agent. Gerb keeps open the possibility that the US team killed the soldiers to keep debris secret (recalling Grusch's claims that crimes were committed to keep UAP secrecy), and links contamination to the Varginha police officer story, the MJ-12 first annual report (four Sandia technicians who died of seizures and bleeding, and a medical adviser's remark about "the alien virus") and the [[interplanetary-phenomenon-unit]] summary (three Sandia technician deaths). See also [[interplanetary-contamination]]. *([gerb/coyame](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-08-09 - The 1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash.md>) 28:20-31:37)* *([gerb/coyame](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-08-09 - The 1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash.md>) 42:42-43:53)*
- The only lead on the Mexican side is from Mexican UFO researcher Alfonso Salazar: a 27 October 1974 El Heraldo de Chihuahua story about a group of Mexican soldiers killed in the crash of a "military transport". Gerb could not find it. *([gerb/coyame](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-08-09 - The 1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash.md>) 37:25-37:58)*

## Witnesses

- Per Torres, a school teacher, Johnny Chambers, and her 10-year-old son told him at a 2012 Presidio, Texas festival that they saw a fireball and a "big explosion in the sky" on the Mexican side, and that within a day residents saw military helicopters, including a large twin-rotor one, returning from Mexico. Gerb notes they appeared in 2012, five years after Torres and Uriarte's initial reporting, so they could be false witnesses. A Facebook commenter ("Melissa P.") said her uncle was ordered to "the Mexico Roswell" and came back changed; Gerb finds this unsupported. *([gerb/coyame](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-08-09 - The 1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash.md>) 37:58-39:25)*
- On 16 March 2024 a Mexican government official, Jesus Alberto (as transcribed; tied by Gerb to the NASA-mummies hearings), posted and deleted a tweet reading roughly "I have the video ... from another world", which Gerb's context links to the Coyame incident. Alberto gave Gerb a thumbs-up but no reply to follow-ups. *([gerb/coyame](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-08-09 - The 1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash.md>) 39:25-40:30)*
