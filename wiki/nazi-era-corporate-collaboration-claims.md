A cross-cutting concept page for claims that major German, American and other Western companies collaborated with the Nazi regime. Sourced from one video by Brofessor Stein (a German-based channel with an entertainment disclaimer: 'some details may be oversimplified or inaccurate'), and explicitly not accusing the companies of current ties to any regime. Related wiki threads: [[operation-paperclip]], [[hjalmar-schacht]], [[prescott-bush]], [[ig-farben]], [[standard-oil]].

## Claims

- Recurring pattern across the video: forced or concentration-camp labor, adaptation of factories to war production, thin postwar consequences for executives, and later reparations funds or statements of regret (Daimler 1988, Volkswagen 1998, Ford-Werke 2000, Hugo Boss 2000/2011, IBM's 2001 statement). *([brof_stein/nazi-brands](<../raw/brof_stein/Brofessor Stein - 2025-07-23 - Every Major Brand with Nazi Ties Explained By a German.md>) 0:00-21:05)*
- Financial channel: under [[hjalmar-schacht]]'s Economics Ministry the Reich raised dollars via schemes like the Rukandra (Registered Mark) program, selling discounted marks to Americans of German descent backed by assets stolen from Jews and refugees, and [[jpmorgan-chase]]'s predecessor Chase National Bank allegedly facilitated it, raising over $20 million for Germany. *([brof_stein/nazi-brands](<../raw/brof_stein/Brofessor Stein - 2025-07-23 - Every Major Brand with Nazi Ties Explained By a German.md>) 0:00-0:37)*
- US firms allegedly kept German subsidiaries running under trustee or local control (Kodak, GM/Opel, Ford-Werke, IBM/Dehomag, ITT) and later received compensation, e.g. GM $32 million and ITT $27 million from the US government for Allied bombing damage. *([brof_stein/nazi-brands](<../raw/brof_stein/Brofessor Stein - 2025-07-23 - Every Major Brand with Nazi Ties Explained By a German.md>) 10:23-21:05)*
- Other companies covered, not given their own pages: Adidas/Puma (Dassler brothers, Nazi party members, Hitler Youth role, split in 1947), Siemens (forced labor at Auschwitz, Ravensbruck and Lublin, about 400 relocated plants), Hugo Boss (SA/SS/Wehrmacht uniforms), Associated Press (only Western agency allowed in Hitler's Germany, agreed not to publish anything harmful; AP points at coercion), Dr. Oetker (led by Waffen-SS member Rudolf-August Oetker; private bank employed Rudolf von Ribbentrop), Continental (about 10,000 forced laborers, Hannover-Stocken shoe-sole testing), Lufthansa (free 1932 campaign aircraft for Hitler, Erhard Milch, disguised air force, over 10,000 forced laborers), Nestle/Maggi (fed both sides, exclusive whey deal). *([brof_stein/nazi-brands](<../raw/brof_stein/Brofessor Stein - 2025-07-23 - Every Major Brand with Nazi Ties Explained By a German.md>) 1:48-2:47, 12:10-12:46, 14:10-17:47, 19:38-20:21)*
- [[standard-oil]] via a German subsidiary (50% stake) dominated Germany's fuel market by 1935, had close ties to [[ig-farben]] and tried to form a cartel (oil for Standard, chemicals for Farben), allegedly withheld synthetic rubber tech from the US, used forced labor including a Stutthof sub camp, and a blocked $24 million deal would have transferred Hungarian plants to Nazi control; today's Exxon Mobil. *([brof_stein/nazi-brands](<../raw/brof_stein/Brofessor Stein - 2025-07-23 - Every Major Brand with Nazi Ties Explained By a German.md>) 12:46-13:37)*
