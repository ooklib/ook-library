Lieutenant Colonel Jack Y. Cannon is, per [[eyes-wide-open]], the third and "by far the most obscure" of the three figures the host originally planned to trace through post-revolution Havana (the other two being [[frank-sturgis]] and [[gerry-patrick-hemming]]). He is introduced at the end of chapter 2 of [[road-to-dallas]]; the host defers his Japan-era career to the coming Willoughby episodes. The sections up to "Additional claims" hold what chapter 2 says; chapter 3 ([[road-to-dallas]]) is his dedicated episode. *([ewa/dallas-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-21 - David Atlee Phillips_ the Spy's Spy - The Road to Dallas Chapter 2.md) 21:16)*

## Claims

- Online mentions are almost all about his postwar Army intelligence (G2) work in Japan and the Far East. Host promises to cover his collaboration with the Yakuza and stay-behind networks in Japan next, via his boss then, [[charles-willoughby]], whom the host calls "in my opinion a major suspect in the JFK hit." *([ewa/dallas-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-21 - David Atlee Phillips_ the Spy's Spy - The Road to Dallas Chapter 2.md) 21:49)*
- Spelling: host says the name is "sometimes incorrectly spelled with two N's" (the ASR itself renders "Canon"); the transcript is ambiguous about which is which, so both are kept as aliases and "Cannon" is used per the video description tags and Scott quote. *([ewa/dallas-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-21 - David Atlee Phillips_ the Spy's Spy - The Road to Dallas Chapter 2.md) 22:42)*
- Host was put on his trail by [[peter-dale-scott]] in *Deep Politics and the Death of JFK*, quoted: "In the late 1950s, US Army Colonel Jack Y. Cannon, a veteran of the Willoughby Yakuza operations in Japan, penetrated Castro's guerilla operation against Batista in Cuba." Host had found no other indication of Cannon in Cuba. *([ewa/dallas-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-21 - David Atlee Phillips_ the Spy's Spy - The Road to Dallas Chapter 2.md) 23:02)*
- Newspaper/journal sources cited by the host: a Dec. 24, 1958 Paris (Texas) News article calling him a "roving intelligence officer"; a June 4, 1959 Victoria (Texas) Advocate biographical article (host: "very strangely written") saying he "switched to the Mediterranean just as the Mideast heated up"; and a 2014 Asia-Pacific Journal article by Tessa Morris-Suzuki saying he served in various Middle East postings "including Cairo during the 1950s." *([ewa/dallas-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-21 - David Atlee Phillips_ the Spy's Spy - The Road to Dallas Chapter 2.md) 23:47)*
- Per most accounts the host cites (including an Asia Times piece on Japan), he was a roving military intelligence officer "on loan with the CIA," undercover worldwide, based primarily at Fort Hood, Texas, which the host notes sits near the geographic middle of Dallas, Fort Worth, San Antonio and Houston. Official record: returned to the US in late 1958. *([ewa/dallas-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-21 - David Atlee Phillips_ the Spy's Spy - The Road to Dallas Chapter 2.md) 24:42, 25:18)*
- Host speculation: because the timing matches [[david-atlee-phillips]]'s wrap-up in Lebanon and Castro's takeover, Cannon "may have met" Phillips in the Middle East, and his work with the Cuban underground in Havana would complement Phillips's. Host leaves his significance open ("what was Jack Cannon's significance to this whole complex?"). *([ewa/dallas-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-21 - David Atlee Phillips_ the Spy's Spy - The Road to Dallas Chapter 2.md) 25:25, 26:00)*
- Host: as Phillips was reposted to Havana and Cannon returned to Fort Hood, "the bonds were starting to form within the CIA's anti-Castro underground... bonds that would lead all the way to Dallas in 1963" (host's framing). *([ewa/dallas-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-21 - David Atlee Phillips_ the Spy's Spy - The Road to Dallas Chapter 2.md) 26:11)*

## Additional claims (Cactus Jack Canon Does Havana, ewa/dallas-3)

Chapter 3 of [[road-to-dallas]] is his dedicated episode. Host framing: a character "I seldom see mentioned in relation to the JFK assassination"; the argument that he ran weapons to the Cuban underground is the host's own reconstruction ("at least based on my research"), with "no smoking gun proof". [[eyes-wide-open]] refers to him as "Cactus Jack" and "Texas Jack".

### Fort Hood court-martial and promotion

- Lt. Col. Jack Young Cannon, "freshly back" as a roving intelligence officer, had been Provost Marshal (ASR "Provice General") at Fort Hood only a few months when he was court-martialed in December 1958; he was briefly hospitalized for a "so-called mental disorder" (Bryan-College Station Eagle, Oct. 13, 1958). *([ewa/dallas-3](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-28 - Cactus Jack Canon Does Havana - The Road to Dallas Chapter 3.md) 0:21-0:53)*
- Charges as reported: some 43 counts of theft of military gear, ammunition and shooting livestock; the Paris (Texas) News says he was held for "withholding information about the theft of ammunition" and old helmets; the Wichita Falls Times Record News says the 43-year-old was charged with shooting two cows. He was found innocent on all counts; local papers were outraged at the prosecution. *([ewa/dallas-3](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-28 - Cactus Jack Canon Does Havana - The Road to Dallas Chapter 3.md) 0:59-1:55)*
- By Jan. 14, 1959 (host: about two weeks after Castro took Havana) he was made executive officer of the Second Ordnance Group at Fort Hood. The Waco Times-Herald noted most Army observers expected him to be transferred after the publicity. Host asks why an officer accused of stealing military hardware would head an ordnance group, and argues his "new mission" was to smuggle surplus US Army gear to Cuba, first for the revolutionary army and later for the anti-Castro underground. *([ewa/dallas-3](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-28 - Cactus Jack Canon Does Havana - The Road to Dallas Chapter 3.md) 1:55-3:08)*

### The "Jack Shea" alias argument

- Host reports that in his 1978 House Select Committee on Assassinations (HSCA) testimony [[gerry-patrick-hemming]] listed Americans who worked with Camilo Cienfuegos (Mitchell Devo, Paul Hughes and "Jack Sheay", ASR of Shea/Shay) and emphasized that Jack Shea "was actually a man named Jack Cannon", written with two N's. *([ewa/dallas-3](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-28 - Cactus Jack Canon Does Havana - The Road to Dallas Chapter 3.md) 19:00-20:37)*
- [[loran-hall]]'s FBI file names a "Lieutenant Jack Sheay" among Americans he met with the revolutionary forces in 1959, alongside [[frank-sturgis]] (as Fiorini) and a Captain Rex Sunderland (host finds no other record of Sunderland). *([ewa/dallas-3](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-28 - Cactus Jack Canon Does Havana - The Road to Dallas Chapter 3.md) 18:06-18:30)*
- Host's argument that the two Cannons are one man: two options are given (a different 2-N Cannon, still worth documenting as a Hemming/Sturgis weapons smuggler, or the same Fort Hood officer). He admits there is "no smoking gun" but believes they are the same, citing [[peter-dale-scott]]'s line that Cannon "penetrated Castro's guerilla operations" and the similar behavior of both. *([ewa/dallas-3](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-28 - Cactus Jack Canon Does Havana - The Road to Dallas Chapter 3.md) 20:37-22:19)*
- Host speculation, labeled as such: the spellings functioned as signifiers: "Cannon" (1 N) for his true Army intelligence identity in memos and papers, "Cannon" (2 N) as a "fake real name" given to Hemming and the Cuba riff-raff, and "Jack Shea" as the street cover name in Havana. He notes the 1-N Cannon vanishes from the public record after January 1959 while the 2-N Cannon/Shea appears in Cuba alongside Hall and Sturgis, giving a window of roughly February-April 1959 for his arrival. *([ewa/dallas-3](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-28 - Cactus Jack Canon Does Havana - The Road to Dallas Chapter 3.md) 22:19-24:09)*
- Host speculation: as ordnance-group chief he was "perfectly positioned" to supply Sturgis's planned Nicaragua invasion force and later the stay-behind anti-Castro groups; he repeats Scott's point that Castro tolerated the Yankee interlopers for their ability to procure US Army surplus. *([ewa/dallas-3](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-28 - Cactus Jack Canon Does Havana - The Road to Dallas Chapter 3.md) 24:09-25:05)*

### Tourist police and the Errol Flynn incident

- The host says newspaper reports and a 1977 HSCA interview indicate he acted undercover in Cuba as a "tourist police" officer. Tourist police were English-speaking officers created under Batista, described in J. A. Gustavsen's *Tension Under the Sun* as wearing white "tourist squad" armbands. Host: ideal cover for hobnobbing with visitors and soldier-of-fortune types and meeting future anti-Castro sponsors. *([ewa/dallas-3](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-28 - Cactus Jack Canon Does Havana - The Road to Dallas Chapter 3.md) 25:05-26:52)*
- Miami Herald, March 26, 1959: Cuban tourist policeman "Jack Sheay" burst "drunk and disorderly" with two guns into Errol Flynn's Commodoro Hotel apartment party on March 11. Flynn said he "never argue[d] with a .45, particularly when it's in the hands of a drunk"; Sheay did not appear at proceedings and is not named again in the host's research. The Herald attributes Shea to Brooklyn, which the host says either separates him from Texan Cannon or was cover. *([ewa/dallas-3](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-28 - Cactus Jack Canon Does Havana - The Road to Dallas Chapter 3.md) 26:52-27:34, 31:36-32:02)*
- Host speculation: "Jack Shea" was also the name of a New York TV director (then working on the Jerry Lewis show and Bob Hope specials), possibly chosen to get onto VIP guest lists; and that Cannon may have been there to pressure Flynn, then vocally pro-Castro (*Cuban Rebel Girls*, *Cuban Story*), to drop the pro-Castro films. Flynn died in Vancouver later that year; the host says the biopsy suggests no foul play. *([ewa/dallas-3](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-28 - Cactus Jack Canon Does Havana - The Road to Dallas Chapter 3.md) 27:42-28:27, 30:16-32:17)*

### Japan-era portrait (Yamaguchi memoir)

- Host cites the memoir of Japanese actress and "suspected intelligence asset" Yoshiko Yamaguchi, which names Colonel Jack Cannon and his "Zed unit" in postwar Japan, while he served under [[charles-willoughby]]. She describes a boorish, heavy-drinking gunslinger who fired a pistol at birds and squirrels while driving near Kamakura, and a party at which a drunk Cannon flung a cat against a wall. Host says this mirrors the Flynn incident and that his Japan work is "the final piece of the puzzle" to be covered in the upcoming Willoughby episodes. *([ewa/dallas-3](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-11-28 - Cactus Jack Canon Does Havana - The Road to Dallas Chapter 3.md) 28:43-30:16, 32:34-33:10)*

## Additional discrepancies (ewa/dallas-3)

- Dallas-2 says Cannon returned to the US in late 1958 as an "official record"; this chapter puts him at Fort Hood from about mid-1958 (court-martial in Dec. 1958, hospitalization reported Oct. 1958). Not necessarily contradictory; flagged only.
- Spelling: this chapter's title/ASR say "Canon" throughout while the host's narration and description use "Cannon" (1 N) for the Army officer and "2 N" for the alias-linked man; the 1-N/2-N "signifier" theory is entirely the host's speculation. The dallas-2 page recorded the host's spelling remark as ambiguous; this chapter says the 2-N form is the one Hemming used.
- Hemming's 1978 HSCA list is host-reported; the Peter Dale Scott quote (Scott, p. 178) is host-reported as well.
- Wiki-internal observation (not from the transcript): [[frank-sturgis]] and Hemming pages do not mention Cannon; this chapter is the only place linking them.
