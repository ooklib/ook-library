"Yankee Blue" is the name [[sean-kirkpatrick|Sean Kirkpatrick]] gave, in a *Wall Street Journal*
article ("Pentagon Disinformation That Fueled America's UFO Mythology"), to an alleged illegal
Air Force hazing program he claimed was the actual origin of UFO legacy-program claims — tricking
tens of thousands of Air Force officers over decades. Gerb treats this claim with heavy
skepticism. *([gerb/secrecy_2](../raw/gerb/secrecy_2.md) 2:15:07–2:15:38)*

## Claims

- Gerb states Kirkpatrick told podcasters off-air that "each branch of the armed forces had
  their own Yankee Blue equivalent" — a detail never mentioned in the *Wall Street Journal*
  article or publicly. Journalist Steven Greenstreet reportedly obtained a copy of the "Yankee
  Blue" document (allegedly from Kirkpatrick) featuring what Gerb calls "inaccurate security
  controls." Gerb challenges Kirkpatrick to release the full document and note whose signature
  appears at the bottom. *([gerb/secrecy_2](../raw/gerb/secrecy_2.md) 2:15:38–2:16:07)*
- On his Judicial Watch interview, [[david-grush|David Grush]] directly rebutted the Yankee Blue
  narrative: he acknowledged awareness of hazing culture generally (having engaged in it himself
  as a lieutenant) but stated the actual code words he provided to the Intelligence Community
  Inspector General (ICIG) "were certainly not the code words" involved in the hazing narrative,
  and that the SAPs/CAPs in question were "essentially covering it up ... performing financial
  fraud ... misappropriating funds and ... taking haircuts across other black programs."
  *([gerb/secrecy_2](../raw/gerb/secrecy_2.md) 2:16:07–2:17:44)*
