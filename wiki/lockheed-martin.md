Lockheed Martin (formed 15 March 1995 from the merger of the Lockheed Corporation and
[[martin-marietta|Martin Marietta]]) is the largest defense contractor in the world ($64.65B
revenue in 2024, $136.39B market cap, 122,000+ employees). Gerb argues Lockheed sits at the
center of UFO legacy-program speculation more than any other contractor — named directly by
whistleblowers from Edgar Fuchs to David Grush — owing to its Skunk Works advanced-programs
division, its multi-decade administration of Sandia National Laboratories and the Tonopah Test
Range, and a well-documented 2008–2011 attempt to divest itself of recovered UFO materials.
*([lockheed](../raw/X6JfbfmvgMo_lockheed.md) 9:46–10:03, 17:47–18:26, 4:49–5:15)*

## Corporate structure & history

- Operates four divisions: Aeronautics (39% of revenue, houses [[skunk-works]]; F-35, C-130,
  F-16, F-22), Missiles and Fire Control (18%), Rotary and Mission Systems (24%; Sikorsky
  helicopters, Aegis combat system), and Space (18%; Trident II, Orion, hypersonic platforms).
  *([lockheed](../raw/X6JfbfmvgMo_lockheed.md) 18:45–20:20)*
- The Lockheed Corporation was founded in 1926, rose to prominence with the P-38 Lightning, and
  founded [[skunk-works|Skunk Works]] in 1943 at its Burbank, California plant.
  *([lockheed](../raw/X6JfbfmvgMo_lockheed.md) 21:16–21:39)*
- Prior to the 1995 merger, [[martin-marietta|Martin Marietta]] made a 1993 acquisition spree —
  General Electric Aerospace, General Dynamics Space Systems Division, and Sandia National
  Laboratories' management contract — that Gerb argues entwined the future Lockheed Martin with
  the UFO portfolio, especially given GE's own alleged UFO-program history (per Leonard
  Stringfield's Wright-Patterson "Mr. Y" case) and both companies' documented 1950s interest in
  anti-gravity/electrogravitic research (T. Townsend Brown's Project Winterhaven).
  *([lockheed](../raw/X6JfbfmvgMo_lockheed.md) 25:35–28:55)*
- IRAD (Independent Research and Development): Lockheed spent $1.5B on IRAD in 2021 alone.
  Gerb, citing [[david-grush|David Grush]]'s claims of fund misappropriation, argues IRAD lets
  Lockheed pursue classified nonhuman-technology work while allowing DoD to reimburse a portion
  of the cost as an indirect expense, all with reduced traditional DoD oversight.
  *([lockheed](../raw/X6JfbfmvgMo_lockheed.md) 12:12–15:26)*
- [[katherine-austin-fitts|Katherine Austin Fitts]] identified Lockheed Martin Information
  Systems (later merged into Leidos in 2015) among IT/accounting providers she says obscured
  DoD/HUD fund destinations during her HUD tenure. *([lockheed](../raw/X6JfbfmvgMo_lockheed.md)
  16:20–16:45)*

## Defense industrial base & FFRDC administration

- Lockheed is a cornerstone commercial-sector member of the Defense Industrial Base (DIB),
  alongside FFRDCs, UARCs, DoD labs, and MRTFBs; is an eligible contractor under the DoD's
  [[corporate-portfolio-program|Corporate Portfolio Program]] (established 2020 by
  [[ellen-lord|Ellen Lord]]) alongside [[science-applications-international-corporation|SAIC]].
  *([lockheed](../raw/X6JfbfmvgMo_lockheed.md) 29:08–31:33)*
- From 1993 (Martin Marietta's acquisition) to 2017, [[sandia-national-laboratories|Sandia
  National Laboratories]] was administered by the Sandia Corporation, a Lockheed Martin
  subsidiary — a 22-year window Gerb treats as central to his Lockheed thesis, given Sandia's
  alleged role reverse-engineering [[alien-reproduction-vehicle-tr3b|TR-3B]] propulsion (with
  [[lawrence-livermore-national-laboratory|Lawrence Livermore]]) per
  [[edgar-fuchs|Edgar Fuchs]]. *([lockheed](../raw/X6JfbfmvgMo_lockheed.md) 38:39–40:16,
  1:06:20–1:06:28)*
- Under Sandia's management, Lockheed by extension administered the classified
  [[tonopah-test-range|Tonopah Test Range]] (Area 52), a DOE weapons-testing site Gerb argues
  is a plausible UFO crash-retrieval/material-exploitation site, based partly on a leaked UAP
  Task Force report (per [[richard-dolan|Richard Dolan]]) describing DARPA "Advance Group Six"
  prototype-craft testing at Area 51 and Tonopah. *([lockheed](../raw/X6JfbfmvgMo_lockheed.md)
  52:09–55:22, 56:19–59:56)*
- Oak Ridge National Laboratory was previously managed by Lockheed Martin Energy Research Corp.
  (now under Battelle); Gerb notes disgraced former AARO director [[sean-kirkpatrick|Shawn
  Kirkpatrick]] moved there as CTO for defense/intelligence programs in December 2023.
  *([lockheed](../raw/X6JfbfmvgMo_lockheed.md) 36:37–36:53, 38:17–38:39)*
- 1993–2017 GE/Honeywell/Sperry Rand asset chain: Sandia's post-2017 administrator (NTESS) is a
  Honeywell subsidiary; Honeywell received Sperry Rand assets after Sperry Rand's 1986 collapse
  — Sperry Rand was named (alongside Bell Labs) by Col. [[philip-j-corso|Philip Corso]] as a UFO
  technology-adaptation contractor. Other Sperry Rand asset recipients: Raytheon, Northrop
  Grumman, and Lockheed Martin. *([lockheed](../raw/X6JfbfmvgMo_lockheed.md) 38:44–39:28)*

## Skunk Works & alleged reverse engineering

- [[skunk-works|Skunk Works]] (Advanced Development Programs), founded 1943 in Burbank and now
  based at [[air-force-plant-42|Air Force Plant 42]], Palmdale, built the U-2, SR-71, F-117,
  F-22, and F-35. Gerb and Dr. Steven Greer both argue Plant 42 and Lockheed's Helendale radar
  cross-section (RCS) range form part of an Antelope Valley UFO legacy-program network.
  *([lockheed](../raw/X6JfbfmvgMo_lockheed.md) 21:39–22:47, 1:10:28–1:13:29)*
- [[edgar-fuchs|Edgar Fuchs]] alleges Lockheed operated as prime contractor (with Boeing,
  Northrop, and Teledyne Ryan, managed by NRO/NSA/CIA) on the triangular
  [[alien-reproduction-vehicle-tr3b|TR-3B]], prototyped from the 1970s to operational status by
  1994 — the year before the Lockheed–Martin Marietta merger.
  *([lockheed](../raw/X6JfbfmvgMo_lockheed.md) 39:52–40:16, 40:45–41:01)*
- Witness "Ed" (Air Force Lt. Col., Edwards 412th Test Wing) claimed Boeing and Lockheed were
  the two most critical contractors on an alien reproduction vehicle (ARV) testing program
  operating between Edwards AFB, Area 51/Groom Lake, and S4 between 1998–2002.
  *([lockheed](../raw/X6JfbfmvgMo_lockheed.md) 41:16–42:11)*
- [[ross-coulthart|Ross Coulthart]] states David Fravor told him the 2004 Nimitz "tic tac" was
  "Lockheed Skunk Works" technology — "categorically Lockheed Martin technology" — a claim also
  made (without evidentiary support, per Gerb) by Steven Greer. *([lockheed](../raw/
  X6JfbfmvgMo_lockheed.md) 1:14:05–1:15:59, 1:16:19–1:16:33)*
- [[ben-rich|Ben Rich]] (Skunk Works director 1975–1991) allegedly told a 1993 UCLA audience
  "we already have the means to travel among the stars" and "we now have the technology to take
  ET home," corroborated by attendees Tom Keller and John Haren, and separately by aerospace
  journalist James Goodall, who recounted Rich telling him "we have things out in the desert...
  50 years beyond what you can comprehend." *([lockheed](../raw/X6JfbfmvgMo_lockheed.md)
  1:17:17–1:23:36)*
- Skunk Works EVP Rob Weiss and 30+-year veteran Steve Justice served as advisers to
  [[to-the-stars-academy|To the Stars Academy]] (founded by Tom DeLonge, 2017).
  *([lockheed](../raw/X6JfbfmvgMo_lockheed.md) 1:24:14–1:25:41)*
- Gerb notes the fictionalized "Astra TR-3B" (nicknamed "the Locust") in DeLonge's novel *Secret

## 1986 GAO carveout security audit

- An August 1986 GAO report to the Secretary of Defense ("Need for DoD Inspections of Special
  Access Contracts") found poor document-control at Lockheed's Burbank plant (Skunk Works'
  original site): 1,460 discrepancies across 40,000 secret/top-secret items at 53 document
  control substations, hundreds left unresolved. Lockheed pulled all aerospace activities from
  Burbank by 1992. *([lockheed](../raw/X6JfbfmvgMo_lockheed.md) 1:36:37–1:42:09)*
- Gerb theorizes this audit — addressed to then-SecDef Caspar Weinberger — may have been the
  near-exposure event that drove the 1993–94 reorganization of the DoD Special Access Program
  Oversight Committee (SAPOC) described in the [[wilson-davis-notes|Wilson-Davis notes]], and
  connects it to Jacques Vallée's account (in *Forbidden Science 5*) of Comptroller General
  Charles Bowsher finding a crashed-UFO program during a classified-projects audit, later
  "quenched" by "a powerful person in DoD." *([lockheed](../raw/X6JfbfmvgMo_lockheed.md)
  1:42:18–1:44:26)*
- Gerb's original (first-ever channel video) theory named Lockheed as the unnamed "large
  defense contractor" in the Wilson-Davis notes; he now favors SAIC instead, but maintains
  Lockheed remains a critical, if shadowed, piece of the SAPOC reorganization story.
  *([lockheed](../raw/X6JfbfmvgMo_lockheed.md) 1:33:51–1:35:11)*

## The Kona Blue material transfer (2008–2011)

- [[operation-kona-blue|Kona Blue]] was a prospective waived special access program (PAP), set
  up under DHS Science & Technology, proposed as a "catcher's mitt" for Lockheed Martin to
  divest itself of recovered UFO materials — reportedly craft hull fragments and other pieces
  recovered since the 1950s, possibly connected to the 1953 [[kingman-arizona-crash-1953|
  Kingman, Arizona]] crash. *([lockheed](../raw/X6JfbfmvgMo_lockheed.md) 1:47:05–1:51:04,
  1:59:43–2:00:22)*
- The proposal was championed by Lockheed Martin Space Systems VP [[james-t-ryder|Dr. James T.
  Ryder]] and routed through [[bigelow-aerospace-advanced-space-studies|Bigelow Aerospace
  Advanced Space Studies (BASS)]] under the DIA's OAP program (initiated by Sen. Harry Reid,
  2007–2008, ~$22M budget over 5 years). *([lockheed](../raw/X6JfbfmvgMo_lockheed.md)
  1:50:35–1:51:04)*
- Sen. Harry Reid, per a May 2021 *New Yorker* account, confirmed Lockheed "had this material for
  decades," that he tried and failed to get access, and that the CIA and DIA killed the transfer.
  *([lockheed](../raw/X6JfbfmvgMo_lockheed.md) 1:44:29–1:46:11)*
- The technology-transfer agreement was blocked by [[glenn-gaffney|Glenn Gaffney]], then deputy
  director of the CIA's Directorate of Science and Technology, per documents entered into the
  November 2024 House Oversight hearing record by Rep. Tim Burchett, corroborated by a
  declassified AARO slide deck. *([lockheed](../raw/X6JfbfmvgMo_lockheed.md) 1:58:00–2:00:13)*
- Gerb names two additional possible participants in blocking the transfer: former Lockheed VP
  [[mary-sturdivant|Mary Sturdivant]] (given her prior CIA DS&T/NRO career and close working
  relationship with Ryder), and [[robert-cardillo|Robert Cardillo]] (then Deputy DNI for
  Intelligence Integration), who by 2019 sat on the board of Peraton — the successor entity to
  Lockheed's divested [[peraton|Enterprise Integration Group]].
  *([lockheed](../raw/X6JfbfmvgMo_lockheed.md) 2:22:52–2:23:03, 2:30:15–2:32:56,
  2:34:10–2:38:19)*
- Per researcher Rob Jones's *Subrosa* (cited by Gerb), after the Kona Blue effort failed,
  Lockheed may have offloaded UFO-related black-program materials by selling its Enterprise
  Integration Group (EIG) — "a unit deeply involved in highly classified systems engineering for
  US intelligence agencies" — to Veritas Capital for $815M in 2010; EIG became
  [[peraton|the SI Organization, then Vencore, then Perspecta, then Peraton]].
  *([lockheed](../raw/X6JfbfmvgMo_lockheed.md) 2:35:41–2:38:00)*
- Gerb's assessment: he believes with "moderate to high conviction" the divested material was
  part or all of the 1953 Kingman, Arizona crash wreckage, which Lockheed had been unable to
  exploit since the 1950s; he does not believe this represented Lockheed's entire UFO-materials
  portfolio. *([lockheed](../raw/X6JfbfmvgMo_lockheed.md) 2:04:22–2:05:06, 2:39:41–2:40:36)*
- [[eric-davis|Eric Davis]] told Gerb directly that no UFO technology has ever been successfully
  adapted for human aerospace use and that ARVs don't exist — a claim Gerb disputes and
  attributes (speculatively) to program-protection messaging possibly originating from Ryder,
  given Davis and [[hal-puthoff|Hal Puthoff]] both served with Ryder on the Starfire Project
  science review team after Ryder's Lockheed career. *([lockheed](../raw/
  X6JfbfmvgMo_lockheed.md) 2:05:36–2:06:55, 2:10:05–2:10:25)*
