J. Robert Oppenheimer, scientific director of the Manhattan Project's Los Alamos laboratory, is
discussed by UAP Gerb primarily for two things: his real, documented friction with General
[[leslie-groves|Leslie Groves]]'s compartmentalized secrecy apparatus, and disputed claims placing
him on 1940s–1950s UFO crash-retrieval teams. UAP Gerb explicitly labels the latter connection
speculative. *([secrecy-1](../raw/jR-h5p2bd-A_secrecy_1.md) 2:00:15–2:00:27)*

## Claims

- At Groves's request, Oppenheimer was installed as scientific director of an isolated Los Alamos
  laboratory; his first task was recruiting scientists and their families to relocate to a
  location, and work on a project, he was not permitted to identify or fully describe.
  *([secrecy-1](../raw/jR-h5p2bd-A_secrecy_1.md) 39:37–40:01)*
- Repeatedly voiced concerns that Groves's need-to-know compartmentalization hindered overall
  scientific progress. UAP Gerb speculates — explicitly flagged as "total speculation" with no
  supporting evidence — that if Oppenheimer personally engaged with the alleged Manhattan Project
  2.0 UFO effort, he may have grown similarly disenchanted with its secrecy, drawing a parallel to
  the 1954 stripping of Oppenheimer's security clearance by the AEC (a process UAP Gerb
  characterizes as a "kangaroo court" masterminded by Lewis Strauss) as possibly connected to a
  need to remove him from the UFO legacy-program portfolio. *([secrecy-1](../raw/
  jR-h5p2bd-A_secrecy_1.md) 2:04:28–2:05:42)*
- The disputed [[interplanetary-phenomenon-unit|Interplanetary Phenomenon Unit]] report (dated 22
  July 1947) lists select Atomic Energy Commission scientists including Oppenheimer as part of a
  UFO crash-retrieval team. William Steinman's 1986 book *UFO Crash at Aztec* separately lists
  Oppenheimer among an alleged Aztec-case "saucer recovery scientific team," alongside three
  accused original [[majestic-12|Majestic 12]] members (Detlev Bronk, Lloyd Berkner, Jerome
  Hunsaker) and Dr. Carl A. Highland; UAP Gerb notes Steinman did not meticulously document his
  sourcing for this roster and cannot rule out it derived from the IPU documents or from
  [[robert-sarbacher|Robert Sarbacher]]'s comments to Steinman. *([secrecy-1](../raw/
  jR-h5p2bd-A_secrecy_1.md) 2:02:48–2:04:15)*
- [[robert-sarbacher|Robert Sarbacher]], in 1983 communications with William Steinman, stated he
  was aware Dr. John von Neumann was "definitely involved" in 1940s–1950s crash retrieval
  programs, and that he "strongly theorized" Oppenheimer was as well. *([secrecy-1](../raw/
  jR-h5p2bd-A_secrecy_1.md) 2:01:43–2:02:06)*
- In an embedded interview clip, [[david-grush|David Grush]] names Oppenheimer (alongside
  [[robert-sarbacher|Sarbacher]]) as one of two deceased figures he would most want to question
  about the 1940s–1950s "scrolling this away" — noting Oppenheimer "was the one who created the
  classification that included the UFO stuff." *([secrecy-1](../raw/jR-h5p2bd-A_secrecy_1.md)
  1:43:22–1:43:41)*
