H.G. Wells (1866–1946), the novelist, was a [[fabian-society]] member from 1903 to 1908 and a
founding member of the British [[eugenics-society]]. Green & Pleasant depicts him leaving the
Fabians on poor terms — he is shown "thumbing his nose" at the society in its own commissioned
stained-glass window.

## Claims

- Fabian Society member 1903–1908; founding member of the British Eugenics Society
  *([gp/secret-society](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-03-28 - The Secret Society Running Britain for 140 Years.md) 17:57)*.
- In *Anticipations* (1901), which he called "the keystone of his life's work," wrote of "those
  swarms of black and brown and dirty white and yellow people who do not come into the needs of
  efficiency... I take it they will have to go"
  *([gp/secret-society](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-03-28 - The Secret Society Running Britain for 140 Years.md) 18:06)*.
- Depicted, after quitting the Fabian Society, "thumbing his nose" at it in the 1910 Fabian
  Window stained-glass artwork designed by George Bernard Shaw
  *([gp/secret-society](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-03-28 - The Secret Society Running Britain for 140 Years.md) 25:13)*.

## Relationships

- Fabian Society member, 1903–1908 (left on bad terms).
- Founding member of the [[eugenics-society]].
- Depicted alongside [[george-bernard-shaw]] and [[sidney-webb]] in the Fabian Window.
