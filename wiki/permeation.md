"Permeation" is the [[fabian-society]]'s own term, per Green & Pleasant, for its founding
strategy: rather than building competing institutions, infiltrate and convert the people already
inside existing ones. The video frames it as the mechanism that turned a nine-person 1884
discussion group into a claimed shaper of British (and, via alumni, several Commonwealth
countries') governing policy for over a century.

## Claims

- George Bernard Shaw, in an 1892 published Fabian tract: "We permeated the party organizations
  and pulled all the wires we could lay our hands on with our utmost adroitness and energy." In
  1908 he described the Fabians' two explicit tasks: producing a parliamentary programme for a
  socialist-converted prime minister, and normalizing socialism as an "ordinary" political
  choice *([gp/secret-society](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-03-28 - The Secret Society Running Britain for 140 Years.md) 5:18, 5:48)*.
- Fabian historian Margaret Cole defined permeation as converting "key persons... in a position
  to take action," explicitly noting formal Fabian membership was unnecessary and often
  undesirable for those being influenced
  *([gp/secret-society](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-03-28 - The Secret Society Running Britain for 140 Years.md) 6:18)*.
- Institution-building as permeation infrastructure: the Fabians founded the [[london-school-of-economics]]
  (1895, explicitly "to teach political economy on more modern and more socialist lines" per
  Sidney Webb) and the [[new-statesman]] (1913), which Margaret Hay Thomas said was "penetrating
  right through Whitehall" via civil-service readership
  *([gp/secret-society](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-03-28 - The Secret Society Running Britain for 140 Years.md) 7:00, 7:23)*.
- International reach claimed via LSE/Fabian-influenced alumni: Jawaharlal Nehru ("vaguely
  attracted to the Fabians," mentored by early Fabian Annie Besant) and India's post-independence
  state-planning economic model; Lee Kuan Yew (studied at LSE, said he had been influenced by
  Fabian ideas but ultimately rejected them: "before distributing the pie, I had first to bake
  it"); Muhammad Ali Jinnah (described as an "avid member" in the early 1930s, though the video
  notes evidence suggests instrumental rather than ideological membership)
  *([gp/secret-society](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-03-28 - The Secret Society Running Britain for 140 Years.md) 7:51, 8:15, 8:31)*.
- Documented "pamphlet to law" pipeline examples: Sidney Webb's Clause IV in Labour's 1918
  constitution (replaced by Tony Blair using a Fabian pamphlet as justification); Ed Balls's 1992
  Fabian pamphlet arguing for Bank of England independence, read by Gordon Brown and enacted five
  days into the 1997 Labour government; a 2022–2024 Unison-commissioned Fabian roadmap that
  reportedly became Labour's 2024 national-care-service manifesto commitment; and — the longest
  pipeline cited — Beatrice Webb's 1909 Minority Report on the Poor Laws, which the video traces
  through to the 1942 Beveridge Report and the 1948 NHS, 39 years later
  *([gp/secret-society](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-03-28 - The Secret Society Running Britain for 140 Years.md) 9:00, 9:38, 9:59, 10:31)*.
