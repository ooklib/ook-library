Covert action programs, defined by 50 U.S. Code § 3093, cover US government activities
(assassination, clandestine operations, and other actions the government does not want to
acknowledge) that are still nominally required to be reported to the congressional Gang of
Eight/Gang of Four. Gerb, drawing heavily on [[david-grush|David Grush]]'s Judicial Watch
interview, argues legacy UFO programs have historically exploited a "White House SAP" designation
to escape even this reporting.

## Claims

- Per Grush: "there's White House special access programs ... there was something called covert
  action programs, 50 U.S. Code 3093 — those are the programs reported [to] the gang of eight,
  gang of four ... but there's a way to develop a White House SAP that is non-covert action ...
  if you look in the law, there are no reporting carveouts" — one of the "tricks of the trade,"
  along with "burying it over in the Department of Energy" and keeping custodianship within a
  small circle ("five people ... know about it, and the president at one time").
  *([gerb/secrecy_2](../raw/gerb/secrecy_2.md) 0:46–1:41, 1:12:47–1:24:09)*
- Covert action programs and SAPs both use compartmentalization, security controls beyond top
  secret, and third-party/cover mechanisms, but differ in authority and purpose: covert action
  falls under 50 USC 3093, while SAPs operate under Executive Order 13526 and 10 USC 119.
  Covert action programs, when protected, are almost always additionally run as SAPs or
  SAP-like structures. *([gerb/secrecy_2](../raw/gerb/secrecy_2.md) 1:24:35–1:25:53)*
- Historically, covert-action approval authority resided entirely within the executive branch
  via the National Security Council's subcommittee on covert action — Eisenhower's
  [[nsc-5412-committee|5412 Committee/Special Group]] (1954–1964), then Nixon's 303 Committee
  (1964–1970), continuing in some unclear successor form through 1994 — because covert action
  approval was an NSC-governed process until the mid-1970s. Gerb ties this directly to his Vol. 1
  claim that the alleged [[majestic-12|MJ-12]]/"Match 12" group existed *within* the 5412
  Committee. *([gerb/secrecy_2](../raw/gerb/secrecy_2.md) 1:25:53–1:26:20)*
- **The loophole**: since the legacy program portfolio was, per Gerb, "primarily always a White
  House/National Security Council-run activity," an NSC-run covert action program can be
  restructured as a White House [[special-access-program|SAP]] and then given the **non-covert
  action** designation under 50 USC 3093. This removes presidential-finding/reporting
  requirements to Congress and intelligence committees; because the resulting activity is not
  generated as a DoD SAP, it also falls outside 10 USC 119/Title 10 requirements — leaving it in
  a reporting-free "gray area" between Title 10 and Title 50, answerable only to the NSC or
  cognizant executive authority. *([gerb/secrecy_2](../raw/gerb/secrecy_2.md) 1:28:44–1:30:18)*
- **Yankee White**, a real, properly-reported-to-Congress White House SAP (a single-scope
  background investigation standard for direct White House support personnel), is cited by Gerb
  as a known, legitimate example of a "White House SAP" — the template he argues legacy programs
  exploit by omitting the reporting step. *([gerb/secrecy_2](../raw/gerb/secrecy_2.md)
  1:28:54–1:29:09)*
- Combining the non-covert-action designation with the [[content-only-special-access-program|
  content-only]] budgetary designation, per Gerb, is "the solution" that fully waives both
  reporting/carveout requirements and budgetary visibility for legacy UFO SAPs.
  *([gerb/secrecy_2](../raw/gerb/secrecy_2.md) 1:34:04–1:34:37)*
- Distinguished from intelligence-community **Controlled Access Programs (CAPs)**, the IC's
  rough equivalent to DoD SAPs, which Gerb notes use similar mechanisms but declines to cover in
  depth in this video. *([gerb/secrecy_2](../raw/gerb/secrecy_2.md) 28:04–28:19)*
