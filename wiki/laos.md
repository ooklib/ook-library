Laos is, per [[eyes-wide-open]], a remote, nearly ungovernable, opium-rich country at the centre of the [[golden-triangle]] that became the site of the CIA's first full-scale secret war ([[secret-war-in-laos]]). The ASR renders the name as "La", "La os" and similar; the host explains he pronounces it with a sounded "s" and uses "Lao" as the demonym. *([ewa/laos-1](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-01 - The Secret War in Laos - South East Asia's Heart of Darkness - LAOS 1.md) 5:54-7:10)*

## Claims

- Truly remote and "nearly ungovernable" by colonial powers before 1954 and by the governments after independence; mostly rural tribal groups in border regions with little infrastructure, effective taxation hard and centralized rule of the periphery "an impossibility". *([ewa/laos-1](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-01 - The Secret War in Laos - South East Asia's Heart of Darkness - LAOS 1.md) 7:37-8:42)*
- Border security was lax; hill peoples ignored Western nation-state borders. The host says ~8 million ethnic Lao lived in northeastern Thailand in 1970 against ~1.5 million in Laos (allowing that some may have been refugees). Citing [[alfred-mccoy]], Lao nationhood was a "fiction" perpetuated by colonial powers and Cold War negotiators. *([ewa/laos-1](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-01 - The Secret War in Laos - South East Asia's Heart of Darkness - LAOS 1.md) 8:45-9:44)*
- Few strategic resources (some tin, tropical wood); its main asset was opium, and the northern highlands had ideal soil and altitude for poppies. The host stresses that most highland farmers were subsistence growers, not wealthy drug barons: rice for sustenance, poppies for profit. *([ewa/laos-1](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-01 - The Secret War in Laos - South East Asia's Heart of Darkness - LAOS 1.md) 10:04-11:41)*
- Served as the "Gateway through Southeast Asia" for contraband: caravan routes from the northern opium fields to Bangkok and to Saigon. *([ewa/laos-1](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-01 - The Secret War in Laos - South East Asia's Heart of Darkness - LAOS 1.md) 11:51-12:26)*
- Its supposed neutrality in the Vietnam War, centrality and lack of central authority caused struggles among neighbours for control of its peripheries and trade routes; the Ho Chi Minh Trail ran along its southeastern border, resupplying South Vietnam's National Liberation Front ("Viet Cong") and prompting the "devastating bombing campaign". *([ewa/laos-1](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-01 - The Secret War in Laos - South East Asia's Heart of Darkness - LAOS 1.md) 12:26-13:18)*
- Formally added to French Indochina in 1893; the French based themselves at Vientiane (ASR "vientian", "venon"), described as a quiet mountain village of tin-roofed huts. See [[french-indochina]]. *([ewa/laos-1](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-01 - The Secret War in Laos - South East Asia's Heart of Darkness - LAOS 1.md) 13:32-13:51)*
- Population about 3 million in the early 1970s, mostly ethnic Lao plus many minorities such as the [[hmong-people]]. *([ewa/laos-1](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-01 - The Secret War in Laos - South East Asia's Heart of Darkness - LAOS 1.md) 8:03-8:19)*

- LAOS 2: the site of the French Hmong maquis in [[operation-x]] and, under the Geneva side agreement, an independent Kingdom of Laos where France kept two bases (one at "Zino" per ASR, on the Thai border, and one near the capital), a 3,500-man garrison and 1,500 trainers for the Royal Lao Army. *([ewa/laos-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-08 - Operation X and the French Indochina War - LAOS 2.md) 30:00-30:07, 39:41-40:38)*

- LAOS 3: host restates that Laos was the smallest and least populated of the four post-Geneva states (about 5 million per this episode; LAOS 1 says about 3 million in the early 1970s: flagged), landlocked and dependent on Vietnam, at the center of the [[golden-triangle]] and only a minor opium producer in 1954. A CIA paper of August 1954, "Post Geneva Outlook in Indochina", said Laos's ability to retain control depends on developments in South Vietnam and French assistance. *([ewa/laos-3](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-15 - The French Connection in South East Asia - LAOS 3.md) 3:14-4:33, 50:56-51:23)*

- LAOS 4: three half-brother princes (Boun Oum, Souphanouvong, [[souvanna-phouma]]), the [[pathet-lao]], and the 1958-1960 CIA-backed coups: see [[laos-coups-1958-1960]]; Geneva 1954 barred foreign troops but not foreign civilians, so the CIA operated under civilian and USAID cover. *([ewa/laos-4](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-22 - The Paramilitary CIA and the Seeds of a Secret War - LAOS 4.md) 12:08-13:01, 17:30-18:29, 23:07-23:32)*
- LAOS 4: Lowland ethnic Lao dominated government and looked down on northern mountain peoples, including the [[hmong-people]]. The host calls the Plain of Jars (Xieng Khouang) the centerpiece of the future secret war, the main opium region and the target of the bombing. *([ewa/laos-4](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-22 - The Paramilitary CIA and the Seeds of a Secret War - LAOS 4.md) 19:01-19:41, 51:51-52:30)*
- LAOS 4: Host discrepancy: LAOS 4 dates the secret war roughly 1961-1972 and calls the Ho Chi Minh Trail campaign in the south a distinct, better-known war from 1959, versus LAOS 1's 1964-1973 bombing span. *([ewa/laos-4](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-22 - The Paramilitary CIA and the Seeds of a Secret War - LAOS 4.md) 1:34-2:56)*
- LAOS 4: 1962 Geneva neutrality agreement: the host says it handed Laos to the CIA. See [[operation-momentum]], [[operation-milpond]]. *([ewa/laos-4](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-22 - The Paramilitary CIA and the Seeds of a Secret War - LAOS 4.md) 1:17:31-1:18:39)*
