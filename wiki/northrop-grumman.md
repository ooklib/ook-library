Northrop Grumman has primary offices in [[crystal-city-virginia|Crystal City]] and is listed
with less confidence among contractors Gerb suspects are involved in MITRE's UFO work.
*([gerb/mitre](../raw/gerb/mitre.md) 39:36–39:44, 1:56:19–1:56:33)* Also cited earlier as an example private contractor (with
Lockheed) that the government would hesitate to give direct custody of recovered materials to.
*([gerb/mitre](../raw/gerb/mitre.md) 26:37–26:45)*

## Claims (from the SAIC video)

- 1999: purchased Teledyne Ryan while, separately, its subsidiary Newport News Shipbuilding
  joint-ventured with [[science-applications-international-corporation|SAIC]] to form
  [[amsc-llc|AMSC LLC]]. *([gerb/saic](../raw/gerb/saic.md) 1:28:26–1:28:51)*
- In 1985, [[science-applications-international-corporation|SAIC]] won more
  [[strategic-defense-initiative|SDI]]-related contracts than Northrop, General Dynamics,
  Raytheon, and RAND combined. *([gerb/saic](../raw/gerb/saic.md) 1:07:16–1:07:34)*
- Named alongside Lockheed, Boeing, and Teledyne Ryan as a primary builder of the
  [[alien-reproduction-vehicle-tr3b|TR-3B]], per Edgar Fuche. *([gerb/saic](../raw/gerb/saic.md)
  1:11:31–1:11:46)*

## Claims (from the Northrop Grumman & TRW Vol.2 video)

Gerb's "part two" investigation argues Northrop Grumman is the prime contractor he believes is
"most involved with clandestine UFO retrieval and reverse engineering operations" — the rare
exception among primes to have allegedly seized on early-2000s [[david-grush|David Grush]]-
described [[ufo-legacy-programs|IRAD "breakaway"]] opportunities to operate largely outside the
traditional legacy chain of command. Unlike [[lockheed-martin|Lockheed Martin]] (labeled by name
in congressional hearings and public whistleblower testimony), Gerb argues Northrop has deployed
"far more effective countermeasures" to stay out of the UFO public zeitgeist.
*([gerb/northrop_trw](../raw/gerb/northrop_trw.md) 13:13–14:22, 2:00:56–2:03:11)*

- Founded 1994 (Northrop Corporation + Grumman Corporation); today the fifth-largest US federal
  contractor and #100 on the Fortune 500. Operates four sections: aeronautic systems, mission
  systems, space systems, and defense systems — Gerb implicates mission, space, and aeronautic
  systems in alleged UFO legacy-program activity. Numerous acquisitions stem from the 1993
  "Last Supper" defense-industry consolidation, including Teledyne Ryan, elements of Sperry Rand,
  Litton Industries, Aerojet, and — "most importantly" per Gerb — Thompson Ramo Wooldridge
  ([[trw|TRW]]), acquired in 2002. *([gerb/northrop_trw](../raw/gerb/northrop_trw.md)
  16:12–18:46)*
- The 2002 TRW acquisition created two new Northrop sectors, mission systems and space
  technologies; by 2018 Northrop's acquisition of Orbital ATK formed NGIS (Northrop Grumman
  Innovative Systems), which merged with space technologies in 2020 into the modern space
  systems sector — meaning TRW's legacy still comprises two of Northrop's four mission sectors.
  *([gerb/northrop_trw](../raw/gerb/northrop_trw.md) 34:42–35:36)*
- Northrop's aeronautic systems division, headquartered in Palmdale, is one of three contractors
  (with [[lockheed-martin|Lockheed Martin]] and [[boeing|Boeing]]) with access to
  [[air-force-plant-42|Air Force Plant 42]] and, per Gerb, likely engages the
  [[hidden-wing-air-force-program|"hidden wing"]] western-range RDT&E programs alongside them.
  *([gerb/northrop_trw](../raw/gerb/northrop_trw.md) 36:26–38:22)*
- In 2023, 2022, and 2021, Northrop Grumman's reported IRAD expenses totaled $1.2B, $1.2B, and
  $1.1B respectively ($3.5B over three years); Gerb is more interested in the early-2000s figures:
  Northrop's IRAD spending "exploded" following the TRW acquisition, from $318–331M in 2001 to
  $429M (2003), $54[0]M (2004, likely a transcription of $540M), and $538M (2005) — coinciding
  with the onset of the war on terror. In 2003, shortly after the TRW purchase, Northrop paid
  $111.2M to settle a False Claims Act suit over TRW's 1990–1997 IRAD mischarging schemes.
  *([gerb/northrop_trw](../raw/gerb/northrop_trw.md) 1:57:42–2:09:24)*
- Gerb theorizes Northrop's [[northrop-advanced-technology-and-design-center|Northrop Advanced
  Technology and Design Center (NATDC)]], Northrop's answer to [[skunk-works|Lockheed's Skunk
  Works]], continues covert operation (likely out of Plant 42) constructing alien reproduction
  vehicles with minimal government-sponsor accountability, funded via self-directed IRAD.
  *([gerb/northrop_trw](../raw/gerb/northrop_trw.md) 2:58:01–2:58:32)*
- Gerb identifies [[terry-phillips|Terry Phillips]] (VP for security since 2023) as running the
  entire security apparatus for Northrop's alleged breakaway UFO programs, with
  [[john-c-freestone|John Freestone]], [[kathy-andrews|Kathy Andrews]], and
  [[vince-devine|Vince Devine]] as directors of security operating under him, plus
  [[michael-dempsey|Michael Dempsey]] (VP for government affairs) among the executive-level
  figures Gerb suspects engage with the UFO portfolio. *([gerb/northrop_trw](../raw/gerb/northrop_trw.md) 2:17:28–2:17:41, 2:50:44–2:51:02)*
- Northrop owned and operated the [[tahone-radar-cross-section-facility|Tahone RCS facility]]
  (purchased outright 1998, officially closed 2011, sold by late 2024), which Gerb believes still
  houses active underground UFO-legacy-program infrastructure behind a staged-derelict surface.
  *([gerb/northrop_trw](../raw/gerb/northrop_trw.md) 2:36:35–2:40:10)*
- Northrop spun off [[huntington-ingalls-industries|Huntington Ingalls Industries]] (HII), the
  largest US shipbuilder, in 2011. *([gerb/northrop_trw](../raw/gerb/northrop_trw.md)
  1:37:33–1:37:49)*
