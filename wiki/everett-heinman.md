Director of [[national-reconnaissance-office|NRO]] Program B (1982–1989), the joint NRO/CIA
program to build and operate SIGINT satellites, ground-based at [[pine-gap-australia|Pine Gap,
Australia]] — where Heinman was the facility's first chief. Also served as
[[cia-directorate-of-science-and-technology|CIA DS&T]] deputy director.
*([gerb/nro](../raw/gerb/nro.md) 23:17–23:36, 2:39:00–2:39:49)*

## Claims

- In a 1989 phone call, Admiral [[bobby-ray-inman|Bobby Ray Inman]] told NASA mission specialist
  [[bob-oechsler|Bob Oechsler]] that Heinman would be "the best person to ask" whether recovered UFO vehicles could
  become available for technological research outside military circles, describing Heinman as
  "a person of very substantial integrity." Heinman and Oechsler subsequently met at CIA
  headquarters, Langley, on 10 August 1989, where Heinman denied any knowledge of UFOs or UFO
  legacy programs. *([gerb/nro](../raw/gerb/nro.md) 2:41:13–2:43:14)*
- In 2022, when contacted on Twitter by user RGH_UFOs about Inman's comments, Heinman did not
  deny knowledge but replied he was "a long way and quite a few years from working in the area
  you are researching," offering to answer questions but never responding again. Gerb also
  attempted to contact Heinman without response. *([gerb/nro](../raw/gerb/nro.md)
  2:43:14–2:43:21)*
- Named by an NRO "leaders" retrospective as "one of the NRO's most distinguished engineers,
  analysts, and weapons technologists." *([gerb/nro](../raw/gerb/nro.md) 2:39:17–2:39:38)*

- [[trw|TRW]] Systems Group's Special Projects unit (led in 1965 by chief scientist Bill Carson)
  built NRO Program B's SIGINT satellites during Heinman's 1982–1989 directorship.
  *([gerb/northrop_trw](../raw/gerb/northrop_trw.md) 1:25:56–1:26:32)*
