[[uap-gerb]] selects four ship-sighting cases from [[project-blue-book]]'s files as USO or transmedium examples, all attributed here to Blue Book records as he relays them. Of about 13,000 sightings, 258 (about 2%) were ships' sightings, 13 rated good; Gerb cites a report on maritime UFO research giving 104 Atlantic versus 155 Pacific, suggesting a Pacific hotspot but not an isolated one. *([gerb/uso-casebook](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-04-26 - USO Case Book_ Unidentified Submerged Objects Throughout History.md>) 1:17-6:27)*

## Claims

- MV Marcala, North Atlantic, 4 August 1950, 10 p.m. EDT: the master, chief mate and third mate saw an elliptical object ("half an egg cut lengthwise", shiny aluminum to metallic white) pass within about 1,000 ft, hovering under 100 ft above the sea at 25-500 mph with a rotary, wobbling motion for about a minute. Blue Book lists it as unidentified and Gerb calls it his favorite case. *([gerb/uso-casebook](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-04-26 - USO Case Book_ Unidentified Submerged Objects Throughout History.md>) 1:40-2:36)*
- SS City of Almaco (spelled "alako" in the transcript), north of Hawaii, 7 November 1959: three witnesses saw a bright object streak across the water from 4 miles away, flash three times and submerge; the ship diverted but found nothing. Blue Book labeled it a meteor, which Gerb doubts given the low pursuit angle. He later ties the triple flashes to Australian cases. *([gerb/uso-casebook](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-04-26 - USO Case Book_ Unidentified Submerged Objects Throughout History.md>) 2:36-3:18,)*
- ORV (operation range vessel) Whiskey, near Ascension Island, 25 June 1960: a small boat retrieving a missile-test data cassette saw a steady white or yellowish light appear from under the surface 100 yd from the cassette, on or near the surface without breaking waves, for 10 seconds; witnesses included an RCA photographer, recovery divers and the recovery plane's pilots. Blue Book said flare, but no plane or vessel released one. Gerb compares it to [[luis-elizondo]]'s missile-recovery story from his previous video. *([gerb/uso-casebook](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-04-26 - USO Case Book_ Unidentified Submerged Objects Throughout History.md>) 3:18-4:36)*
- SS Morgantown Victory, 11 January 1966, about 1,000 miles southeast of Tokyo: the third mate, helmsman and lookout saw an orange-yellow cigar-shaped object (estimated 200-250 ft long) approach within a mile, turn 180 degrees, hover about 30 seconds and change direction over 3 minutes before entering the sea; the master believed a plane had crashed and searched. Blue Book blamed Cosmos 33 satellite decay, which Gerb says does not fit the hovering and direction changes. *([gerb/uso-casebook](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-04-26 - USO Case Book_ Unidentified Submerged Objects Throughout History.md>) 4:36-5:47)*
