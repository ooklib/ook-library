A call sign for objects entering or leaving Earth's atmosphere, slowwalker for low velocity and fastwalker for high velocity. [[uap-gerb]] argues in [[gerb-fastwalkers-video]] that the term originated with [[norad]] and that it lets the government avoid FOIA obligations because requests for "UFO" or "UAP" material do not match it. Per two technical papers the term itself denotes uncataloged objects seen by reflected sunlight moving too fast to be in the atmosphere; that these are UFOs is Gerb's framing.

## Claims

- Definition: the walker call sign covers objects entering or leaving the atmosphere at variable speeds, and Gerb says the distinction helps the US government avoid FOIA requests. *([gerb/fastwalkers](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-02-27 - FASTWALKERS - UFOs Outside Earth.md>) 0:59-1:33)*
- FOIA 2023: in late 2023 John Greenwald of [[black-vault]] got a full denial from the US Space Force on fastwalker and slowwalker requests, citing FOIA Exemption 1 (classified by executive order in the interest of national defense or foreign policy). *([gerb/fastwalkers](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-02-27 - FASTWALKERS - UFOs Outside Earth.md>) 1:33-2:15)*
- FOIA 2013: a similar 2013 request went to USNORTHCOM, which oversees [[norad]]; the response said the documentation is currently and properly classified under Executive Order 13526, and only two papers were released. *([gerb/fastwalkers](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-02-27 - FASTWALKERS - UFOs Outside Earth.md>) 2:15-3:25)*
- FOIA 1979: Gerb cites the book Clear Intent (Greenwood and Fawcett), which contains a scanned NORAD UAP reporting sheet and a FOIA chapter in which NORAD answered a request for UAP tracking data with over 18,000 man-hours and $155,000 (over half a million dollars today); NORAD refused to waive fees on a follow-up three years later. *([gerb/fastwalkers](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-02-27 - FASTWALKERS - UFOs Outside Earth.md>) 3:34-4:22)*
- Paper 1: "Orbit Determination of Sunlight Illuminated Objects Detected By Overhead Platforms" (1989) by USAF Captain Richard P. (name transcribed "OSX"), describing joint NORAD and [[air-force-foreign-technology-division]] efforts on uncataloged objects detected by reflected sunlight, recorded since 1972 by ballistic missile early warning satellites in geostationary positions; the orbit problem was deemed unsolvable by earlier work, and the paper says analysis grew more important as geosynchronous satellites increased, since ground sensors cannot detect them. *([gerb/fastwalkers](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-02-27 - FASTWALKERS - UFOs Outside Earth.md>) 4:43-5:56)*
- Paper 2: "Space Based Satellite Tracking and Characterization Utilizing Non-Imaging Passive Sensors" (2008) by Captain Bradley R. Townson of the Air Force Institute of Technology, which revisits the orbit problem, describes fastwalkers as objects moving too fast relative to the background to be in the atmosphere, and again fails to determine range. *([gerb/fastwalkers](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-02-27 - FASTWALKERS - UFOs Outside Earth.md>) 6:21-7:21)*
- Hynek: on 6 January 1967 [[j-allen-hynek]] allegedly told Goddard Space Flight Center that NORAD tracks UFOs and has since 1957, and that every continental US military case in the [[project-blue-book]] files is designated a NORAD case. *([gerb/fastwalkers](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-02-27 - FASTWALKERS - UFOs Outside Earth.md>) 7:21-7:53)*
- Conclusion: Gerb says the term is not new and can be traced to the 1976 Iran case and the 1984 DSP case. *([gerb/fastwalkers](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-02-27 - FASTWALKERS - UFOs Outside Earth.md>) 20:47-21:11)*
