The "Lao crisis" of 1958-1960, as told by [[eyes-wide-open]] in LAOS 4: after independence [[laos]] split among three half-brother princes (neutralist [[souvanna-phouma]], leftist Prince Souphanouvong of the [[pathet-lao]], and right-wing Boun Oum), and Washington, unhappy with Souvanna's unity government, allegedly used aid, the CIA and the State Department to replace it with a reliably anti-communist client, [[phoumi-nosavan]]. All of this is the host's account, drawn from Martin Stuart-Fox, Peter Dale Scott, Alfred McCoy, Arthur Dommen and others; the wiki records it as an attributed claim. *([ewa/laos-4](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-22 - The Paramilitary CIA and the Seeds of a Secret War - LAOS 4.md) 12:08-12:36)*

## Claims

- Three princes: after 1954 the parliament, the Royal Lao Army and guerrilla groups were the real power centres; the three half-brother princes led the three sides of the later civil war. *([ewa/laos-4](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-22 - The Paramilitary CIA and the Seeds of a Secret War - LAOS 4.md) 12:08-13:01)*
- Aid as lever: Bernard Fall is quoted saying Laos was "the only country in the world where the United States supports the military budget 100%"; Stuart-Fox is quoted for over $120 million of US aid 1955-1958, about four times what France spent in the previous eight years. The implicit condition was excluding the Pathet Lao from politics. *([ewa/laos-4](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-22 - The Paramilitary CIA and the Seeds of a Secret War - LAOS 4.md) 21:02-22:01)*
- 1957-58 unity: in 1957 Souvanna and Souphanouvong agreed to integrate Pathet Lao troops into the army in return for political recognition; by New Year 1958 Laos was, for the first time, fully unified. US ambassador J. Graham Parsons told Congress he had "struggled for 16 months to prevent a coalition". *([ewa/laos-4](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-22 - The Paramilitary CIA and the Seeds of a Secret War - LAOS 4.md) 30:00-31:31)*
- Operation Booster Shot: before the May 1958 special election the CIA airdropped about 1,500 tons of supplies to Pathet Lao strongholds in the north, one of the first uses of [[civil-air-transport]] in postwar Laos; host calls it a failure that fed Pathet Lao propaganda. *([ewa/laos-4](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-22 - The Paramilitary CIA and the Seeds of a Secret War - LAOS 4.md) 32:15-32:35, 34:23-34:37)*
- 1958 election: 21 seats, Pathet Lao 9, neutralists 4, right wing 8; the host calls it surprisingly free and fair and the last genuinely democratic election in Laos. *([ewa/laos-4](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-22 - The Paramilitary CIA and the Seeds of a Secret War - LAOS 4.md) 33:47-34:16)*
- Aid cut-off: the State Department "in essence cut all aid" in mid-1958; a June 4, 1958 embassy telegram is quoted urging a merger of conservative parties into an anti-communist government. Souvanna resigned in late July 1958 (becoming ambassador to France) and Phoui Sananikone took over, banned the Pathet Lao and dismissed parliament; aid resumed. *([ewa/laos-4](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-22 - The Paramilitary CIA and the Seeds of a Secret War - LAOS 4.md) 35:07-36:42)*
- Escalation: on Dec 28, 1958 [[allen-dulles]] told the National Security Council "drastic changes in Laos government will be required" and that "we have a great deal to fear in the 1959 general elections"; Arthur Schlesinger is quoted that CIA operatives then set up a Committee for the Defense of the National Interest (CDNI), bringing back from France as its chief the ambitious officer [[phoumi-nosavan]]. *([ewa/laos-4](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-22 - The Paramilitary CIA and the Seeds of a Secret War - LAOS 4.md) 37:07-38:06)*
- CIA station chief Henry Hecksher (ASR "hexer"), an OSS/CIA veteran of Berlin counterintelligence, the 1954 Guatemala coup ([[operation-pbsuccess]]) and, per the host, a colleague of "Ted Shockley" (ASR; presumably Ted Shackley, not otherwise identified here), helped Phoumi form a coup group under the CDNI. *([ewa/laos-4](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-22 - The Paramilitary CIA and the Seeds of a Secret War - LAOS 4.md) 38:22-39:20)*
- Manufactured pretext: in March 1959 the CIA allegedly sent KMT guerrillas (the ones supplied under [[operation-paper]]; see [[li-mi]]) across into western Laos, supplied by civil transport planes, forcing Phoui's army to guard the west. Pathet Lao and Vietnamese guerrillas then stepped up activity in the east along the Ho Chi Minh Trail, which the host calls the birth of the trail. Western press called this the "invasion crisis". *([ewa/laos-4](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-22 - The Paramilitary CIA and the Seeds of a Secret War - LAOS 4.md) 40:11-41:09)*
- Embedded journalism: the host suggests Joseph Alsop, who had worked with Chennault's Flying Tigers ([[claire-chennault]]) and earlier for the CIA at its request, was probably tipped to appear in Sam Neua in 1959 to shape coverage; photos show him with a general the transcript calls "Juan radicon" (normalized here to [[ouane-rattikone]] by my inference), later the Lao Army officer in charge of opium production. Speculation. *([ewa/laos-4](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-22 - The Paramilitary CIA and the Seeds of a Secret War - LAOS 4.md) 41:22-42:37)*
- First [[air-america]] planes landed in Vientiane in 1959 (NYT, Aug 23, 1959), speculated to have delivered equipment to Phoumi's coup forces. *([ewa/laos-4](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-22 - The Paramilitary CIA and the Seeds of a Secret War - LAOS 4.md) 42:37-43:10)*
- Coup: per Peter Dale Scott the CIA gave the go-ahead on Dec 30, 1959 for Phoumi to march into Vientiane; he took the capital in a bloodless coup. In 1958-59 the country had three governments, one soft coup and one military coup, both heavily instigated by foreign meddling. *([ewa/laos-4](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-22 - The Paramilitary CIA and the Seeds of a Secret War - LAOS 4.md) 43:16-44:06)*
- April 1960 election: host calls it "laughably illegitimate": in Sam Neua the Pathet Lao got 13 votes to the winner's 6,108; Dommen is quoted that CIA agents rigged the election "with or without the authority of the American ambassador", and a Foreign Service officer reported seeing CIA agents give bags of money to village headmen in Phoumi's hometown Savannakhet. *([ewa/laos-4](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-22 - The Paramilitary CIA and the Seeds of a Secret War - LAOS 4.md) 44:18-45:37)*
- Kong Le coup, Aug 9, 1960: neutralist paratroop captain [[kong-le]] took Vientiane while Phoumi and the CDNI were at Luang Prabang, demanding an end to corruption and foreign interference and restoring [[souvanna-phouma]]. Around early September Phoumi, per McCoy, broke off unity negotiations and set up a Revolutionary Committee in Savannakhet; air America planes began landing there from Bangkok. By December 1960, after three days of street fighting, Kong Le retreated, Boun Oum became prime minister and Phoumi defense minister with the real power. *([ewa/laos-4](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-22 - The Paramilitary CIA and the Seeds of a Secret War - LAOS 4.md) 45:45-48:20)*
- Aftermath: the CIA and Pentagon had begun ramping up paramilitary/counterinsurgency training from about 1959; the civil war started with Phoumi's re-entry in December 1960 and lasted to 1975. *([ewa/laos-4](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-22 - The Paramilitary CIA and the Seeds of a Secret War - LAOS 4.md) 49:03-49:34)*
