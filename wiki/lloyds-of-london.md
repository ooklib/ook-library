Lloyd's of London is the marine (and later general) insurance market that grew out of Edward
Lloyd's late-17th-century coffee house in the City of London, where merchants and ship owners met
to arrange insurance. Green & Pleasant treats it as the clearest documented example of the
Square Mile's financial engine being directly implicated in the transatlantic slave trade, a
claim the video says Lloyd's itself has now formally acknowledged.

## Claims

- Grew out of a coffee house opened by Edward Lloyd in the late 1600s, becoming, over more than a
  century, the center of the world's marine insurance market
  *([gp/older-than-parliament](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-06-03 - The Government Inside Britain That's Older Than Parliament.md) 11:07, 11:15, 11:23)*.
- That market insured the transatlantic slave trade: by Lloyd's own published account, cited in
  the video, slavery-related business made up between a third and 40% of maritime insurance
  premium income in the second half of the 18th century — ships, and their enslaved human
  "cargo," insured in the City of London. In 2020 Lloyd's formally apologized, calling its role
  "an appalling and shameful period of history"
  *([gp/older-than-parliament](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-06-03 - The Government Inside Britain That's Older Than Parliament.md) 11:29, 11:37, 11:45, 11:53)*.
- [[john-julius-angerstein]], "sometimes called the father of Lloyd's," is cited as having been
  formally identified as a trustee of a slave plantation in Grenada
  *([gp/older-than-parliament](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-06-03 - The Government Inside Britain That's Older Than Parliament.md) 11:53, 12:02)*.
- The former headquarters site of the [[east-india-company]] on Leadenhall Street is, per the
  video, now occupied by the Lloyd's of London building — "the spot where a company governed a
  subcontinent for a century," with no plaque marking that history
  *([gp/older-than-parliament](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-06-03 - The Government Inside Britain That's Older Than Parliament.md) 12:41, 12:50)*.

## Relationships

- Headquartered within the [[city-of-london-corporation]]'s "Square Mile," having grown out of a
  17th-century City coffee house.
- Associated with [[john-julius-angerstein]], described as "the father of Lloyd's."
- Its present building occupies the former [[east-india-company]] headquarters site.
