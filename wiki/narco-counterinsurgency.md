Eyes Wide Open's term for a CIA strategy in which armed proxies are given a short initial injection of
aid and then fund themselves with the narcotics trade, off the books and beyond congressional
oversight; the host says [[operation-paper]] is its blueprint. *([ewa/operation-paper](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2023-10-04 - THE HISTORY OF THE CIA - Operation PAPER and Trouble in the Golden Triangle [pt. 6].md) 30:40-31:55, 49:31-49:51)*

## Claims

- Two rationales: untraceable profits give plausible deniability, and the force becomes self-reliant.
  The host says it recurs in Afghanistan, Nicaragua, Guatemala and Laos. *([ewa/operation-paper](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2023-10-04 - THE HISTORY OF THE CIA - Operation PAPER and Trouble in the Golden Triangle [pt. 6].md) 30:40-31:49)*
- Paired with a "client state" stepping stone (Thailand for Burma) and proprietary firms
  ([[civil-air-transport]], [[sea-supply-corporation]]). *([ewa/operation-paper](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2023-10-04 - THE HISTORY OF THE CIA - Operation PAPER and Trouble in the Golden Triangle [pt. 6].md) 48:39-49:31)*
- LAOS 1 claims the [[french-indochina]] administration set up 'some of the first narco counterinsurgency programs ever', later repurposed by the CIA, and calls Laos 'the test run' for decades of narco-fueled guerrilla wars; see [[secret-war-in-laos]]. *([ewa/laos-1](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-01 - The Secret War in Laos - South East Asia's Heart of Darkness - LAOS 1.md) 4:16-4:58)*

- LAOS 2 is the development: the host names [[operation-x]] as the French narco-counterinsurgency program of the [[first-indochina-war]], "arguably the single most impactful innovation to counterinsurgency work during the beginning of the Cold War", "refined later by the CIA and Edward Lansdale". Its formula: a self-funding indigenous army paid by the drug trade, off the books. *([ewa/laos-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-08 - Operation X and the French Indochina War - LAOS 2.md) 17:11-17:41, 18:03-18:11)* [[allen-dulles]] was "particularly impressed" and the host says CIA versions would be "much more deadly". *([ewa/laos-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-08 - Operation X and the French Indochina War - LAOS 2.md) 48:04-48:39)*
- Precedent cross-reference: the host says the CIA had reached the same conclusion in Burma with [[operation-paper]]. *([ewa/laos-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-08 - Operation X and the French Indochina War - LAOS 2.md) 48:13-48:21)*

- LAOS 3: the host extends the model to South Vietnam: the Ngo brothers, with CIA and Corsican help, tapped Laos opium to fund the counterinsurgency and secret police; Laos opium is described as "an untraceable and openly accessible cash source" that whichever power needed covert funds, first the French, then Nhu, could tap. Lansdale and Conein are contrasted as public and shadow sides of the same policy. *([ewa/laos-3](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-15 - The French Connection in South East Asia - LAOS 3.md) 48:44-49:57, 41:08-41:39)*

- LAOS 4: host argues Lao opium profits funded the Royal Lao Army and Hmong army, letting the CIA run a war with no on-the-books US military personnel; in 1962 [[phoumi-nosavan]] assigned covert opium funding to [[ouane-rattikone]] ($35,000 a month, per McCoy). *([ewa/laos-4](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-22 - The Paramilitary CIA and the Seeds of a Secret War - LAOS 4.md) 10:33-11:03, 50:16-50:56)*
- LAOS 4: proprietaries (SEA Supply and [[world-commerce-corporation]]) as the earlier template for the privatized CIA institutionalized by the secret-war men. *([ewa/laos-4](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-22 - The Paramilitary CIA and the Seeds of a Secret War - LAOS 4.md) 1:02:40-1:03:38)*
