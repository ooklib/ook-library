The Wilson-Davis notes are disputed but (per Gerb) "highly likely accurate" notes transcribed
by physicist [[eric-davis|Dr. Eric Davis]], leaked from the estate of late astronaut
[[edgar-mitchell|Edgar Mitchell]]. They recount a 2002 conversation between Davis and Vice
Admiral (and former DIA director) [[thomas-wilson|Thomas Wilson]] in the parking lot of
contractor [[eg-and-g|EG&G]] in Nevada. *([gerb/saic](../raw/gerb/saic.md) 34:39–35:19)*

## Claims

- Wilson recounts finding, but being stonewalled from accessing, UFO recovery/material
  exploitation programs — protected by the DoD [[special-access-program-oversight-committee|
  SAP Oversight Committee (SAPOC)]] Senior Review Group (SRG) and "a large defense contractor,"
  which Gerb wagers is [[science-applications-international-corporation|SAIC]].
  *([gerb/saic](../raw/gerb/saic.md) 35:19–35:35)*
- Per Wilson, he was told by General Michael Ward to search a special records group in the
  Office of the Under Secretary of Defense for Acquisition and Technology (OUSDA&T) belonging to
  legacy holdover programs predating early-1990s SAP restructuring.
  *([gerb/saic](../raw/gerb/saic.md) 35:35–35:52)*
- Per the notes, UFO programs were reorganized under SAPOC/SRG after an internal audit "almost
  revealed" them; this reorganization began 5 January 1994, when the Deputy Secretary of
  Defense significantly strengthened DoD SAP management/control structures.
  *([gerb/saic](../raw/gerb/saic.md) 35:52–36:23)*
- A GAO May 1993 report ("DoD Special Access Programs: Administrative Due Process Not Provided
  When Access Is Denied or Revoked") discusses Navy/Air Force noncompliance with DoD regulation
  62000.2/R on SAP access; per Gerb, the Army complied more consistently — matching his broader
  sense that Army UFO material-exploitation programs operated in a stricter, controlled
  environment than the more "loose" Navy/Air Force legacy programs.
  *([gerb/saic](../raw/gerb/saic.md) 36:23–37:16)*
- SAPOC/SRG, per Wilson, was chaired at reorganization time by Deputy Secretary of Defense
  [[john-m-deutch|John Deutch]] — though the restructuring itself was conducted by his
  predecessor, [[bill-perry|William Perry]]; Deutch immediately followed suit in the chair role,
  having just come from Under Secretary of Defense for Acquisition and Technology (the same
  office Wilson learned held the UFO legacy files). *([gerb/saic](../raw/gerb/saic.md)
  37:23–37:56)*
- Deutch also served as a consultant to TRW, [[los-alamos-national-laboratory|Los Alamos]], and
  [[mitre-corporation|MITRE]] before SAPOC work, and (pre-1995, possibly during his DepSecDef
  tenure) as director of [[science-applications-international-corporation|SAIC]], working in
  SAIC's La Jolla office — the same office as [[denise-mckenzie|Denise McKenzie]].
  *([gerb/saic](../raw/gerb/saic.md) 38:11–38:51)*
- William Perry, who conducted the 1994 SAP reorganization, once served on SAIC's board.
  *([gerb/saic](../raw/gerb/saic.md) 39:14–39:32)*
- Wilson spoke to two permanent SAPOC members: Brig. Gen. Michael Costanik and
  [[paul-kaminski|Paul Kaminski]] (USD A&T, preceded by Deutch); Kaminski and permanent SRG
  member [[linton-wells-ii|Lynn (Linton) Wells]] are both lifelong MITRE Corporation members.
  *([gerb/saic](../raw/gerb/saic.md) 39:32–39:49)*
- A permanent SAP member post-restructuring: Admiral [[william-a-owens|William A. Owens]],
  former VCJCS, senior military assistant to [[dick-cheney|Dick Cheney]], and SAIC CEO. Gerb
  notes 2 of 6 SAPOC personnel Wilson identified were former SAIC alumni — supporting his
  identification of SAIC as the "large defense contractor" from the notes.
  *([gerb/saic](../raw/gerb/saic.md) 39:49–40:25)*
- A "critical third man": Naval Reserve Commander [[willard-miller|Willard Miller]] attended a
  1997 meeting with VADM Wilson and [[steven-greer|Steven Greer]] (confirmed to have taken
  place); during the EG&G meeting, Davis showed Wilson a 2002 letter from Miller offering to aid
  Davis and [[hal-puthoff|Hal Puthoff]] with UFO crash-retrieval research, including access to a
  senior official with ARV knowledge, a list of civilian DoD contractors involved in UFO crash
  retrieval, and a possible crash-retrieval team. *([gerb/saic](../raw/gerb/saic.md)
  40:33–41:22)*

- In his first-ever channel video, Gerb wagered the unnamed "large defense contractor" Wilson
  refused to name was [[lockheed-martin|Lockheed Martin]]; after his SAIC investigation he now
  favors [[science-applications-international-corporation|SAIC]] instead, but still considers
  Lockheed a critical, shadowed piece of the SAPOC reorganization story — particularly a 1986 GAO
  audit of classified-document control failures at Lockheed's Burbank (Skunk Works) plant, which
  he theorizes could have been a near-exposure event driving the 1993–94 restructuring. See
  [[lockheed-martin#1986 GAO carveout security audit]]. *([gerb/lockheed](../raw/gerb/lockheed.md) 1:33:51–1:35:11, 1:42:18–1:44:26)*

## Claims (from the "Secrecy" Vol. 1 video)

- UAP Gerb states he now possesses "supremely high confidence, extremely high confidence" —
  independent of Eric Davis or "the whole circular reporting crew" — that the notes' account of a
  "watch committee" telling Wilson their program was "formed out of necessity to protect
  themselves after a near disaster in the past almost blew their cover... something to do with an
  agreement reached with a couple of Pentagon SES's overseeing SAPs" describes a real historical
  sequestering event: he ties this "near disaster" audit directly to the 1983
  [[yellow-fruit|Yellow Fruit]] scandal and mid-1980s DoD SAP audits, arguing this chain of events
  drove the final 1994 restructuring under [[bill-perry|William Perry]] discussed in the notes.
  *([gerb/secrecy_1](../raw/gerb/secrecy_1.md) 2:45:06–2:45:33, 3:08:04–3:08:36)*

## Claims (from "Special Access Required" Vol. 2)

- Wilson found the legacy program record within the then-Office of the Under Secretary of
  Defense for Acquisition and Technology (OUSDA&T, now
  [[under-secretary-of-defense-for-acquisition-and-sustainment|USD A&S]]) contained **zero
  budget information** — kept in separate records "for audit purposes." Gerb calls this "probably
  the most critical piece of the entire Wilson-Davis notes," directly corroborating his
  [[content-only-special-access-program|content-only SAP]] theory. Per the notes, then-Army
  Chief of Staff-level official Gen. Michael Costanik separately stated the legacy program's
  *security* budget alone ran 2–3x (at times as high as 6–7x) the actual program budget — a
  figure Gerb argues is consistent with (not contradictory to) a content-only funding structure,
  since the attributable program budget itself is deliberately minimized/hidden.
  *([gerb/secrecy_2](../raw/gerb/secrecy_2.md) 57:48–58:31, 1:09:12–1:09:52)*
- The notes' account of the [[special-access-program-oversight-committee|SAPOC/SRG]] blocking
  Wilson — "I was to immediately drop the matter ... it didn't fall within my oversight" — is
  reaffirmed by Gerb as an accurate description of SAPOC/SRG's gatekeeping function, distinct
  from the separate [[ufo-control-group|control group]]. *([gerb/secrecy_2](../raw/gerb/secrecy_2.md)
  1:19:56–1:20:19)*
- Air Force General H. Marshall Ward — former [[special-access-program-central-office|SAPCO]]
  director, former executive secretary of SAPOC, and former director of special programs within
  the modern-day USD A&S — is again named as the official who told Wilson about the special
  records group. *([gerb/secrecy_2](../raw/gerb/secrecy_2.md) 57:14–57:47)*
