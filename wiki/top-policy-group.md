The Top Policy Group was the small, tight-knit civilian/political oversight body through which
the president retained personal control over the Manhattan Project while bypassing normal
government channels to preserve secrecy. UAP Gerb treats it as the direct historical template for
his alleged [[ufo-control-group|UFO control group]] atop the "Manhattan Project 2.0" legacy
program pyramid. *([gerb/secrecy_1](../raw/gerb/secrecy_1.md) 1:24:39–1:25:04)*

## Claims

- Initial membership: President FDR (replaced by Truman upon FDR's death), Vice President Henry
  Wallace, Secretary of War Henry Stimson, Army Chief of Staff [[george-c-marshall|George
  Marshall]], [[vannevar-bush|Vannevar Bush]] of the Office of Scientific Research and
  Development, and Bush's deputy James Conant. *([gerb/secrecy_1](../raw/gerb/secrecy_1.md)
  1:25:04–1:25:27)*
- When Truman became FDR's running mate for the 1944 election, he was not briefed into the
  Manhattan Project; he only received a briefing from the Top Policy Group after taking office in
  1945. As a senator, Truman had earlier chaired the "Truman Committee" reviewing defense programs
  and witnessed millions of dollars funneled into a vaguely-labeled "expediting production"
  project without explanation; when Truman pressed Stimson for answers, Stimson told him only that
  the information was classified to protect national security. *([gerb/secrecy_1](../raw/gerb/secrecy_1.md) 1:25:27–1:26:28)*
- UAP Gerb theorizes Truman carried the Top Policy Group model forward onto the alleged Manhattan
  Project 2.0, relegating oversight in 1947 to the newly formed
  [[national-security-council|National Security Council]]; Eisenhower, shortly after taking
  office, "deepsixed" this UFO top-policy-group model one level further by creating the
  [[nsc-5412-committee|NSC's 5412 Committee/Special Group]] in 1954 — following the same
  principles (small readin membership, minimized paper trail, presidential control through a
  trusted intermediary). *([gerb/secrecy_1](../raw/gerb/secrecy_1.md) 1:32:35–1:32:53,
  1:36:48–1:37:24)*
