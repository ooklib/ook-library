An interview dated 3 December 1990 in which [[brad-sorenson]] told William B. Scott, senior engineering editor of Aviation Week and Space Technology, about the 1988 Norton exhibit. Gerb says he found the interview notes in "documents never before seen discussed" and believes it is the only time Sorenson spoke about the show outside conversations with [[mark-mccandlish]]. Scott had received McCandlish's sketches in December 1990 and reached out to Sorenson; the ARV segment was never published, and Gerb notes Scott wrote he had trouble believing the flying-saucer material. *([gerb/mccandlish](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-10-21 - Alien Reproduction Vehicle - the Testimony of Mark McCandlish.md>) 22:39-23:20,)*

## Claims (gerb/mccandlish)

- Sorenson was not officially invited: he was passed off as the aide of a man who had served in the Carter administration (Gerb notes [[frank-carlucci]] was Carter's deputy CIA director). He said he took part because he was a Democrat who opposed the military spending; the meeting was for appropriating billions to develop new craft, shown to about 30 senior people aged 45-60 to attract investors. *([gerb/mccandlish](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-10-21 - Alien Reproduction Vehicle - the Testimony of Mark McCandlish.md>) 23:29-24:11)*
- Briefers were specialists in suits or Air Force colonel/major uniforms; no generals were noticed. He also saw the V-22 Osprey and depleted-uranium shell talk, and Senator Alan Cranston. *([gerb/mccandlish](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-10-21 - Alien Reproduction Vehicle - the Testimony of Mark McCandlish.md>) 24:11-25:08)*
- The Aurora was an unmanned, 100-110 ft flattened football with internal engines and pulse detonation engines for Mach 6-8, boasted to reach every Soviet city over a million within 1h20m; the exhibit also had B-2 competitor prototypes. Asked if the Aurora was the most exotic thing on display, he said no ([[aurora-astra]]). *([gerb/mccandlish](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-10-21 - Alien Reproduction Vehicle - the Testimony of Mark McCandlish.md>) 25:08-26:07)*
- McCandlish's ARV drawing was so accurate he could add little. The craft were described as needing no fuel source, using something "everywhere in the universe" 500 times light speed; attendees were told only three existed and that they had "been everywhere in the solar system and there's no life" (corrected from "universe"). *([gerb/mccandlish](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-10-21 - Alien Reproduction Vehicle - the Testimony of Mark McCandlish.md>) 26:07-28:24)*
- Asked what the vehicles were produced from, the non-uniformed presenter said something about 1947: a contact told the US it had no right to use nuclear weapons and to cease and desist, the US attacked, the visitors tried to destroy their hardware and leave, but not all was destroyed; the presenters said they could not make the found craft work and built their own, "built for our consumption", copying what they saw. *([gerb/mccandlish](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-10-21 - Alien Reproduction Vehicle - the Testimony of Mark McCandlish.md>) 28:33-29:57)*
- Gerb stresses this converts McCandlish's second-hand story into first-hand testimony, and that Sorenson never learned who the vehicles belonged to or whom the men reported to; the men wanted the Bush administration to fund a fleet as Reagan would have. *([gerb/mccandlish](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-10-21 - Alien Reproduction Vehicle - the Testimony of Mark McCandlish.md>) 30:04-31:39)*
