The City of London Corporation is the local governing body of the "Square Mile" — the historic
financial district at the heart of London, distinct from the modern Greater London Authority —
which Green & Pleasant presents as a self-governing institution roughly 958 years old, predating
Parliament, the Bank of England, and the office of Prime Minister. It has its own Lord Mayor, its
own police force, a business-weighted electoral system, and a permanent, unelected official (the
[[remembrancer]]) embedded inside the House of Commons. The video frames it as the fourth
"Hidden Government of Britain" the channel has investigated, after the [[fabian-society]] and
[[chatham-house]], and its central, source-stated thesis is that the Corporation's interests have
shaped UK financial policy continuously across parties and decades. As with the wiki's other
Green & Pleasant sources, all claims below are attributed to that source and its cited
public/primary records, not independently verified by this wiki.

A second, independent source, tax-justice economist [[richard-j-murphy]]'s "The secret power
behind the City of London," covers the same institution but is explicit first-person advocacy
rather than documentary narration: Murphy argues the Corporation is the epicenter of the global
offshore tax haven network and calls for its abolition as a political entity. His claims are
recorded separately below and are flagged as his own political-economy argument, not established
fact.

## Claims

### Ancient liberties and legal foundation

- 1066: William of Normandy conquered England, then, after harshly suppressing rebellion
  elsewhere ("the harrying of the north"), stopped at the gates of London and negotiated rather
  than fought. In 1067 he issued the city a charter — a 6-inch strip of vellum still on the
  UNESCO Memory of the World register — promising to leave its property alone. Historians
  describe the charter as granting "nothing new," only confirming rights the city already held,
  implying the city's autonomy predates the Norman Conquest
  *([gp/older-than-parliament](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-06-03 - The Government Inside Britain That's Older Than Parliament.md) 1:36, 1:52, 2:09)*.
- 1215 Magna Carta, Clause 13: "The city of London shall enjoy all its ancient liberties and free
  customs by land and by water." London is the only city named anywhere in the document. Of the
  roughly 63 clauses, only three remain live law in England 800 years on — one protecting the
  church, one guaranteeing a fair trial, and Clause 13. The barons, distrusting King John,
  appointed a 25-strong committee to enforce the charter; the sole commoner on it was the Mayor of
  London, giving the city both a permanent guarantee of its privileges and a seat on the body
  enforcing the whole document
  *([gp/older-than-parliament](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-06-03 - The Government Inside Britain That's Older Than Parliament.md) 2:28, 2:52, 3:00, 3:21, 3:29)*.
- Clarification stressed in the video: there are two distinct "Mayors of London." The Mayor of
  London (currently Sadiq Khan) heads the Greater London Authority, a role created in 2000. The
  Lord Mayor of London is a different, much older office (dating to 1189) running only the
  Square Mile. Britain had no Prime Minister until Robert Walpole in 1721 — making the Lord
  Mayoralty 532 years older than the premiership; by the time Britain had its first PM, the city
  had already worked through 671 Lord Mayors
  *([gp/older-than-parliament](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-06-03 - The Government Inside Britain That's Older Than Parliament.md) 3:44, 3:52, 4:01, 4:09, 4:18, 4:26)*.

### A state within a state

- The City has its own police force, separate from the Metropolitan Police, wearing a distinct
  red-and-white checker (every other UK force wears blue-and-white) — the only one of its kind in
  the country. It has its own flag and coat of arms, and ten silver dragon statues mark the road
  entrances to the Square Mile as an unmarked border
  *([gp/older-than-parliament](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-06-03 - The Government Inside Britain That's Older Than Parliament.md) 5:57, 6:04, 6:10, 6:17)*.
- A ceremony for the monarch: the King's carriage halts at the Old Temple Bar on Fleet Street and
  the Lord Mayor offers the pearl sword (a gift from Elizabeth I set with roughly 2,500 pearls);
  the King touches the hilt and the procession continues. The video debunks the popular claim
  that the monarch legally needs the Lord Mayor's permission to enter the city — the Corporation
  itself denies this — but confirms a real, still-observed convention that armed troops ask the
  Lord Mayor's leave before marching through the Square Mile
  *([gp/older-than-parliament](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-06-03 - The Government Inside Britain That's Older Than Parliament.md) 6:26, 6:41, 6:49, 6:56)*.

### The business vote

- The City is governed by the Court of Common Council, elected via two separate voter registers:
  residents and businesses. A registered company gets votes by headcount (a small firm gets a
  couple; a large one up to 79); the individuals who cast those votes are chosen by company
  management, not staff, and the company need not even be British — a foreign-owned bank can
  nominate voters in a British election
  *([gp/older-than-parliament](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-06-03 - The Government Inside Britain That's Older Than Parliament.md) 7:05, 7:13, 7:20, 7:27, 7:35, 7:44)*.
- Britain abolished business voting in general elections in 1948 and in every other local
  election in 1969 — except the City. In 2002 a Labour government passed a private act that
  *expanded* the business vote (more company categories, more nominated voters). At the most
  recent election cited, businesses cast 13,748 votes against 6,475 resident votes: the roughly
  8,600 people who live in the Square Mile are outvoted better than two to one by their own
  landlords — the only square mile of British soil where companies outvote citizens, entirely
  legally
  *([gp/older-than-parliament](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-06-03 - The Government Inside Britain That's Older Than Parliament.md) 0:09, 0:21, 7:51, 7:58, 8:09, 8:19, 8:26, 8:32, 8:40)*.

### Financial empire and the offshore network

- Late 1600s: Edward Lloyd's coffee house in the City became the meeting point for merchants
  arranging marine insurance, growing into [[lloyds-of-london]]. See that page for the
  slave-trade-insurance and Angerstein/National Gallery detail.
- The [[east-india-company]], chartered 1600 and headquartered on Leadenhall Street, at its
  height governed roughly 60 million people in India with a private army at times about twice
  the size of Britain's own; it grew opium in India and sold it into China against Chinese law,
  and when China tried to stop it, Britain sent the Royal Navy — the wars that made Hong Kong
  British. The company's former headquarters site now hosts the Lloyd's of London building, with,
  per the video's on-the-ground research, no plaque marking a century of subcontinental governance
  (only a nearby plaque commemorating a postage stamp)
  *([gp/older-than-parliament](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-06-03 - The Government Inside Britain That's Older Than Parliament.md) 12:10, 12:17, 12:25, 12:33, 12:41, 12:50)*.
- Formal empire "ended" roughly in 1956: during the Suez Crisis, the United States refused to
  support the pound and Britain's attempt to retake the canal collapsed within weeks. Per Green &
  Pleasant, the City's business did not end with the empire — it moved offshore. As colonies
  gained independence, a network of British-linked financial centers grew in their place: the
  Crown Dependencies and Overseas Territories (Jersey, the Cayman Islands, the British Virgin
  Islands, Bermuda), "British enough to offer the cover of British law, separate enough to write
  their own rules on tax and secrecy"
  *([gp/older-than-parliament](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-06-03 - The Government Inside Britain That's Older Than Parliament.md) 12:50, 13:03, 13:07, 13:14, 13:22, 13:31)*.
- [[tax-justice-network]] and a 2017 documentary, *The Spider's Web*, are cited naming this
  arrangement "Britain's Second Empire," with the City as the spider and the territories as its
  web. Per the Tax Justice Network's most recent aggregate estimate (cited by the video), the
  United Kingdom and these territories together account for close to a quarter of all corporate
  tax revenue lost worldwide — "a quarter from one country and the islands it never quite let go
  of." Green & Pleasant notes the method has critics but calls the scale "hard to wave away"
  *([gp/older-than-parliament](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-06-03 - The Government Inside Britain That's Older Than Parliament.md) 13:39, 13:49, 13:57, 14:05, 14:14, 14:23)*.

### Modern political influence

- 1986: Margaret Thatcher's government deregulated the London Stock Exchange in a single day
  ("Big Bang") after the City had wanted it for years. Twenty-two years later the deregulated
  banking system collapsed; the House of Commons Library puts the peak cash cost of the resulting
  bailout at £137 billion, borne by the British taxpayer. The video's framing: "the City lobbied
  for the rules. The public paid when they broke"
  *([gp/older-than-parliament](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-06-03 - The Government Inside Britain That's Older Than Parliament.md) 14:31, 14:41, 14:50, 15:00)*.
- 2022: Liz Truss became Prime Minister; her chancellor's budget of large unfunded tax cuts
  triggered a bond-market reaction within hours, borrowing costs rose so sharply that the Bank of
  England had to intervene to stop pension funds collapsing, and Truss resigned after 49 days —
  the shortest premiership in British history. Green & Pleasant's summary: "Liz Truss was elected
  by Conservative members, appointed by the Queen and removed by the bond market"
  *([gp/older-than-parliament](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-06-03 - The Government Inside Britain That's Older Than Parliament.md) 15:07, 15:15, 15:22, 15:30)*.
- Every year the Chancellor of the Exchequer delivers a major regulatory-policy speech at Mansion
  House — the Lord Mayor's official residence — as the City's guest, rather than at the
  parliamentary dispatch box. In July 2025, Labour Chancellor Rachel Reeves used that speech to
  announce the "Leeds Reforms," rolling back parts of the post-2008-crash banking regulation and
  calling regulation "a boot on the neck of business." The video's point: a Labour chancellor,
  inside the Lord Mayor's house, unwound safeguards a Labour government had built after the last
  financial crisis — "the government changed, the party changed, and the city's position did not
  move an inch"
  *([gp/older-than-parliament](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-06-03 - The Government Inside Britain That's Older Than Parliament.md) 15:39, 15:47, 15:54, 16:02, 16:09, 16:14, 16:22)*.
- Overarching thesis (explicitly Gerb-style attribution, this time to Green & Pleasant): "every
  government of the last 40 years has had to pass an interview the public never saw" — Big Bang,
  the 2008 bailout, the Truss budget, and the Reeves Mansion House speech are framed as one
  continuous story rather than separate ones
  *([gp/older-than-parliament](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-06-03 - The Government Inside Britain That's Older Than Parliament.md) 1:03, 1:10, 1:18, 1:28)*.

### Richard Murphy's political-economy argument (rjm/city-of-london)

- Murphy frames the Corporation as "a state within a state" whose lobbying drives "the
  anti-democratic attack by the city on the UK" more broadly than the well-known charge that
  deregulated finance harms British industry
  *([rjm/city-of-london](../raw/rjm/Richard J Murphy - 2026-05-17 - The secret power behind the City of London.md) 0:29, 0:37)*.
- Murphy argues London itself, not the "tropical islands with brassplate offices," is the
  epicenter of the global tax haven system; he names Jersey, Guernsey, an ASR-mangled place name
  transcribed as "the Alaman" (likely the Isle of Man or Alderney — this wiki does not resolve
  it), Cayman, Gibraltar, Bermuda, and the British Virgin Islands as outposts connected outward
  from that London center
  *([rjm/city-of-london](../raw/rjm/Richard J Murphy - 2026-05-17 - The secret power behind the City of London.md) 1:43, 1:56, 2:03)*.
- Murphy claims the Lord Mayor of London is treated as if a UK cabinet minister when traveling
  abroad, receiving honors as a consequence — offered as evidence of the Corporation's
  "privileged access to ministers and policy makers," a specific claim not made in the gp source
  *([rjm/city-of-london](../raw/rjm/Richard J Murphy - 2026-05-17 - The secret power behind the City of London.md) 2:40, 2:47)*.
- Murphy's characterization: the Corporation "is simply a lobbying organization for finance while
  pretending to be a civic body," operating "within the UK constitution and yet ... far apart
  from it"
  *([rjm/city-of-london](../raw/rjm/Richard J Murphy - 2026-05-17 - The secret power behind the City of London.md) 2:52, 3:00, 3:03)*.
- Murphy argues the Corporation's historic role was never to serve the broader UK economy but "to
  protect capital from democratic accountability" and to extend British financial power abroad;
  he calls this continuation "colonization in action" — formal empire ended, but he argues the
  financial structures it created evolved into today's offshore network
  *([rjm/city-of-london](../raw/rjm/Richard J Murphy - 2026-05-17 - The secret power behind the City of London.md) 3:14, 3:22, 3:38, 3:44)*.
- Murphy's three-function model of the offshore network: (1) permitting wealth to escape
  taxation, (2) permitting wealth to escape regulation via opaque shell-company/trust structures,
  and (3) undermining democratic governments by giving mobile capital a credible exit threat that
  constrains elected policy — he calls the offshore world "the aircraft carriers of the
  anti-democracy movement"
  *([rjm/city-of-london](../raw/rjm/Richard J Murphy - 2026-05-17 - The secret power behind the City of London.md) 3:52, 4:14, 4:28, 4:37)*.
- Murphy states, as a claim about his own record, that he created country-by-country reporting —
  a system requiring large multinationals to report their tax-haven operations to tax
  authorities — and that he was "instrumental" in lobbying for automatic information exchange
  between tax havens and originating tax authorities; he says both measures weakened but did not
  eliminate offshore secrecy
  *([rjm/city-of-london](../raw/rjm/Richard J Murphy - 2026-05-17 - The secret power behind the City of London.md) 5:12, 5:18, 5:28, 5:44)*.
- Murphy argues the City "has consistently promoted deregulation and financial liberalization"
  and the ideology that "what is good for finance is good for Britain," which he calls
  "demonstrably wrong"; he attributes to City dominance a broader list of economic harms —
  weakened productive investment, deepened regional inequality, housing turned into a speculative
  asset, abandoned industrial policy, and asset bubbles replacing real economic development
  *([rjm/city-of-london](../raw/rjm/Richard J Murphy - 2026-05-17 - The secret power behind the City of London.md) 6:08, 6:17, 6:22, 6:34, 6:47)*.
- Murphy claims that, at the time of this video's publication, commentary about a possible change
  of Labour leadership included predictions that UK interest rates would be forced up by the City
  if the leader "goes," which he cites as a live example of City leverage over government; he
  separately calls the incumbent Labour leader "the leader of the Labor Party in the interest of
  bankers and not in the interest of workers." (The ASR transcript renders the leader's name as
  "Karmama"; this wiki does not resolve the reference to a specific named individual.)
  *([rjm/city-of-london](../raw/rjm/Richard J Murphy - 2026-05-17 - The secret power behind the City of London.md) 6:56, 7:04, 7:08, 7:12)*.

### Why this stays unmentioned, and proposed reforms

- Green & Pleasant's stated reason nobody in mainstream politics names the Corporation directly:
  both major parties' front benches draw money and people from the City, and the press typically
  calls it "the markets" rather than "the corporation"
  *([gp/older-than-parliament](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-06-03 - The Government Inside Britain That's Older Than Parliament.md) 16:46, 16:55, 17:03)*.
- Three reforms are proposed as non-radical: (1) extend the 1969 ban on business voting to the
  Square Mile; (2) withdraw the Remembrancer's parliamentary seat, as one MP proposed in 2013;
  (3) require the offshore territories to publish genuinely public beneficial-ownership
  registers — a reform Parliament voted for in 2018 and, per the video, still has not
  implemented. Green & Pleasant argues no mainstream party will act because every party needs the
  City more than the City needs them
  *([gp/older-than-parliament](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-06-03 - The Government Inside Britain That's Older Than Parliament.md) 17:17, 17:23, 17:32, 17:40, 17:48, 17:56)*.
- Murphy proposes a more radical reform than Green & Pleasant's: the Corporation "should ... be
  abolished as a separate political entity" and its corporate voting rights removed entirely,
  rather than merely extending the 1969 business-voting ban to the Square Mile
  *([rjm/city-of-london](../raw/rjm/Richard J Murphy - 2026-05-17 - The secret power behind the City of London.md) 9:27, 9:49)*.
- Murphy's other proposed reforms: public registers of beneficial ownership for both offshore
  companies and trusts; full public accounts for all offshore-registered companies; rigorous
  independent monitoring of the automatic information exchange system (rather than reliance on
  local regulators he says may be "captured" by local finance industries); and sanctions —
  specifically tax withholding on money flowing into non-compliant "secrecy jurisdictions"
  *([rjm/city-of-london](../raw/rjm/Richard J Murphy - 2026-05-17 - The secret power behind the City of London.md) 10:01, 10:12, 10:22, 10:39, 10:44)*.
- Murphy's closing framing: elected governments "must govern for the people who elected them and
  not for the benefit of mobile capital," and he calls explicitly for what he terms a "revolution"
  to suppress the City's power — presented throughout as his own normative/political argument,
  not a claim of documented fact
  *([rjm/city-of-london](../raw/rjm/Richard J Murphy - 2026-05-17 - The secret power behind the City of London.md) 10:51, 10:59, 11:01)*.

## Relationships

- Created and operates the [[remembrancer]] office, embedding a permanent, unelected official in
  the House of Commons since 1571.
- Alleged to have influenced [[margaret-thatcher]] (1986 Big Bang deregulation),
  [[liz-truss]] (2022 mini-budget collapse), and [[rachel-reeves]] (2025 Mansion House "Leeds
  Reforms" speech) — presented as one continuous 40-year pattern rather than three isolated
  events.
- Financial institutions historically centered in the Square Mile include [[lloyds-of-london]]
  and the [[east-india-company]].
- Investigated, alongside its offshore-territory network, by [[tax-justice-network]].
- Fourth subject in Green & Pleasant's documentary series, after [[fabian-society]] and
  [[chatham-house]] — a shared-series framing, not a stated institutional or personnel link, per
  this wiki's convention of only recording relations the source itself asserts.
- Investigated and argued against by tax-justice economist [[richard-j-murphy]], who frames the
  Corporation as the epicenter of the global offshore tax haven network and calls for its
  abolition as a political entity and the removal of its corporate voting rights.

## Speculation flag

Green & Pleasant frames this video, like its predecessors in the series, as sourced from public
records (charters, Magna Carta, Hansard, Charity Commission/House of Commons Library figures,
the Corporation's own public statements) rather than speculation. Per this wiki's schema, all
substantive assertions above — including causal framing (e.g. that the Big Bang directly caused
the 2008 crash, or that "every government" has effectively answered to the City) — are
nonetheless attributed to Green & Pleasant as claims, not independently verified by this wiki.

Richard Murphy's video, by contrast, is explicit first-person advocacy/opinion rather than
documentary narration — he states his own political conclusions directly ("this corporation
should then be abolished," "we need a revolution") rather than presenting them as findings of
investigation. His claims above are recorded per this wiki's "claims, not fact" convention as his
own political-economy argument, including his claims about his own record (country-by-country
reporting, automatic information exchange lobbying).
