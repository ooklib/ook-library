South Vietnam's president, killed in 1963. Per Eyes Wide Open, [[lucien-conein]] eventually gave the green light to South Vietnamese generals to kill him, one of the last defining moments of the Kennedy presidency. *([ewa/detachment-202](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2023-09-04 - SPY STORIES OSS Detachment 202 - Secret Cold Warriors [pt. 5.4].md) 16:35-16:45)*

## Claims

- Killed in 1963 with alleged Conein sign-off. *([ewa/detachment-202](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2023-09-04 - SPY STORIES OSS Detachment 202 - Secret Cold Warriors [pt. 5.4].md) 16:35-16:45)*

- LAOS 2: the host says Diem's family was among the "significant Catholic convert community" allied with the French in Saigon, and that the [[binh-xuyen]] pirates "would challenge his authority almost as soon as he became president" (deferred to LAOS 3). He was to be established before the 1956 elections, and [[edward-lansdale]] was sent to advise him. *([ewa/laos-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-08 - Operation X and the French Indochina War - LAOS 2.md) 1:38-1:45, 25:04-25:19, 41:52-42:08, 46:43-46:49)*

- LAOS 3 (main biography): born 1901 to a Catholic bureaucrat father in the French-installed court, one of nine children, unmarried and presumed celibate; briefly interior minister for the French in Tonkin and Annam, resigning within three months (per Thomas Ahern) in protest at French interference, then led a minor nationalist group in WWII and spent most of the war in exile after refusing offers from both Ho Chi Minh and the French. *([ewa/laos-3](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-15 - The French Connection in South East Asia - LAOS 3.md) 6:44-8:51)*
- With his brother Bishop Ngo Dinh Thuc (ASR "tuk") he toured for Western backers, meeting [[wesley-fishell]] in Tokyo in 1950; Fishell escorted him to [[francis-spellman]], [[joseph-kennedy]], John F. Kennedy and in spring 1951 [[william-donovan]], the group later called the [[vietnam-lobby]]. The host argues Diem was handpicked by US intelligence, specifically the CIA, not just by the US. *([ewa/laos-3](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-15 - The French Connection in South East Asia - LAOS 3.md) 8:51-12:11)*
- Bao Dai offered him the premiership in mid-1954; the host says the circumstances are obscure, and that State/CIA reportedly helped elevate him (John W. Haynes: support was "rammed through single-handedly through our intelligence and military communities"; Richard Bissell reportedly noted deep CIA involvement). He was formally installed as president in the 1955 election, the monarchy abolished by referendum, and "as has been recently revealed" CIA cash was used by [[edward-lansdale]] to rig the election. *([ewa/laos-3](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-15 - The French Connection in South East Asia - LAOS 3.md) 26:14-28:33)*
- He refused the 1956 unification election mandated by [[geneva-conference-1954]], citing communist intimidation; the host says he would likely have lost, and notes a declassified 1954 remark by [[allen-dulles]] to the NSC that the evidence showed a majority in Vietnam supported the Viet Minh, and disputes whether Diem chose or was told not to hold elections. *([ewa/laos-3](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-15 - The French Connection in South East Asia - LAOS 3.md) 28:33-30:09)*
- His "personalism" is described as a shifting ideology, in practice authoritarian, Catholic-chauvinist and family-based; he installed relatives, most prominently [[ngo-dinh-nhu]]. Diem spoke fluent English but reportedly only spoke to Lansdale through an interpreter. *([ewa/laos-3](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-15 - The French Connection in South East Asia - LAOS 3.md) 36:02-37:21, 52:02-52:09)*
- Suffered from the sect crisis ([[battle-of-saigon-1955]]) and later Buddhist unrest; his regime drew on a revived Laos-to-Saigon opium trade ([[air-laos-commerce]]); host previews his 1963 overthrow and murder with [[lucien-conein]] overseeing it. *([ewa/laos-3](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-15 - The French Connection in South East Asia - LAOS 3.md) 32:28-33:36, 47:26-47:39, 50:10-50:17)*
