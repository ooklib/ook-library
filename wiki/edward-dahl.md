Dr. Edward Bushnell Dahl (1912–1998; the source transcript's ASR renders his surname
inconsistently as "Dah," "Doll," and "Dole") earned a PhD in electrical engineering and served on
the Manhattan Project's Project Alberta (the section responsible for designing, procuring, and
assembling the atomic bomb shapes dropped on Hiroshima and Nagasaki). Gerb calls him a "critical
and underreported figure" in the 1953 [[kingman-arizona-crash-1953|Kingman, Arizona]] UFO crash
retrieval, tracing his subsequent career from crash-retrieval coordinator to senior TRW executive
as a template for how 1950s-era crash-retrieval personnel "parlay into valuable and actionable
study of figures today." *([gerb/northrop_trw](../raw/gerb/northrop_trw.md) 19:26–20:12,
3:13:09–3:14:04)*

## Claims

- Named by primary Kingman witness Arthur Stansel Jr. as the man who summoned him to the crash
  site, telling him he would "go on a special job." Stansel's own diary, dated 20 May 1953,
  records: "funny call from Dr. D about 1000. I'm going on a special job tomorrow." Researcher
  Raymond Fowler attempted to track Dahl down with little success, while Bill Moore (who admitted
  operating within UFO circles on behalf of the intelligence community) allegedly ran interference
  against Fowler by contacting Dahl himself. *([gerb/northrop_trw](../raw/gerb/northrop_trw.md)
  1:15:34–1:17:14)*
- On 27 March 1953 (two months before the Kingman crash), [[sri-international|SRI International]]
  supplied key scientific assistance to the upcoming Operation Upshot–Knothole atomic tests,
  and Dahl was tasked as director of the military effects group for that test series (a joint
  DoD/Atomic Energy Commission organization). *([gerb/northrop_trw](../raw/gerb/northrop_trw.md)
  1:19:56–1:20:23)*
- In 1959, served as technical director for two major special-weapons programs under the
  [[armed-forces-special-weapons-project|Armed Forces Special Weapons Project (AFSWP)]].
  *([gerb/northrop_trw](../raw/gerb/northrop_trw.md) 1:20:31–1:20:44)*
- Joined TRW's Space Technology Laboratories (STL) in 1955, was elected VP of STL by 1959, and
  by 1966 was promoted to senior VP within TRW Systems Group (STL's renamed successor, which
  spearheaded TRW's entire electronics and defense sector, including ICBM/Titan missile
  development and the Apollo 11 lunar module). Integrated into TRW's corporate side as group
  assistant general manager in 1971; retired in 1977 (Gerb's outro says 1979) as executive VP for
  TRW Systems Group. *([gerb/northrop_trw](../raw/gerb/northrop_trw.md) 1:23:08–1:24:17,
  3:16:09–3:16:23)*
- Following the Manhattan Project, became chairman of Stanford University's physics department
  and a senior member of [[sri-international|SRI International]]. *([gerb/northrop_trw](../raw/gerb/northrop_trw.md) 1:17:19–1:17:26)*
- Gerb theorizes that whatever "secret legacy information, materials, and projects" Dahl carried
  into TRW Systems Group are now the property of Northrop Grumman via its 2002 TRW acquisition,
  through [[northrop-grumman|Northrop's]] mission systems and space systems divisions.
  *([gerb/northrop_trw](../raw/gerb/northrop_trw.md) 1:51:12–1:52:17)*
