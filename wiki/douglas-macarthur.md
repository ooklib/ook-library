General Douglas MacArthur was Supreme Commander of Allied forces in the Pacific in WWII and chose [[charles-willoughby]] as his intelligence chief. Willoughby 1 (Dec 19 2025) touches him only briefly; the MacArthur-focused episode Willoughby 2 (Dec 25 2025, "50 Years of Empire Building in the Pacific") supplies the family, career and WWII Pacific detail below, all as [[eyes-wide-open]]'s account and opinion rather than verified fact.

## Claims

- Willoughby's "second hero" after [[francisco-franco]]; Willoughby reportedly toasted MacArthur as the greatest general in the world. *([ewa/willoughby-1](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-12-19 - America’s Fascist Spymaster - WILLOUGHBY 1.md) 34:23-34:48)*
- MacArthur called Willoughby "my friendly fascist" (host: "endearingly"). *([ewa/willoughby-1](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-12-19 - America’s Fascist Spymaster - WILLOUGHBY 1.md) 26:31-26:38,)*
- Host: MacArthur was the youngest brigadier general in the US Army at the end of WWI, awarded the Distinguished Service Cross by Pershing, and selected Willoughby as intelligence chief "for the duration of the conflict and beyond". *([ewa/willoughby-1](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-12-19 - America’s Fascist Spymaster - WILLOUGHBY 1.md) 16:59-17:19)*
- Host: during WWII MacArthur "continued to stonewall" [[office-of-strategic-services]] attempts to operate in the Pacific War, feeding a quiet rivalry between [[william-donovan]]'s OSS and Willoughby's Army G-2 (detail promised later). *([ewa/willoughby-1](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-12-19 - America’s Fascist Spymaster - WILLOUGHBY 1.md) 14:14-14:39)*
- Preview of the next episode: an hour-20 documentary on MacArthur's "obsession with an American Imperium in the Pacific", his resentment of the "Eastern elite", and how the traits rubbed off on Willoughby. *([ewa/willoughby-1](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-12-19 - America’s Fascist Spymaster - WILLOUGHBY 1.md) 36:20-36:56)*

## Willoughby 2: the MacArthur legacy (ewa/willoughby-2)

Source: "The MacArthur Legacy: 50 Years of Empire Building in the Pacific - Willoughby 2" (2025-12-25; [[eyes-wide-open]] season 3 finale). Framing: MacArthur was "the single man who most influenced" [[charles-willoughby]]; the host calls him "the US's last truly legendary military leader" (stature, "not in any moral sense") whose "growing and increasingly destructive ego" and independence brought conflict with the War Department and "the Wall Street Yankee bluebloods who architected the post-war world order". *([ewa/willoughby-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-12-25 - The MacArthur Legacy - 50 Years of Empire Building in the Pacific - WILLOUGHBY 2.md) 0:17-1:16)*

### Family and formation
- Son of [[arthur-macarthur-jr]] (China-market memorandum, Philippine military governor, feud with [[william-howard-taft]]); grandson of a Scottish-born judge. The host says father's outcast status and the Taft "clique" shaped Douglas's resentment of Eastern civilian elites; he saw the Philippines as "a sacred mission to fulfill the ambitions his father had been prevented from reaching". *([ewa/willoughby-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-12-25 - The MacArthur Legacy - 50 Years of Empire Building in the Pacific - WILLOUGHBY 2.md) 4:02-4:21, 17:15-17:34, 23:05-23:32)*
- Host: three Wall Street "patricians" (Taft, Root, Roosevelt) -- see [[william-howard-taft]] and [[elihu-root]] -- for the Skull and Bones and [[council-on-foreign-relations]] links. *([ewa/willoughby-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-12-25 - The MacArthur Legacy - 50 Years of Empire Building in the Pacific - WILLOUGHBY 2.md) 8:43-11:08)*
- West Point admission delayed to 1899 (age 19); bullied by mostly Southern cadets; top of his class; posted to the Philippines in 1903 as an engineer lieutenant, befriending Manuel Quezon and Sergio Osmena. Participated in the 1914 Veracruz expedition (host notes the [[pancho-villa-expedition]] came shortly after). *([ewa/willoughby-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-12-25 - The MacArthur Legacy - 50 Years of Empire Building in the Pacific - WILLOUGHBY 2.md) 21:28-22:32, 23:38-24:36)*
- WWI: organized the 42nd "Rainbow" Division (27 National Guard units) as its chief of staff, youngest brigadier general in the AEF, decorations including the Distinguished Service Cross "alongside" [[william-donovan]], who commanded the Fighting 69th under him; host suggests tension because Donovan received the Medal of Honor and MacArthur did not (MacArthur got it for WWII). *([ewa/willoughby-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-12-25 - The MacArthur Legacy - 50 Years of Empire Building in the Pacific - WILLOUGHBY 2.md) 24:49-26:30, 1:00:57-1:01:34)*
- Paranoia thesis: relationships with Pershing and his aide [[george-c-marshall]] soured in WWI; host quotes Manchester's *American Caesar* that MacArthur believed "deskbound men" wanted to get him, "the beginning of his paranoia". Host says [[charles-willoughby]] came to share MacArthur's paranoia and resentment of "an amorphous east coast elite, an old school army establishment and a secret communist threat". *([ewa/willoughby-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-12-25 - The MacArthur Legacy - 50 Years of Empire Building in the Pacific - WILLOUGHBY 2.md) 26:36-29:07)*
- 1919-22 West Point superintendent: introduced the cadet honor code and civil-affairs courses; frustrated by the old brass. Host notes he later avoided West Pointers on his own staff, preferring atypical officers and civilians with local connections. *([ewa/willoughby-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-12-25 - The MacArthur Legacy - 50 Years of Empire Building in the Pacific - WILLOUGHBY 2.md) 29:13-31:40, 42:35-43:06, 59:14-59:25)*
- 1932 Bonus Army: as Army chief of staff he ordered Major George Patton to lead infantry, cavalry and five tanks against WWI veterans; bayonets and tear gas used; death toll unclear per host. He believed the marchers were a communist conspiracy, though army intelligence found only 3 of about 26 leaders were Communist Party members. Host: this made him "one of the most visible faces of American anti-communism" and shows the impulse behind his choice of generals. *([ewa/willoughby-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-12-25 - The MacArthur Legacy - 50 Years of Empire Building in the Pacific - WILLOUGHBY 2.md) 31:45-34:02)*
- 1930-34 affair with Filipina vaudeville actress Isabel Rosario Cooper (host: aged 16, 18 or 21; MacArthur was 50), hidden in a Georgetown apartment, ended with a $15,000 payment; she died by suicide in 1960 (host, citing Manchester). Host calls this his "lowest" and "statutory". *([ewa/willoughby-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-12-25 - The MacArthur Legacy - 50 Years of Empire Building in the Pacific - WILLOUGHBY 2.md) 38:24-40:40)*
- 1935: posted as military adviser to the Philippine Commonwealth (President Quezon); Field Marshal of the Philippine Army in 1936; brought his mother. Dwight Eisenhower ([[dwight-d-eisenhower]]) served as his aide 1935-39; host says they were "oil and water", the seed of a lifelong rivalry culminating at the 1952 Republican convention. *([ewa/willoughby-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-12-25 - The MacArthur Legacy - 50 Years of Empire Building in the Pacific - WILLOUGHBY 2.md) 36:02-36:26, 40:40-42:35)*
- Staff of "rubber stampers": host cites Joseph Rauh's 1989 oral history (staff 1942-45) saying he "can't think of anybody that wasn't" a rubber stamp. *([ewa/willoughby-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-12-25 - The MacArthur Legacy - 50 Years of Empire Building in the Pacific - WILLOUGHBY 2.md) 46:35-47:20)*

### Pacific War
- Host (his reading): MacArthur saw a Pacific war as both likely and an opportunity, staffing up with intelligence and civil-affairs men to shape the Philippines "in the American mold". On 26 July 1941 Roosevelt created US Army Forces in the Far East under MacArthur, a week before the oil embargo -- the host's inference that FDR ([[franklin-d-roosevelt]]) anticipated Japanese retaliation. Host says MacArthur became supreme commander in the Pacific in November 1941 and elevated Colonel Willoughby to intelligence chief (host wording; MacArthur's formal command titles are not spelled out). *([ewa/willoughby-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-12-25 - The MacArthur Legacy - 50 Years of Empire Building in the Pacific - WILLOUGHBY 2.md) 51:33-54:33)*
- After 8 Dec 1941 the Japanese destroyed half his planes; forces withdrew to Bataan/Corregidor. FDR ordered him out (22 Feb 1942); on 11 March, with about 30 people in five PT boats, MacArthur left for Mindanao (then Australia), saying "I shall return". The escapees -- Willoughby, Gen. William Marquat, Gen. Richard Sutherland (later chief of staff) -- became the "Bataan Gang/Boys", "an exclusive group that resented and suspected outsiders" (Foy); Whitney arrived later. *([ewa/willoughby-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-12-25 - The MacArthur Legacy - 50 Years of Empire Building in the Pacific - WILLOUGHBY 2.md) 54:53-57:44)*
- Joseph McMicking (deputy G-2, sent ahead to Australia; only Filipino officer on the staff; son of a Manila lawyer once at the same firm as Whitney; later Ayala Corp executive; married into the "Rojas Zobel" (ASR) family) represented his preference for civilians with local ties; he and Willoughby worked to bolster Filipino resistance. *([ewa/willoughby-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-12-25 - The MacArthur Legacy - 50 Years of Empire Building in the Pacific - WILLOUGHBY 2.md) 58:02-59:32)*
- Headquarters Melbourne then Brisbane; the [[allied-intelligence-bureau]] was "MacArthur's OSS"; MacArthur repeatedly blocked [[william-donovan]]'s [[office-of-strategic-services]] from operating in the Pacific, partly to stay independent of Washington and partly out of personal approval of Willoughby, whom he called "unsurpassed in excellence" in the Philippines. The host adds that the CIA only "inserted itself" into postwar Japan in 1948-49. *([ewa/willoughby-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-12-25 - The MacArthur Legacy - 50 Years of Empire Building in the Pacific - WILLOUGHBY 2.md) 1:00:20-1:02:41)*
- 1943-44 presidential bid: Willoughby sent to Washington (summer 1943) to sound out the "old right" and found proxies (Sen. [[arthur-vandenberg]], Gen. [[robert-e-wood]]); host frames the split between "Atlanticist Yankees" (Wilson, FDR, Eisenhower) and "Asia Cowboys" who saw America's destiny in China, Japan, the Philippines and "much later Vietnam", MacArthur belonging to the latter. At the 1944 convention [[thomas-dewey]] won with 1,056 votes to MacArthur's one; he would try again in 1948 and 1952 (promised later). *([ewa/willoughby-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-12-25 - The MacArthur Legacy - 50 Years of Empire Building in the Pacific - WILLOUGHBY 2.md) 1:05:06-1:10:44)*
- October 1944: landing at Leyte and return to the Philippines; facing Gen. Yamashita ("Tiger of Malaya"). Filipino resistance (64 groups per Foy) including the [[hukbalahap]]; the [[courtney-whitney]] vs Willoughby feud over them. *([ewa/willoughby-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-12-25 - The MacArthur Legacy - 50 Years of Empire Building in the Pacific - WILLOUGHBY 2.md) 1:20:19-1:22:23)*
- Plans for Operation Downfall were shelved when Truman ([[harry-truman]]) ordered the atomic bombs on 6 and 9 August 1945; MacArthur and his cadre signed the surrender aboard the USS Missouri on 2 September. Host: "Willoughby's war was simply beginning" (postwar Japan is the next episode). *([ewa/willoughby-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-12-25 - The MacArthur Legacy - 50 Years of Empire Building in the Pacific - WILLOUGHBY 2.md) 1:23:21-1:24:11)*

### Wiki-internal notes and discrepancies
- Willoughby 1 quotes MacArthur in ebullient terms as Willoughby's "second hero"; Willoughby 2's "rubber stampers" and "sycophants" framing is the host's more critical counterpoint, not a contradiction.
- Host's timing: he says Willoughby was elevated intelligence chief when MacArthur was made supreme commander "in November" 1941 -- I record it as spoken.
