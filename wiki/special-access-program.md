A Special Access Program (SAP) is a US classification mechanism providing enhanced security for
particularly sensitive programs, layered atop standard Top Secret/SCI classification. Central
mechanism of the alleged [[ufo-legacy-programs|legacy program]] apparatus.

## Claims / terminology used

- SCI (Sensitive Compartmented Information) is "information that requires special handling
  beyond top secret clearance." *([gerb/mitre](../raw/gerb/mitre.md) 51:14–51:36)*
- A Special Security Office (SSO) manages SCI and SAP involvement; FFRDCs with sensitive
  national-program work (National Labs, Lincoln Lab, [[mitre-corporation|MITRE]]) maintain their
  own dedicated SSO and accredited SCIF (secure compartmented information facility).
  *([gerb/mitre](../raw/gerb/mitre.md) 51:06–51:54)*
- **Waived Unacknowledged SAP (WUAP/WOAP)**: the most restrictive SAP tier, with waived
  reporting requirements and unacknowledged budget — the classification Gerb alleges applies to
  both MITRE legacy activities discussed in this video. *([gerb/mitre](../raw/gerb/mitre.md) 57:53–58:16, 1:22:08–1:22:22)*
- **MNTK** (managed need-to-know) constructs and **code words** are used to further
  compartmentalize even within a SAP. *([gerb/mitre](../raw/gerb/mitre.md) 1:53:09–1:53:24)*
- **"HVSACO"** (handled via special access channels only) is described as an internal MITRE
  security control used to add sensitive handling around even unclassified information.
  *([gerb/mitre](../raw/gerb/mitre.md) 55:57–56:15, 1:53:24–1:53:33)*
- 1994 reforms under [[bill-perry]] significantly strengthened the
  [[special-access-program-oversight-committee|SAPOC]]/SRG gatekeeping structure. See that page.

## History (from the "Secrecy" Vol. 1 video)

- Grew out of Manhattan Project-era practices ([[leslie-groves|Groves]]'s tiered need-to-know
  compartmentalization) that predate any formal SAP framework; through the 1950s–1960s, agencies
  used ad hoc "top secret codeword readon access" for programs like early
  [[national-reconnaissance-office|NRO]] activity — a period Gerb calls the SAP "Wild Wild West."
  *([gerb/secrecy_1](../raw/gerb/secrecy_1.md) 2:27:33–2:28:38, 3:21:44–3:22:08)*
- Nixon's Executive Order 11652 (March 1972) formally established the SAP framework, legitimizing
  enhanced access controls beyond top-secret handling while maintaining tight congressional
  bottlenecks. A 1973 House Committee on Government Operations report subsequently documented
  "widespread and uncontrolled growth" of unauthorized special-access labels across DoD
  components, used by both classification-authority-holding agencies and agencies with no such
  authority at all. *([gerb/secrecy_1](../raw/gerb/secrecy_1.md) 2:29:53–2:31:41)*
- Reagan's Executive Order 12356 (1982, "National Security Information") — the next iteration
  after Nixon's EO 11652 and Eisenhower's EO 10501 — expanded classifiable-information categories
  and lowered the classification threshold, but crucially *tightened* (rather than loosened)
  standardization and oversight of the SAP framework, ending the prior "Wild West" era of loose
  top-secret-codeword access. SAPs were first publicly acknowledged in the early 1980s, per the
  Defense Counterintelligence and Security Agency's SAP training course. National Security
  Decision Memorandum 519 (1985) further tightened SAP controls shortly after the
  [[yellow-fruit|Yellow Fruit]] scandal broke. *([gerb/secrecy_1](../raw/gerb/secrecy_1.md)
  2:34:09–2:37:19, 3:25:22–3:25:52)*
- The 1983 exposure of the Army's [[yellow-fruit|Yellow Fruit]] USAP (via a financial audit) and
  its connection to the broader Iran-Contra scandal (1981–1986) directly preceded and, per Gerb,
  causally drove this tightening — he argues the near-exposure of Yellow Fruit's covert
  Contra-support activities to Congress and non-read-in personnel forced UFO-adjacent programs
  hiding within similar SAP umbrellas to "burrow deeper" into cover offices (e.g. the Air Force's
  [[saf-aa|SAF/AA]] "outside activities" under [[edward-c-aldridge|Edward C. Aldridge]]).
  *([gerb/secrecy_1](../raw/gerb/secrecy_1.md) 2:48:15–2:59:04)*
- Under DoD Directive 5205.07's SAP-umbrella/compartment/subcompartment matrix, Gerb theorizes
  UFO-related material could be nested as a subcompartment inside a broader-mission SAP umbrella
  (as he argues [[operation-sand-dollar|Project Sand Dollar]] was nested inside the
  [[polaris-program|Polaris]] program, per Dr. [[john-craven|John Craven]]'s account) — a
  structural template he also applies to Yellow Fruit. *([gerb/secrecy_1](../raw/gerb/secrecy_1.md) 2:48:39–2:49:11, 2:53:26–2:54:04)*
- This 1980s SAP-reform schism culminated in the 5 January 1994 restructuring under Deputy
  Secretary of Defense [[bill-perry|William Perry]], which greatly expanded the
  [[special-access-program-central-office|SAPCO]] and created/strengthened the
  [[special-access-program-oversight-committee|SAPOC]]/Senior Review Group — the event Gerb
  identifies as the final transition of legacy-program oversight into a "quasi government and
  industry control panel of just over two dozen individuals," matching the
  [[wilson-davis-notes|Wilson-Davis notes]]' account of a "watch committee" describing prior
  near-exposure by an audit. *([gerb/secrecy_1](../raw/gerb/secrecy_1.md) 2:46:53–2:47:23,
  3:07:41–3:08:36)*

## Claims (from "Special Access Required" Vol. 2 — structural deep dive)

Gerb frames SAPs (governed by Executive Order 13526 and 10 US Code 119, referencing DoD
Instructions 5205.11 and 5205.07) as the primary "vessel" legacy activities are alleged to mold
and stretch to stay funded and hidden. *([gerb/secrecy_2](../raw/gerb/secrecy_2.md) 41:09–41:33)*

- EO 13526 defines a SAP as a program "established for a specific class of classified
  information that imposes safeguarding and access requirements exceeding those normally
  required." SAPs are not a classification level above Top Secret and not a way to hide money —
  they hold the *standard* Confidential/Secret/Top Secret levels with enhanced safeguarding.
  *([gerb/secrecy_2](../raw/gerb/secrecy_2.md) 44:40–47:23)*
- **Three types of SAP** (by mission): **acquisition** (75–80% of all DoD SAPs; protects
  RDT&E/procurement; overseen by [[under-secretary-of-defense-for-acquisition-and-sustainment|
  USD A&S]]), **intelligence** (protects sensitive intel/counterintel planning and execution;
  overseen by [[under-secretary-of-defense-for-intelligence-and-security|USD I&S]]), and
  **operations and support (OSAP)** (protects planning/execution/logistics of sensitive
  operations). Gerb's illustrative example: a hidden-wing triangular airframe with
  reverse-engineered propulsion/cloaking would have its *build* protected by an acquisition SAP,
  its *fielded missions* protected by an OSAP, and the *sourcing/justification* for recovering
  and exploiting the underlying NHI technology protected by an intelligence SAP.
  *([gerb/secrecy_2](../raw/gerb/secrecy_2.md) 52:32–53:37, 1:05:05–1:06:46)*
- **Three protection levels**: **acknowledged** (existence openly recognized, funding usually
  unclassified, full congressional reporting), **unacknowledged (USAP)** (existence itself
  protected, funding classified/hidden in the federal budget, requires added indoctrination
  beyond TS/SCI; per the 1994 Joint Security Commission report "Redefining Security," USAP
  security costs run 3–10x acknowledged-SAP costs, up to 40% of total program cost), and
  **waived (WUAP/WOAP — "the apex of secrecy")** (created under extremely limited
  circumstances; per DODI 5205.05, access-approval authority rests solely with the Deputy
  Secretary of Defense, special under secretaries, or an amorphous "cognizant authority"
  component head; still nominally reportable to the Congressional Gang of Eight, a requirement
  Gerb says is routinely evaded for legacy programs). *([gerb/secrecy_2](../raw/gerb/secrecy_2.md)
  1:06:46–1:11:24)*
- SAPs use hierarchical tiers called **umbrellas**, with **compartments** and
  **subcompartments** nested beneath grouping similar Critical Program Information (CPI) —
  Gerb cites [[yellow-fruit|Yellow Fruit]] and the [[polaris-program|Polaris]]/
  [[operation-sand-dollar|Sand Dollar]] nesting (from Vol. 1) as examples. SAPs are named with an
  unclassified two-word **Program Identifier (PID)** nickname (e.g. "Yellow Fruit," or the 2010
  DHS prospective SAP "Kona Blue" meant to receive Lockheed's divested UFO materials), sometimes
  with an added classified code word, e.g. "TS SAR Hangar Bully WAIVED." Gerb states he is aware
  of UFO-related SAPs using the "NOFORN" (not releasable to foreign nationals) marking, and of
  whistleblower testimony that legacy SAPs rotate names as often as monthly to help identify
  security leaks. *([gerb/secrecy_2](../raw/gerb/secrecy_2.md) 49:50–52:32)*
- Control markers layered onto waived SAPs include **BIGOT** (need-to-know restricted to a named
  list of individuals) — per [[david-grush|David Grush]]'s Judicial Watch interview, "one of the
  most serious SAPs you can ask for" is a "bigoted, waived special access program."
  *([gerb/secrecy_2](../raw/gerb/secrecy_2.md) 1:12:41–1:13:06)*
- **The central "loophole" of the video — content-only SAPs and non-covert-action White House
  SAPs.** See [[content-only-special-access-program]] and [[covert-action-program]] for the full
  breakdown of how Gerb, citing Grush's Judicial Watch interview, argues legacy programs
  transform covert-action White House SAPs into "non-covert action" content-only SAPs to
  completely waive Title 10/Title 50 reporting and budgetary carveouts. Gerb calls this "the most
  important part of the entire video." *([gerb/secrecy_2](../raw/gerb/secrecy_2.md) 30:09–31:21,
  1:30:18–1:36:20)*
- DOE SAPs draw authority from both EO 13526 *and* the 1954 [[atomic-energy-act-1954|Atomic
  Energy Act]], letting DOE/[[national-nuclear-security-administration|NNSA]] elements hide
  programs under statutory "nuclear secret" classification (restricted data, formerly restricted
  data, special nuclear material) independent of standard executive-order declassification. Gerb
  again cites Grush on the vague Section 51 "sizable amount of atomic energy" definition of
  special nuclear material being used to "transclassify" UFO material into a nuclear secret. He
  states witness "Ed" of the [[hidden-wing-air-force-program|Hidden Wing]] confirmed DOE
  involvement "from day-to-day operations ... all the way up to Air Force acquisition," disputing
  [[luis-elizondo|Lou Elizondo]]'s *Age of Disclosure* org chart placing DOE as an equal-footing
  player rather than baked into every layer. *([gerb/secrecy_2](../raw/gerb/secrecy_2.md)
  1:36:44–1:40:25)*
- Day-to-day SAP material handling occurs in accredited SAP Facilities (SAPFs), temporary SAPFs
  (TSAPFs, 12-month limit), SAP Compartmented Areas (SAPCAs), SAP Working Areas (SAPWAs), and SAP
  Temporary Security Working Areas (SAPTSWAs, 40 hrs/month limit) — all meeting or exceeding SCIF
  standards, often placed on contractor sites. Roles include the Government Program Manager
  (GPM), Contractor Program Manager (CPM), Program Security Officer (PSO), Government SAP
  Security Officer (GSSO), and Contractor Program Security Officer (CPSO).
  *([gerb/secrecy_2](../raw/gerb/secrecy_2.md) 1:40:49–1:43:15)*
- Gerb's closing legal argument: the SAP system itself is not illegal — it rests on legitimate
  (if "massaged") statutory/executive authority dating to 1940s–1950s classified presidential
  executive orders — but he argues the *activities* legacy programs conduct through this
  structure (misappropriation of funds, possible violence against insiders, "white and blue
  collar crimes") are illegal. He explicitly rejects [[steven-greer|Steven Greer]]'s claim that
  the underlying NDAs are unenforceable/void. *([gerb/secrecy_2](../raw/gerb/secrecy_2.md)
  3:15:00–3:16:47)*
