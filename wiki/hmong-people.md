The Hmong are the northern-highland opium-growing people of [[laos]] whom the French colonial administration encountered as "their newest clients in the opium business". The ASR consistently renders them as "Hong" (my normalization to Hmong, inferred from context and from the existing [[golden-triangle]] and [[vang-pao]] pages, which use Hmong). *([ewa/laos-1](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-01 - The Secret War in Laos - South East Asia's Heart of Darkness - LAOS 1.md) 14:44-14:54)*

## Claims

- Relatively new to Laos, expelled from homelands in southern China in the first half of the 19th century. *([ewa/laos-1](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-01 - The Secret War in Laos - South East Asia's Heart of Darkness - LAOS 1.md) 14:54-14:59)*
- Lived for centuries in southern China growing the opium poppy as a cash crop under the Chinese tributary system; in 1644 the incoming Manchu dynasty ended the tributary policy and began integrating the Hmong petty kingdoms into the imperial bureaucracy, with nearly two centuries of violent policies to subdue their kings and control the lucrative crop. *([ewa/laos-1](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-01 - The Secret War in Laos - South East Asia's Heart of Darkness - LAOS 1.md) 15:02-15:39)*
- Droves fled to Laos, and by the early 1900s "essentially all" had immigrated and re-established themselves as small independent opium tribes whose main customer became the French. *([ewa/laos-1](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-01 - The Secret War in Laos - South East Asia's Heart of Darkness - LAOS 1.md) 15:40-16:01)*
- Enjoyed 40 years (1893 to WWII) of "continued cooperation" with French authority while the opium trade grew gradually. *([ewa/laos-1](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-01 - The Secret War in Laos - South East Asia's Heart of Darkness - LAOS 1.md) 16:04-16:20)*
- Highland farmers generally saw opium as economic reality, not an addictive narcotic. *([ewa/laos-1](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-01 - The Secret War in Laos - South East Asia's Heart of Darkness - LAOS 1.md) 11:05-11:21)*

## Relationships
