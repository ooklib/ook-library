Wesley R. Fishell was, per [[eyes-wide-open]], a Michigan State University academic (later political-science professor) who met [[ngo-dinh-diem]] in Tokyo in 1950 and became his lifelong friend, American sponsor, and unofficial top adviser, ending as head of the [[michigan-state-university-vietnam-advisory-group]]. The host argues Fishell's role makes Diem the chosen man of US intelligence specifically, not just of Washington generally. ASR renders the name "fishell"/"fal"/"FAL"/"F's" (normalization is my inference). *([ewa/laos-3](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-15 - The French Connection in South East Asia - LAOS 3.md) 9:34-9:45)*

## Claims

- Introduced to Diem by a mutual friend in Tokyo in 1950; "instantly swayed" by Diem's blended anti-communist, anti-colonial ideology, he escorted Diem around the US on his 1951-54 tour to meet power brokers, allegedly boasting of Diem's large support network in Vietnam which the host says did not exist. *([ewa/laos-3](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-15 - The French Connection in South East Asia - LAOS 3.md) 9:26-10:43)*
- Before Diem's 1953 return he secured him a symbolic post at Michigan State's government research bureau as a Southeast Asia consultant. The host notes the college had grown close to the US defense apparatus, and that Fishell received a United Nations medal for top-secret Korean War work on "language deficiencies and cultural gaps" ("whatever the hell that means"). *([ewa/laos-3](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-15 - The French Connection in South East Asia - LAOS 3.md) 12:47-13:32)*
- Hired by Diem as a top adviser from 1954 and, per colleagues, had Diem's ear and trust in a way [[edward-lansdale]] and [[lucien-conein]] never did; unofficial adviser to the Ngo brothers until 1962. He led the [[michigan-state-university-vietnam-advisory-group]]. *([ewa/laos-3](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-15 - The French Connection in South East Asia - LAOS 3.md) 53:37-54:14)*
- He and MSU President John Hannah first said the police-training program was ordered by then Vice President [[richard-nixon]], later that it came from an authority "even higher"; per the 1966 Ramparts article that leaves [[allen-dulles]], [[john-foster-dulles]] or [[dwight-d-eisenhower]]. The host finds the Allen Dulles suggestion compelling. *([ewa/laos-3](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-15 - The French Connection in South East Asia - LAOS 3.md) 56:34-57:05)*
- He denied the CIA-front allegations for life, but his New York Times obituary quotes him as saying members of his police-training contingent were "CIA agents on leave", which he did not see as a problem. The host recalls Fishell had been a Navy and Army intelligence officer in WWII and adds his own mantra that "one never stops working for the CIA". *([ewa/laos-3](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-15 - The French Connection in South East Asia - LAOS 3.md) 58:14-58:58)*
- Discrepancy note: the host says Diem returned to Vietnam in 1953 at one point and 1954 at others (Fishell secures the MSU post "before DM returned to Vietnam in 1953"; the courtship continues "after his return to Vietnam in 1954"). *([ewa/laos-3](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-15 - The French Connection in South East Asia - LAOS 3.md) 12:47-12:54, 13:52-13:57)*
