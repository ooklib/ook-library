Two cases around the turn of the 20th century documented by the US Hydrographic Bureau, which [[uap-gerb]] picks after passing over the alleged Christopher Columbus "wax candle" sighting for lack of evidence. *([gerb/uso-casebook](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-04-26 - USO Case Book_ Unidentified Submerged Objects Throughout History.md>) 15:05-15:34)*

## Claims

- 12 November 1887, SS Siberian (English steamer, Captain R. F. Moore), 10 nautical miles off Cape Race, Newfoundland: an enormous fireball rose from the sea to about 50 ft, moved toward the ship, then sped into the sky. The Hydrographic Bureau called it one of the rarest and most difficult to explain electrical phenomena; Gerb has not seen ball lightning exit the sea. *([gerb/uso-casebook](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-04-26 - USO Case Book_ Unidentified Submerged Objects Throughout History.md>) 15:34-16:07)*
- 1902, Gulf of Guinea, the "Fort Salisbury" (as transcribed): four men saw an immense cigar-shaped object (nearly 200 m long by 300 m wide, as transcribed) sink into the ocean and pass under the ship, with two orange lights at the front and two blue-green at the other end. Gerb thinks it could be a submarine, but early German U-boats were only about 41 m long, so he still finds it anomalous. *([gerb/uso-casebook](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-04-26 - USO Case Book_ Unidentified Submerged Objects Throughout History.md>) 16:07-16:42)*
