The Round Table movement (also called the "Milner group" by historian Carroll Quigley) was an
informal network of Oxford-educated British imperial administrators built around [[alfred-milner]]
during and after his tenure as High Commissioner for Southern Africa. Green & Pleasant presents it
as the direct institutional precursor to [[chatham-house]], via [[lionel-curtis]], one of its
members ("Milner's Kindergarten"), who convened Chatham House's 1919 founding meeting.

## Claims

- After Britain's victory in the Second Boer War (which Milner's own policies are said to have
  directly precipitated), Milner assembled a group of young Oxford-educated men — including
  Lionel Curtis, Philip Kerr (later Lord Lothian), and Geoffrey Dawson (later editor of the
  Times) — to administer the conquered Transvaal territories. They were known as "Milner's
  Kindergarten" *([gp/chatham-house](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-04-10 - Britain's Most Powerful Secret Organisation - Chatham House Exposed.md) 13:57, 14:10, 14:19, 14:26, 14:34)*.
- Historian Keith Breckenridge documented that the administrative systems built by Milner's
  Kindergarten in the Transvaal created a system designed to control the identities, movements
  and behavior of Black workers. The 1905 Lagden Commission, established under Milner's
  administration, formalized racial segregation and is described as "a blueprint for apartheid"
  *([gp/chatham-house](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-04-10 - Britain's Most Powerful Secret Organisation - Chatham House Exposed.md) 14:59, 15:08, 15:13)*.
- Georgetown historian Carroll Quigley, who studied the network for 20 years with access to its
  private papers, is quoted as writing: "The Royal Institute of International Affairs is nothing
  but the Milner group writ large" — i.e. Chatham House is presented as this network's
  continuation into a formal institution
  *([gp/chatham-house](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-04-10 - Britain's Most Powerful Secret Organisation - Chatham House Exposed.md) 16:39)*.
- See [[chatham-house]] for the 1919 Paris meeting where Lionel Curtis, a Kindergarten member,
  converted this informal network into a permanent institution split into a British body
  (Chatham House) and an American one (the [[council-on-foreign-relations]]).

## Relationships

- Assembled by [[alfred-milner]] out of his administration of the conquered Transvaal after the
  Second Boer War.
- Included [[lionel-curtis]], who went on to found [[chatham-house]].
- Thematically comparable to the [[fabian-society]] as another elite network from the same broad
  era pursuing influence through inside institutions rather than mass politics — but no direct
  organizational or personnel link between the two is stated in the source; the connection
  between the two wiki sub-graphs runs instead through [[dennis-healey]]'s later, separate
  membership in both [[chatham-house]] and the Fabian Society.

## Speculation flag

"Round Table movement" and "Milner group" are historians' and Green & Pleasant's own labels for
an informal network, not a single formal organization with membership rolls; treat all claims
above — especially the direct line drawn from Milner's Kindergarten to Chatham House's founding —
as Green & Pleasant's presentation of Quigley's and other historians' research, not this wiki's
independent verification.
