The Aerospace Corporation is a systems-and-integration-center FFRDC, spun off from TRW in 1960
(per Gerb, "in the same short span of years" as MITRE's founding relative to Roswell) due to
concerns over TRW's close Air Force relationship. *([mitre](../raw/107BtDYhTkA_mitre.md) 21:59–22:14, 1:03:23–1:03:46)*

## Claims

- Gerb considers it an FFRDC "I believe is inferior, but in the same ballpark as MITRE in
  regards to UFO activities." *([mitre](../raw/107BtDYhTkA_mitre.md) 1:03:23–1:03:39)*
- [[stephanie-osullivan]] joined its board of directors after her IC career, and
  [[william-laplante]] serves on its corporate board today. *([mitre](../raw/107BtDYhTkA_mitre.md) 1:40:44–1:40:51, 1:47:53–1:48:04)*

- Spun off in 1960 "from half of TRW's Space Technology Laboratories," due to other contractors
  fearing TRW's Air Force advisory role gave it unfair access to cutting-edge technologies. Gerb
  compares the split to [[science-applications-international-corporation|SAIC]] spinning off
  Leidos in 2013: nominally different business focuses but shared personnel, corporate practices,
  SAP access, and (per Gerb) UFO-legacy-program involvement. Today it is a nonprofit FFRDC
  sponsored by the DoD and Air Force, headquartered in El Segundo, California — also home to an
  AFOSI/PJ permanent station. Gerb believes the Aerospace Corporation "led legacy program efforts
  in a parallel track to TRW and then Northrop Grumman." *([northrop-trw](../raw/
  0kLSp0lH-JQ_northrop_trw.md) 50:40–52:36)*
- On the SecoActivo podcast, Rep. [[eric-burlison|Eric Burlison]] stated legal interrogatories
  (drafted with [[david-grush|David Grush]]'s help) went to the CIA, the Navy, and the Aerospace
  Corporation; the CIA declined to answer and redirected Burlison/Grush to AARO, while the
  Aerospace Corporation was "the only other entity to respond," sending corporate members to a
  secure in-person DC briefing where they claimed to know nothing about UFO legacy programs.
  Gerb calls the response "absolutely pitiful" and states he is "in full solidarity with Grush
  that the Aerospace Corporation is absolutely involved in legacy programs."
  *([northrop-trw](../raw/0kLSp0lH-JQ_northrop_trw.md) 52:37–56:01)*
- [[randall-g-walden|Randall G. Walden]] (former director/PEO of the Air Force Rapid Capabilities
  Office) was elected to the Aerospace Corporation's Board of Trustees on 7 June 2023.
  [[edward-c-aldridge|Edward C. Aldridge]] (former Under Secretary/Secretary of the Air Force and
  NRO director) served as the Aerospace Corporation's president and CEO for nearly 10 years. Gerb
  frames both men's moves from senior Air Force legacy-program roles to Aerospace Corporation
  executive positions as evidence of the "stars and stripes for a pinstriped suit" pattern he sees
  across the wiki's gatekeeper figures. *([northrop-trw](../raw/0kLSp0lH-JQ_northrop_trw.md)
  57:59–58:34, 1:10:39–1:10:54)*
