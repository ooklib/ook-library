The Atomic Energy Act of 1954 expanded the 1946 Atomic Energy Act's concept of "restricted data"
and created a suite of classification tools — including "special nuclear material," "foreign
intelligence information," and "transclassified foreign nuclear information (TFNI)" — that UAP
Gerb argues form a statutory "track two" of US classification, wholly separate from and immune to
executive-order-based classification (confidential/secret/top secret and the SCI/SAP layers). He
treats this statute as the mechanism by which he alleges UFO materials and information have been
"transclassified" as nuclear secrets to keep them under Department of Energy custody, outside
ordinary oversight. *([gerb/secrecy_1](../raw/gerb/secrecy_1.md) 1:09:56–1:10:43)*

## Claims

- Track one (executive-order-based): confidential, secret, and top secret classification, plus
  the SCI/[[special-access-program|SAP]] compartment layers, currently under Obama's 2009
  Executive Order 13526 (itself an iteration of Reagan's 1982 Executive Order 12356). Track two
  (statutory): the Atomic Energy Act system of "restricted data," overseen by the Department of
  Energy. The two tracks have entirely different legal bases, declassification procedures, and
  oversight mechanisms — meaning, per UAP Gerb, that a presidential executive order to release
  "UFO files" would not touch DOE restricted data covered by this statutory authority.
  *([gerb/secrecy_1](../raw/gerb/secrecy_1.md) 1:10:04–1:11:02, 3:22:23–3:23:30)*
- Section 51's definition of "special nuclear material" is, per UAP Gerb, "ultra vague": "anything
  that gives off a sizable amount of atomic energy." He argues this vagueness allows UFO materials
  to be "transclassified" into nuclear secrets via legal interpretation. He attributes similar
  characterizations to [[david-grush|David Grush]] and to the language of Senator Schumer's UAP
  Disclosure Act (UAPDA) amendment, which explicitly cites the 1954 Act.
  *([gerb/secrecy_1](../raw/gerb/secrecy_1.md) 1:11:47–1:12:20, 1:14:41–1:14:59)*
- Section 142 gave statutory authority for "transclassified foreign nuclear information" (TFNI),
  formally named as such in 2010 under 32 CFR part 2001. TFNI is a type of classified information
  (not a classification level like restricted data): information can be transclassified out of
  the restricted-data category, then handled like ordinary national security information while
  still retaining Atomic Energy Act protections such as exemption from mandatory declassification
  review — effectively enjoying both classification frameworks at once.
  *([gerb/secrecy_1](../raw/gerb/secrecy_1.md) 1:13:40–1:14:33)*
- The 1946 Atomic Energy Act (predecessor statute) created "restricted data" as the only US
  classification that applies automatically to information by subject matter rather than by
  formal classification decision, codifying [[leslie-groves|Leslie Groves]]'s informal Manhattan
  Project practice of information being "born secret." It also created the AEC's own personnel
  clearance system — Q clearance (equivalent to Top Secret/Restricted Data) and L clearance
  (equivalent to Confidential/Restricted Data) — separate from DoD/Executive Branch clearance
  frameworks. *([gerb/secrecy_1](../raw/gerb/secrecy_1.md) 1:08:44–1:09:16,
  3:22:32–3:22:56)*
- UAP Gerb argues this statutory framework explains why the [[department-of-energy|DOE]] is
  "quite literally baked into every single layer" of the alleged legacy-program structure, and
  underpins DOE/NNSA special-teams authority (SRT, [[office-of-secure-transportation|OST]],
  [[doe-nuclear-emergency-support-team|NEST]]) for continental-US crash-retrieval logistics.
  *([gerb/secrecy_1](../raw/gerb/secrecy_1.md) 1:11:02–1:11:34, 1:22:53–1:23:21)*
