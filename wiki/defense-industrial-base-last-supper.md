July 1993 meeting at which Defense Secretary Les Aspin and Deputy Secretary [[bill-perry]] told prime contractors only a few in each sector would survive post-Cold-War cuts. Major US defense primes shrank from 51 to five ([[lockheed-martin]], [[raytheon]], [[northrop-grumman]], General Dynamics, [[boeing]]) between 1993 and 1997. *([gerb/timeline](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2026-09-18 - 80 Years of UFO Crash Retrieval and Reverse Engineering - A Timeline.md>) 1:45:00-1:45:44)*

## Claims ([[uap-gerb]])

- Several contractors with legacy-program involvement were absorbed: Lockheed + [[martin-marietta]] (1995), Boeing acquiring Rockwell and [[mcdonnell-douglas]] (1996-97), Northrop acquiring Ryan Aeronautical, Raytheon purchasing [[e-systems]] (former LTV Aerospace, "a CIA cutout shell company"). *([gerb/timeline](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2026-09-18 - 80 Years of UFO Crash Retrieval and Reverse Engineering - A Timeline.md>) 1:45:44-1:46:08)*
- Gerb ranks the December 2002 Northrop-[[trw]] deal the most crucial acquisition of the era. *([gerb/timeline](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2026-09-18 - 80 Years of UFO Crash Retrieval and Reverse Engineering - A Timeline.md>) 2:01:03-2:01:19)*
