William Beveridge (1879–1963) authored the 1942 Beveridge Report, the blueprint for the British
welfare state, the NHS, and Social Security. Green & Pleasant presents him as trained by the
[[fabian-society]] (as a researcher under [[beatrice-webb]]), installed by them as director of
the [[london-school-of-economics]], and simultaneously a long-serving council member of the
[[eugenics-society]] who told eugenicists his welfare blueprint served their aims.

## Claims

- Worked as a researcher on Beatrice Webb's 1909 Minority Report to the Royal Commission on the
  Poor Laws; the Fabians made him LSE director in 1919
  *([gp/secret-society](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-03-28 - The Secret Society Running Britain for 140 Years.md) 19:56, 20:04, 20:13)*.
- Authored the 1942 Beveridge Report, the blueprint for the NHS and postwar welfare state.
- Sat on the Eugenics Society's consultative council until the late 1950s (he died in 1963).
- On 16 February 1943, the same afternoon the House of Commons debated the Beveridge Report, he
  left the parliamentary gallery to deliver the Galton Lecture to the Eugenics Society, titled
  "Eugenic Aspects of Children's Allowances," telling the audience his welfare blueprint was
  "eugenic in intent and would prove so in effect." The children's-allowance scheme is alleged to
  have been developed within the Eugenics Society with the twin aims of encouraging more children
  among "the educated professional classes" while limiting births among poor households
  *([gp/secret-society](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-03-28 - The Secret Society Running Britain for 140 Years.md) 18:29, 18:58, 19:08, 19:15, 19:32)*.
