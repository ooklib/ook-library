George Orwell (born Eric Blair, 1903–1950) is presented by Green & Pleasant as the man who "tried
to warn" about the [[fabian-society]] from the inside of British socialism — a democratic
socialist who fought in Spain, was never a Fabian, and broke publicly with the society after it
rewrote his own words without consent.

## Claims

- As a pupil of [[aldous-huxley]] (his French teacher at Eton, c. 1917), had an early indirect
  connection to the eugenics-adjacent circles around the Fabian Society
  *([gp/secret-society](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-03-28 - The Secret Society Running Britain for 140 Years.md) 4:06, 4:21)*.
- A democratic socialist who fought in the Spanish Civil War; "never a conservative, never a
  Fabian, and never fooled by any of this," per the video
  *([gp/secret-society](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-03-28 - The Secret Society Running Britain for 140 Years.md) 20:18)*.
- In *The Road to Wigan Pier* (1937), wrote that "the Cranks, Webbs, Stracheys, etc., etc., are
  not exactly proletarian writers," arguing middle-class socialists "cling like glue to their
  miserable fragments of social prestige," and warned against technocratic socialism "planned by
  experts, for the people, without the people"
  *([gp/secret-society](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-03-28 - The Secret Society Running Britain for 140 Years.md) 20:27, 20:48, 20:56)*.
- Wrote of the Holodomor that events "involving the deaths of millions of people have actually
  escaped the attention of the majority of English Russophiles" — an implicit rebuke of Fabian
  figures like Shaw and the Webbs
  *([gp/secret-society](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-03-28 - The Secret Society Running Britain for 140 Years.md) 23:08)*.
- Gave a single lecture to the Fabian Society, "Culture and Democracy" (1941), submitted as a
  manuscript. When published, the Fabians had rewritten it without permission or notice; Orwell's
  July 1942 letter of complaint said they had "gone all through my manuscript and altered every
  phrase which revealed that I was delivering a spoken lecture," giving "a quite false impression
  of my written style." In 1949 he instructed his literary executors that the lecture had been
  "substantially altered and deformed all the way through without my knowledge or consent" and
  ordered it never be reprinted
  *([gp/secret-society](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-03-28 - The Secret Society Running Britain for 140 Years.md) 26:17, 26:44, 26:52, 26:59)*.
- Went on to write *1984*. Green & Pleasant explicitly debunks two related conspiracy theories:
  that the title referenced a "Fabian centenary" (the manuscript shows the date was changed three
  times and the working title was *The Last Man in Europe*, so there's no evidence for this), and
  that the Fabians control Orwell's literary estate (it passed to his wife Sonia and then to his
  son Richard Blair, unrelated to Tony Blair)
  *([gp/secret-society](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-03-28 - The Secret Society Running Britain for 140 Years.md) 27:16, 27:33, 27:41, 27:50, 27:56)*.

## Relationships

- Taught French by [[aldous-huxley]] at Eton.
- Critiqued [[sidney-webb]], [[beatrice-webb]], and other Fabian intellectuals in print.
- Broke with the [[fabian-society]] over its 1941–42 alteration of his lecture.

## Speculation flag

Orwell's critique of the Fabians and the details of the lecture dispute are presented by Green &
Pleasant as documented from his own letters; the video itself explicitly flags and rejects two
adjacent conspiracy theories (the *1984*/Fabian-centenary title claim and Fabian control of his
estate) as unsupported — this page follows the source in debunking those specific claims while
still attributing the underlying documented dispute to the source.
