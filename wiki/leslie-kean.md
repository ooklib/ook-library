Leslie Kean is the journalist, co-author of the 2017 New York Times article that brought the Pentagon UAP videos and the ASAP (AATIP) story to the public, whom [[uap-gerb]] cites for her Kecksburg work. (ASR: "Keane", "Keen".) *([gerb/kecksburg](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-09-12 - The 1965 Kecksburg, Pennsylvania UFO Crash.md>) 30:38-30:55)*

## Claims

- Around 2000 Kean corresponded with Nicholas Johnson, NASA chief scientist for orbital debris at the Johnson Space Center, who per Gerb determined that Cosmos 96 could not have landed at Kecksburg near 5 p.m. on 9 December 1965, that no man-made space object came down over Pennsylvania that day, and that the Corona canister recoveries were also excluded. *([gerb/kecksburg](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-09-12 - The 1965 Kecksburg, Pennsylvania UFO Crash.md>) 30:55-32:24)*
- Working with the Coalition for Freedom of Information she sued NASA over stonewalled Kecksburg files. A 2007 settlement required NASA to produce hundreds of never-before-seen documents and pay legal fees; NASA completed its search by August 2009 and posted 20 pages of Moon Dust documents with no relation to Kecksburg. A NASA public-affairs document says NASA sent its files to the National Archives two years after the incident and in 1996 the Archives said they had been marked lost since 1987. *([gerb/kecksburg](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-09-12 - The 1965 Kecksburg, Pennsylvania UFO Crash.md>) 32:24-33:12)*
- Gerb calls the loss of the files a "very strange convenience" and connects the topic to [[project-moon-dust]]. *([gerb/kecksburg](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-09-12 - The 1965 Kecksburg, Pennsylvania UFO Crash.md>) 33:12-33:34)*
