MIT Lincoln Laboratory is a DoD-sponsored FFRDC, reopened in 1951 as successor to
[[vannevar-bush]]'s MIT Radiation Lab (established 1940), which had developed WWII microwave
radar technologies for navigation and combat until 1945. *([gerb/mitre](../raw/gerb/mitre.md) 20:42–21:07)*

## Claims

- Sponsored by the DoD and the Under Secretary of Defense for Research and Engineering.
  *([gerb/mitre](../raw/gerb/mitre.md) 20:59–21:07)*
- Initially undertook Project Lincoln, which became [[sage-semi-automatic-ground-environment|
  SAGE]], the US's first computer-based national air defense system, first conceived 1951,
  deployed 1963. *([gerb/mitre](../raw/gerb/mitre.md) 30:44–31:06, 31:34–31:41)*
- In 1958, the Air Force and MIT Lincoln Labs spun off a new group — [[mitre-corporation]] —
  from Lincoln Lab's research and engineering group personnel, to spearhead SAGE's systems
  engineering. *([gerb/mitre](../raw/gerb/mitre.md) 31:41–32:15, 2:02:44–2:03:00)*
- First technical director of MITRE, [[robert-r-everett]], came to MITRE from sensitive MIT
  Navy programs such as [[project-whirlwind]], underscoring a Navy technical lineage predating
  MITRE's Air Force administrative founding. *([gerb/mitre](../raw/gerb/mitre.md) 35:47–36:10)*
- Gerb notes that as of this video's posting, a promised "Captain Edward J. Rupelt film" that
  MIT Lincoln Labs was reportedly to deliver to Rep. [[eric-burlison]]'s office had still not
  been produced. *([gerb/mitre](../raw/gerb/mitre.md) 2:00:14–2:00:32)*
- Gerb recommends MITRE's congressional response detail its "ongoing legacy relationship" with
  MIT Lincoln Labs regarding shared facilities. *([gerb/mitre](../raw/gerb/mitre.md) 1:55:41–1:55:51)*

## Relationships

- Successor institution to [[vannevar-bush]]'s MIT Radiation Lab.
- Parent organization from which [[mitre-corporation]] was spun off in 1958.
- Operated the [[sage-semi-automatic-ground-environment|SAGE]] project (as "Project Lincoln").
