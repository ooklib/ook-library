The Atomic Energy Commission (AEC), created by the 1946 Atomic Energy Act, was the civilian
custodian of the US nuclear weapons enterprise from the end of the Manhattan Project until its
abolition in 1974 (its functions split between the Energy Research and Development
Administration, which became the [[department-of-energy|Department of Energy]] in 1977, and the
Nuclear Regulatory Commission). Gerb treats the AEC — and specifically its 1954 Atomic Energy Act
— as the statutory foundation for the entire alleged DOE/[[national-nuclear-security-
administration|NNSA]] UFO legacy-program gatekeeping structure. *([gerb/sandia](../raw/gerb/sandia.md) 1:25:36–1:26:26)*

## Claims

- On 1 January 1947, [[los-alamos-national-laboratory|Los Alamos]]'s Z Division — the future
  [[sandia-national-laboratories|Sandia]] — and its lands, facilities, and equipment were
  transferred from Army control to AEC control, a moment Gerb calls "likely integral to
  establishing UFO legacy program gatekeeping structures." *([gerb/sandia](../raw/gerb/sandia.md)
  17:36–18:02)*
- Per former New Mexico State Rep. [[j-andrew-kissner|J. Andrew Kissner]]'s 1994 statement, custody of
  discs downed by the US Army from 15 May–31 July 1947 (including Roswell) was vested by a 1948
  classified presidential executive order in civilian authorities: the AEC's contractor-operated
  labs (Los Alamos and the soon-to-be-separate Sandia), assisted by Air Force Systems Command,
  the Naval Research Laboratory, and Vannevar Bush's Research and Development Board.
  *([gerb/sandia](../raw/gerb/sandia.md) 27:39–29:25)*
- The Eisenhower Briefing Document (the first alleged [[majestic-12|Majestic 12]] leak, dated 18
  November 1952) states recovered materials from a 6 December 1950 Del Rio/Eldorado, Texas crash
  retrieval were transported to "the atomic energy facility at Sandia" for study — which Gerb
  reads as corroborating Kner's account of AEC-lab custody. *([gerb/sandia](../raw/gerb/sandia.md) 36:50–37:29)*
- The 1954 Atomic Energy Act's ultra-broad definition of "special nuclear material" (section 51:
  "anything that gives off a sizable amount of atomic energy") is, per Gerb, the mechanism by
  which UFO materials and information were allegedly "transclassified" as nuclear secrets —
  alongside "foreign intelligence information" (FII) and "non-contract" caveats restricting
  release to contractors/consultants — keeping them under AEC/DOE custody and out of ordinary
  classification/oversight channels. *([gerb/sandia](../raw/gerb/sandia.md) 20:16–20:30,
  1:22:36–1:24:54)*
- Established the Nevada Proving Ground (later [[nevada-national-security-site|Nevada Test
  Site]]) in 1951 under President Truman, out of the Nellis AFB gunnery and bombing range; 928
  nuclear tests were conducted there 1951–1994. *([gerb/sandia](../raw/gerb/sandia.md)
  1:45:49–1:46:13)*
- February 1952: sponsored [[project-twinkle|Project Twinkle]], a study of "green fireball" UFOs
  at Los Alamos, White Sands, Holloman AFB, and Sandia Base — one month before Project Blue
  Book's creation; the Air Force reportedly advised against declassifying it because the
  phenomena could not be debunked as natural. *([gerb/sandia](../raw/gerb/sandia.md)
  41:58–42:28)*
- Gerb believes the 1954 Atomic Energy Act specifically (not just the AEC's general existence)
  drove a 1955-era shift of custody of non-human materials into national-lab/FFRDC hands as "a
  perfect way ... to start keeping these recovered materials out of traditional oversight
  channels" — connecting this to Alfred O'Donnell's account (via [[george-knapp|George Knapp]])
  of a recovered disc moved from Indian Springs AFB to an Area 51-adjacent facility in 1955.
  *([gerb/sandia](../raw/gerb/sandia.md) 2:09:24–2:11:24)*

## Claims (from the "Secrecy" Vol. 1 video)

- Founded in 1946 as the Manhattan Project's immediate civilian successor, the AEC developed its
  own personnel security clearance system separate from DoD/Executive Branch frameworks: Q
  clearance (equivalent to Top Secret/Restricted Data) and L clearance (equivalent to
  Confidential/Restricted Data). *([gerb/secrecy_1](../raw/gerb/secrecy_1.md) 1:08:35–1:08:52)*
- The 1946 Atomic Energy Act, passed alongside the AEC's founding, created "restricted data" — the
  only US classification that applies automatically to information by subject matter rather than
  by formal classification decision — codifying [[leslie-groves|Leslie Groves]]'s informal
  Manhattan Project practice of information being "born secret." See
  [[atomic-energy-act-1954]] for the 1954 successor statute's further expansion of this authority.
  *([gerb/secrecy_1](../raw/gerb/secrecy_1.md) 1:08:58–1:09:45)*
- UAP Gerb frames the AEC/DOE's statutory classification system as a "track two," running
  entirely parallel to and independent of the executive-order-based "track one" classification
  system (confidential/secret/top secret plus [[special-access-program|SAP]] compartments) — with
  different legal bases, declassification procedures, and oversight, meaning an executive order to
  release "UFO files" would not touch AEC/DOE-held restricted data. *([gerb/secrecy_1](../raw/gerb/secrecy_1.md) 1:10:04–1:11:02)*
