Dr. Eric Henry Wang is, per Gerb, one of the most enigmatic figures associated with early-1950s
Air Force UFO crash retrievals, named in connection with both the
[[kingman-arizona-crash-1953|1953 Kingman, Arizona]] and [[aztec-new-mexico-crash-1948|1948
Aztec, New Mexico]] crash-retrieval cases. Gerb, after years without finding any photograph or
substantive record, located Wang's 1956 [[kirtland-air-force-base|Kirtland AFB]] yearbook photo
and an April 1961 *Mechanical Engineering* magazine obituary that finally fleshes out his career.
*([gerb/hidden_wing](../raw/gerb/hidden_wing.md) 3:07:02–3:15:22)*

## Claims

- Primary Kingman witness Arthur Stansel Jr. (disclosing under the pseudonym "Fritz Warner")
  named Wang as leading a reverse-engineering team analyzing wreckage/ballistics from the Kingman
  crash. Stansel, a WWII Army veteran and engineer who had worked on the Manhattan Project, was
  independently confirmed to have worked directly under Wang during 1953's Operation Upshot-Knothole
  nuclear tests, where Wang served as technical/scientific monitor for the Air Force program and
  chief of the Special Studies Office out of [[wright-patterson-air-force-base|Wright-Patterson
  AFB]], under the Air Research and Development Command (precursor to
  [[air-force-special-weapons-center|AFSWC]]/[[air-force-material-command|AFMC]]).
  *([gerb/hidden_wing](../raw/gerb/hidden_wing.md) 3:07:41–3:10:19)*
- UFO researcher William Steinman, investigating the 1948 Aztec crash for his 1986 book *UFO
  Crash at Aztec: A Well-Kept Secret*, separately encountered Wang's name, quoting a source: "When
  one mentions the name of Dr. Eric Henry Wang to anyone who knew about his work, especially the
  intelligence department personnel, no matter what their rank and responsibility, they shudder
  and ask you to leave them alone. ... The work that Dr. Wang was involved in was so highly
  classified that there were no more levels of secrecy left above that. He was involved and in
  charge of some of the most secret segments pertaining to the ultimate secrets, the flying saucer
  program." *([gerb/hidden_wing](../raw/gerb/hidden_wing.md) 3:11:07–3:11:51)*
- Steinman's obituary find: Wang was born in Austria on 6 May 1906, earned a teaching degree in
  Cincinnati, Ohio, and served 1949–1956 as head of the Department of Special Studies at Wright
  Air Development Center (the research directorate of the [[air-force-special-weapons-center|Air
  Force Special Weapons Center]]). In 1956 his department moved to the Sandia Laboratory Complex
  at [[kirtland-air-force-base|Kirtland AFB]], where he remained until his death in 1960 at age
  54. *([gerb/hidden_wing](../raw/gerb/hidden_wing.md) 3:11:58–3:14:38)*
- Steinman contacted an Albuquerque source known only as "MW" who knew Wang well and stated
  Wang's personal papers were confiscated by military intelligence upon his death and locked in a
  secret section of the Kirtland AFB library. MW is revealed (via the obituary) to have been Maria
  Wang, Eric Wang's wife, who told Steinman Wang's boss and head of the "flying saucer program"
  while Wang was active was "quite possibly Henry Kissinger" (referred to by initials "H.A.K.").
  Steinman's mail was subsequently tampered with and a tracking device was found on his van; MW
  never responded to further correspondence. *([gerb/hidden_wing](../raw/gerb/hidden_wing.md)
  3:12:42–3:14:12)*
- Wang literally served within the highest echelons of the AFSWC that Gerb argues forms the direct
  organizational skeleton of the modern [[air-force-material-command|Air Force Material
  Command]] (via both [[air-force-research-laboratory|AFRL]] and Air Force Systems Command).
  *([gerb/hidden_wing](../raw/gerb/hidden_wing.md) 3:14:38–3:15:22)*
