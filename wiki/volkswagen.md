Volkswagen is a German automaker. This page also holds the video's claims on Porsche and Audi (Auto Union), which are entangled with its origins.

## Claims

- Established in 1937 by the German Labor Front under the Nazi 'Strength Through Joy' program to produce an affordable people's car, designed by Ferdinand Porsche. *([brof_stein/nazi-brands](<../raw/brof_stein/Brofessor Stein - 2025-07-23 - Every Major Brand with Nazi Ties Explained By a German.md>) 5:47-6:01)*
- In wartime it made the Kubelwagen, the amphibious Schwimmwagen and the V1 flying bomb; at the Fallersleben plant it used 15,000 forced laborers, many concentration-camp inmates supplied by the SS, with historians estimating about 80% of the wartime workforce was slave labor; formally acknowledged in 1998, with a $12 million reparations fund. *([brof_stein/nazi-brands](<../raw/brof_stein/Brofessor Stein - 2025-07-23 - Every Major Brand with Nazi Ties Explained By a German.md>) 6:11-7:00)*
- Porsche (founded 1931): Ferdinand Porsche, allegedly a Nazi party and SS member, designed the Beetle at Hitler's request with state funding, engineered tanks, the Schwimmwagen, the Elefant tank destroyer, and contributed to the V1; he used around 3,000 forced laborers and was arrested by the French after the war; son Ferry took over, and the company later gave $2.5 million to a German reparation fund. *([brof_stein/nazi-brands](<../raw/brof_stein/Brofessor Stein - 2025-07-23 - Every Major Brand with Nazi Ties Explained By a German.md>) 4:41-5:45)*
- Audi (then Auto Union), Germany's second-largest carmaker, built tanks, aircraft engines and submarine parts using over 18,000 forced laborers including about 3,500 concentration-camp inmates, notably at Leitmeritz; a 2014 report confirmed moral responsibility for 4,500 deaths there. Daimler-Benz bought 88% in the late 1950s and it became part of Volkswagen in 1964. *([brof_stein/nazi-brands](<../raw/brof_stein/Brofessor Stein - 2025-07-23 - Every Major Brand with Nazi Ties Explained By a German.md>) 7:01-8:02)*
