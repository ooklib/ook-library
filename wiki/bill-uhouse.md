Retired USMC captain and mechanical engineer who, per [[uap-gerb]], claimed to have been a pilot and design engineer for a flying disc simulator built from a recovered disc, and who tied that craft to the 1953 [[kingman-arizona-crash-1953]]. He went public through [[steven-greer]]'s Disclosure Project (transcribed "U house"; spelling as in the transcript).

## Claims

- Background as Gerb tells it: 14 years of USMC service, retired as captain, flight testing of exotic experimental aircraft at [[wright-patterson-air-force-base]] for a defense contractor. *([gerb/kingman](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-03-05 - The 1953 Kingman, Arizona UFO Crash.md>) 13:22-13:46)*
- Uhouse's claims: the disc simulator became operational in 1963-64 (he also says it was not functional until about 1958), based on the roughly 30 meter craft that crashed at Kingman in 1953 (he hedges "53 or 52"); the craft was taken to Area 51 ([[groom-lake]]) and the dead pilots to [[los-alamos-national-laboratory]]; he also mentioned a recovered humanoid codenamed "J-Rod". *([gerb/kingman](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-03-05 - The 1953 Kingman, Arizona UFO Crash.md>) 13:46-15:11)*
- Gerb argues he should not be dismissed for going through Greer, and says his Marine record checks out via a March 1949 reenlistment clipping, a 30 March 1953 newspaper article (8 years' service, Korea, Second Engineer Battalion) and a service company report. A "How and Why" article says a Link Aviation man invited him to help build the F-102 and B-47 simulators and later the flying disc simulator based on Kingman. *([gerb/kingman](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-03-05 - The 1953 Kingman, Arizona UFO Crash.md>) 15:11-17:12)*
