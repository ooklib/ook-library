The publicly documented 1985 controversy in which the Navy was found paying Grumman 660 dollars for E-2C Hawkeye ashtrays. Gerb, who has implicated Grumman in UFO legacy programs before its merger with Northrop, wagers that such excess funds were siphoned off to fund "clandestine deep six UFO crash retrieval and material exploitation programs". The documented overpricing is public record; the funding claim is Gerb's speculation.

## Claims (gerb/navy)

- The Navy paid Grumman 660 dollars each for E-2C Hawkeye ashtrays, 400 dollars per socket wrench for F-14 seats and raised ground-lock payments from 102 dollars in 1982 to 2,710 dollars a year later; it had earlier paid 954 dollars for ashtrays. Defense Secretary Caspar Weinberger removed three officers before any investigation. *([gerb/navy](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2025-04-11 - US Navy UFO Crash Retrieval & Reverse Engineering Programs.md>) 1:34:08-1:35:14)*
