A 1960s CIA Directorate of Science and Technology program (named in April 1962) that used clandestine submarines, ships, radar reflectors and jamming to feed Soviet air defenses fake targets and gauge their capabilities. Gerb, following researcher Jeff Crookshank, says it is referenced in early Majestic 12 documents and reads that as evidence the documents' author knew a program only about six people knew of.

## Claims (gerb/navy)

- Palladium was spearheaded by Samuel Gene Potit, a CIA scientific intelligence officer who was also technical director of the Navy Special Program Office at the NRO. It employed clandestine submarines, radar reflectors and jamming to "fill Soviet radar systems with fake UAP signatures". *([gerb/navy](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2025-04-11 - US Navy UFO Crash Retrieval & Reverse Engineering Programs.md>) 1:25:49-1:26:59)*
- Crookshank argues the psychological-warfare plans in the 28 June 1961 JFK "Tur" memo to [[allen-dulles]] (rated moderate to high authenticity by Ryan Wood) reference Palladium, although it only got its name in April 1962 under DS&T head Albert Wheelon; the medium-confidence "Operation Review the MJ12 Project" document of 5 November 1961 describes mapping Soviet air defense with airborne electronic countermeasures and balloon decoys to see how they perceived UFOs. *([gerb/navy](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2025-04-11 - US Navy UFO Crash Retrieval & Reverse Engineering Programs.md>) 1:26:59-1:28:31)*
- Because Potit wrote that only six people knew of Palladium in the 1960s and it was not declassified until a 1998 CIA journal, and these documents surfaced in 1984 via [[jaime-shandera]], Gerb infers the author knew of or belonged to Palladium. *([gerb/navy](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2025-04-11 - US Navy UFO Crash Retrieval & Reverse Engineering Programs.md>) 1:28:31-1:29:09)*
