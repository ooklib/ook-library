A 1992 memo titled "Research Findings on the Chihuahua Disc Crash", posted on an electronic bulletin board and then mailed anonymously to UFO researchers, dated 23 March 1992 and signed "JS" to "all Deneb team members". It is the primary document for the [[coyame-crash-1974]]. Its origin is unknown; [[uap-gerb]] treats it as a possibly leaked or purposefully leaked memo. *([gerb/coyame](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-08-09 - The 1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash.md>) 2:41-3:38)*

## Claims

- Provenance: paper copies were mailed anonymously to researchers such as [[elaine-douglass]] and [[nick-redfern]], and Elaine Douglass passed the original to [[leonard-stringfield]]. Gerb compares this to the MJ-12 documents' origins. Stringfield's Crash Retrieval Status Report said it was written with correct military terminology and, unlike a hoax, separated hard evidence from speculation. *([gerb/coyame](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-08-09 - The 1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash.md>) 3:05-3:22, 15:04-15:45)*
- Stringfield had heard rumors of the case in the late 1970s or early 1980s, and at his 1978 MUFON presentation was reportedly told circuitously by the CIA it would be inadvisable to discuss crash information in Mexico or West Germany. *([gerb/coyame](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-08-09 - The 1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash.md>) 15:45-16:10)*
- Who is JS: [[noe-torres]] and [[ruben-uriarte]] hypothesize JS is an acronym for a US government department such as Joint Staff or Joint Services; Gerb speculates the "Deneb team" was an internal intelligence community group discussing crash cases on early message boards during the "dark ages" between Blue Book's end (1969) and ATIP (2007), noting [[luis-elizondo]]'s statement that unknown UAP programs ran in the 1980s. The memo mentions a "file UFO 3263", implying it was one of many cases studied. *([gerb/coyame](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-08-09 - The 1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash.md>) 16:10-17:26)*
- The memo says its facts were gathered from two eyewitness accounts, illegally copied documentation and a partially destroyed document, copied in 1978 by a person since dead, and reaching the group only in February 1992. Gerb reads the author as lacking a direct line to CIA, NORAD or other agencies, and the memo repeatedly admits not knowing the craft's analysis, final destination or whether it was manned. *([gerb/coyame](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-08-09 - The 1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash.md>) 13:46-14:53, 17:26-18:13)*
- The name: Deneb is a highly luminous blue supergiant in Cygnus, near where the Milky Way's Great Rift begins, which southern Mexican shamans called the birth of all life (Xibalba be, as transcribed). *([gerb/coyame](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-08-09 - The 1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash.md>) 18:13-18:46)*
- Elaine Douglass died in 2014; her website's documents, possibly including a scan of the original report, are missing from the web archive, and Ryan S. Wood does not hold copies of the original either. *([gerb/coyame](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-08-09 - The 1974 Coyame, Mexico UFO Crash.md>) 44:36-45:23)*

The memo's full contents are summarized at [[coyame-crash-1974]].
