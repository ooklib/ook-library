SAGE (Semi-Automatic Ground Environment) was the US's first comprehensive, computer-based
national air defense system, first conceived in 1951 (as "Project Lincoln") and deployed in
1963, serving as the backbone for NORAD into the 1980s.

## Claims

- Processed and coordinated data from hundreds of radar sites, 24 direction centers, and three
  combat centers into a single unified US airspace picture, using massive IBM computers.
  *([mitre](../raw/107BtDYhTkA_mitre.md) 30:52–31:18)*
- Exceeded the Manhattan Project in cost and personnel; addressed high-speed interception,
  low-altitude radar coverage, air traffic tracking, and data processing/display.
  *([mitre](../raw/107BtDYhTkA_mitre.md) 31:18–31:34)*
- Was a "mixed success": revolutionary computing, but fell short against emerging Soviet ICBM
  threats, was quickly outpaced in hardware, and its systems were easily jammed. *([mitre](../raw/107BtDYhTkA_mitre.md) 32:48–33:04)*
- Gerb speculates SAGE and MITRE detected "a plethora of ... anomalous signatures displaying
  unknown detections over US airspace," citing 1970s SALT-era US–Soviet UFO-alert hotline
  protocols as supporting evidence. *([mitre](../raw/107BtDYhTkA_mitre.md) 33:04–33:51)*
- Archival narration clip: SAGE radar continuously compares expected air traffic (from
  pre-filed IBM punch-card flight schedules) against real radar returns; unexplained "blips" —
  unknown flying objects — are flagged for identification. *([mitre](../raw/107BtDYhTkA_mitre.md) 34:06–35:12)*
