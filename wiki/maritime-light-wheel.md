A recurring phenomenon reported by sailors, especially in the Eastern Hemisphere in the late 19th and early 20th century: a wheel of spokes of brilliant white light appearing beneath the water surface or hovering in the air. [[uap-gerb]] presents it as a possible transmedium craft but is torn whether it is a craft or an anomalous natural phenomenon; the mainstream view, noted in the Russian USO documentary and a paper he links, attributes it to bioluminescent algae interacting with pressure waves in calm water, which he says has no consensus. *([gerb/uso-casebook](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-04-26 - USO Case Book_ Unidentified Submerged Objects Throughout History.md>) 16:42-19:54,)*

## Claims

- 27 March 1873, ship Adhy (as transcribed) from Lisbon to Singapore, Captain Lebman: a blinding milky-white light beneath the waves two ship lengths away, like flakes of snow, followed the ship at 4-5 knots for about half an hour; recorded in an 1873 *Argus* article. *([gerb/uso-casebook](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-04-26 - USO Case Book_ Unidentified Submerged Objects Throughout History.md>) 20:10-20:50)*
- 15 May 1879, Persian Gulf, J. Pringle of HMS Vulture (reported to the Admiralty by hydrographer Captain Evans): luminous pulsations passing under the ship at great speed, then two objects east and west like revolving wheels with illuminated spokes about 25 ft long, rotating in opposite directions. Gerb says it is unclear whether they were submerged. *([gerb/uso-casebook](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-04-26 - USO Case Book_ Unidentified Submerged Objects Throughout History.md>) 20:50-21:37)*
- May 1880, Persian Gulf, British India steamer Patna: two enormous luminous wheels on each side of the ship around 11:30 p.m., spokes 200-300 yards long (about 16 per wheel, wheels 500-600 yards across) brushing along the ship, like flashing a bull's-eye lantern along the water. *([gerb/uso-casebook](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-04-26 - USO Case Book_ Unidentified Submerged Objects Throughout History.md>) 21:37-22:35)*
- 4 April 1901, Persian Gulf, steamship Kila (as transcribed): shafts of light revolving at about 60 mph for about 15 minutes. *([gerb/uso-casebook](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-04-26 - USO Case Book_ Unidentified Submerged Objects Throughout History.md>) 22:35-22:53)*
- 5 January 1880, Malabar coast, SS Shahjahan (as transcribed): a milky-white circle like a nebula seen in the sky at about 10 p.m. on a calm, moonless night. *([gerb/uso-casebook](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-04-26 - USO Case Book_ Unidentified Submerged Objects Throughout History.md>) 22:53-23:30)*
- 10 June 1909, Strait of Malacca, SS Bintang, reported in *Scientific American* from the Danish Meteorological Institute's annual: a vast revolving wheel of light under the water, long arms from a center, so large only half was visible, varying in speed and rotation and vanishing when the center was under the vessel. *([gerb/uso-casebook](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-04-26 - USO Case Book_ Unidentified Submerged Objects Throughout History.md>) 23:30-24:16)*
- 1910, South China Sea, Dutch steamship Valen chin (as transcribed), same Danish source: captain, mates and engineer saw an object hovering above the water like a horizontal, rapidly turning wheel. *([gerb/uso-casebook](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-04-26 - USO Case Book_ Unidentified Submerged Objects Throughout History.md>) 24:16-24:48)*
