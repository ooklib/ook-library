The Northrop Advanced Technology and Design Center (NATDC, also referred to as ADTC), formed by
at least 1991 at Northrop's Pico Rivera facility in Los Angeles County (the primary B-2 bomber
development/production site), is Gerb's candidate for Northrop's answer to
[[skunk-works|Lockheed's Skunk Works]] and Boeing's Phantom Works. It was created to preserve
Northrop's core-defense-program expertise and adapt B-2 breakthroughs onto new airframes.
*([northrop-trw](../raw/0kLSp0lH-JQ_northrop_trw.md) 2:53:51–2:54:40)*

## Claims

- A 1991 Dayton Air Show handout (confirmed by a 1992 *Aviation Week & Space Technology* piece)
  lists several NATDC airframe concepts and a composite STOVL (short takeoff/vertical landing)
  wing design, including an F-16 derivative, a multi-role fighter, a high-reliability fighter,
  the "AX," "CASX," a low-cost fighter, advanced pilot-interface technologies, an "arrowhead AX"
  design, and a special-purpose stealth aircraft for special-operations transport.
  *([northrop-trw](../raw/0kLSp0lH-JQ_northrop_trw.md) 2:54:59–2:55:57)*
- Gerb draws a speculative parallel between these designs and Brad Sorenson's 1988 Norton AFB Air
  Show "Flux Liner" (three man-made flying saucers nicknamed Mama, Papa, and Little Bear)
  testimony, which also described classified airframes including a remote-piloted Aurora and VTOL
  troop transports, wondering whether NATDC contributed to the Flux Liner craft.
  *([northrop-trw](../raw/0kLSp0lH-JQ_northrop_trw.md) 2:55:57–2:56:47)*
- Engineer Richard Kerrion led several NATDC propulsion projects — an Affordable Lightweight
  Fighter (ALF), a STOVL Joint Tactical Aircraft (SJTA, where he was "responsible for coordinating
  all technical design activities for this multi-role fighter IRAD"), and a Multi-Role Fighter
  (MRF) — the same three airframes named in the 1991 handout; none were ever realized as
  functional aircraft despite multi-million-dollar IRAD spending, leading Gerb to wonder if they
  were a front. *([northrop-trw](../raw/0kLSp0lH-JQ_northrop_trw.md) 2:58:59–3:00:34)*
- Air Force Brigadier General Delbert H. Jacobs served as VP and center manager for NATDC in 1995,
  describing it (in a 1995 book, *Creative Action and Organization*) as "responsible for
  originating, coordinating, and directing all Northrop research technology development and
  design efforts in future manned and unmanned aircraft systems as well as other advanced
  applications"; his obituary separately described the role as head of "advanced stealth fighter
  design and development." *([northrop-trw](../raw/0kLSp0lH-JQ_northrop_trw.md)
  3:00:53–3:01:41)*
- Irv Walland — the engineer who led the B-2 Spirit Bomber's design team and was named VP/chief
  designer of Northrop's NATDC in 1988 — delivered a 1991 AIAA Wright Brothers Lecture describing
  a "spectrum of designs" including stealth programs featuring a delta/diamond-shaped vehicle
  alongside the B-2. Gerb notes Walland's own CV curiously omits any extensive mention of his
  NATDC work outside this one speech. *([northrop-trw](../raw/0kLSp0lH-JQ_northrop_trw.md)
  3:02:55–3:04:44)*
- Mentions of NATDC largely vanish after 1992/1999 (sparse later references include a 1994 NASA
  technical report, the 1996–97 *World Aircraft and Systems Directory*, and a 1999 NASA paper on
  gravitational research). Around 2000–2001, Pico Rivera's labs/facilities moved to El Segundo,
  California (home of the Aerospace Corporation) and were rolled into a newly established
  Northrop Grumman Advanced Systems Development Center (ASDC), critical to early-2000s UAV RDT&E
  including the X-47A Pegasus (notably "company funded" as a "private venture," which Gerb
  suggests may indicate a major IRAD project). By 2003 — the year of Northrop's TRW acquisition —
  both NATDC and ASDC disappear from the record entirely.
  *([northrop-trw](../raw/0kLSp0lH-JQ_northrop_trw.md) 3:05:00–3:06:41)*
- Gerb theorizes NATDC's disappearance, timed suspiciously close to Northrop's alleged breakaway
  UFO crash-retrieval and reverse-engineering activity, actually reflects continued operation —
  likely out of [[air-force-plant-42|Air Force Plant 42]] — as Northrop's own in-house RDT&E
  institution for alien reproduction vehicles and derivative airframes, funded through
  self-funded IRAD with little to no government-sponsor accountability.
  *([northrop-trw](../raw/0kLSp0lH-JQ_northrop_trw.md) 2:57:43–2:58:32, 3:06:22–3:06:57)*
