[[ted-shackley]]'s term for covert action, especially paramilitary and counterinsurgency operations, as a "third option" between diplomacy and open war, set out in his 1981 book *The Third Option: An American View of Counterinsurgency*. [[eyes-wide-open]] (`ewa/shackley`) uses it and his two other books to characterize him and as source-critical context.

## The Third Option (1981)

- First book, published two years after his 1979 retirement; "a fundamentally bitter work" dedicated to the Hmong fighters he employed in Laos ([[secret-war-in-laos]]) and opening with a denunciation of the Carter administration that terminated him and colleagues ([[halloween-massacre]]). *([ewa/shackley](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-10-03 - The CIA's Blond Ghost - Ted Shackley.md) 13:37-13:58)*
- Presents covert action as outside the diplomacy/warfare dichotomy; quote: "all aspects of covert action, which in some lexicons is defined as the influencing of people and the shaping of events through the use of political, economic, or military factors without the sponsor of this activity being identified in an attributable manner." Host: an advocacy of plausible deniability and of relying more openly on intelligence agencies against communist insurgencies. *([ewa/shackley](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-10-03 - The CIA's Blond Ghost - Ted Shackley.md) 14:04-15:13)*
- Prediction quoted: the 1980s as a decade of "reacting to challenges much like a counterpunching boxer" with irregular warfare focused on "armed clandestine organizations." *([ewa/shackley](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-10-03 - The CIA's Blond Ghost - Ted Shackley.md) 15:31-15:53)*
- Host: its release coincided with the Reagan administration, [[william-casey]] at CIA and [[george-h-w-bush]] as VP, who would oversee Iran-Contra, "a worldwide narco counterinsurgency operation" with Shackley as off-the-books logistics figure; the book "reads more like a policy prescription" institutionalized under [[ronald-reagan]]. Interpretation by the host. *([ewa/shackley](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-10-03 - The CIA's Blond Ghost - Ted Shackley.md) 16:02-16:59)* See [[narco-counterinsurgency]].

## Still the Target (1989) and Spy Master (2005)

- *Still the Target: Coping with Terror and Crime* (co-author Richard Finney; ASR "Finn"/"Finny"): travel-safety advice on terrorism; host: apparently barely sold, timed for the incoming Bush administration and the post-Cold War shift toward a terror paradigm, an early anticipation of the global war on terror later "designed and rolled out by Bush the father's cronies." Host interpretation. *([ewa/shackley](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-10-03 - The CIA's Blond Ghost - Ted Shackley.md) 17:14-18:59)*
- *Spy Master: My Life in the CIA*: posthumous 2005, manuscript finished before his December 2002 death, co-written by Finney; host calls it "a truly sanitized history" published by "PTOAC Books" (ASR, publisher name uncertain), a house of similar official CIA memoirs (mentions U-2 pilot Francis Gary Powers), said to be based in Dulles, Virginia; host speculates (flagged as speculation) it is agency-affiliated. He uses it sparingly, noting Shackley "tells on himself inadvertently" at points; e.g. claims he would have been DCI with an Ivy League degree, which the host reads as coping. *([ewa/shackley](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-10-03 - The CIA's Blond Ghost - Ted Shackley.md) 18:59-21:16, 37:13-37:29)*
- Secondary source: David Corn's 1994 *Blond Ghost*, the only full-length outside biography; host warns Corn had unusual CIA access and his sources may be "limited hangouts," unverifiable. *([ewa/shackley](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-10-03 - The CIA's Blond Ghost - Ted Shackley.md) 21:16-22:52)*
