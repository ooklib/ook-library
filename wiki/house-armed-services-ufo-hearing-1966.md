A closed session on UFOs held on 5 April 1966 by the House Armed Services Committee, which [[uap-gerb]] calls Congress's first, called for by Congressman Gerald Ford (later president). See also the later [[mendel-rivers-hearings-1968]].

## Claims

- Ford noted the ridicule he received for arranging it; part of his reasoning was dissatisfaction with [[j-allen-hynek]]'s swamp-gas and weather-balloon explanations, particularly the 1966 Dexter, Michigan sighting. *([gerb/stigma](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-02-09 - The Origin of the UFO Stigma.md>) 13:01-13:42)*
- Witnesses: Blue Book head Maj. Hector Quintanilla (1963-69), Hynek and Air Force Secretary Harold Brown. Quintanilla said Blue Book had "no radar cases that are unexplained", which Gerb calls an irrefutable lie, citing Hynek's later pointing to unexplained radar cases at Goose Bay 1951, Lakenheath 1956 and Shreveport 1957. *([gerb/stigma](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-02-09 - The Origin of the UFO Stigma.md>) 13:42-14:37)*
- Hynek, feeling used as a "puppet of the Air Force", prepared a surprise statement asking the USAF to commission a civilian panel of physical and social scientists; he and [[james-e-mcdonald]] asked for seats and were denied, and Edward Condon was selected in summer 1966 for about $313,000. *([gerb/stigma](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-02-09 - The Origin of the UFO Stigma.md>) 14:37-15:39)*
- Condon received offers of files from retired Marine Maj. Donald Keyhoe, [[nicap]], McDonald and Hynek and, per Gerb, declined many; the committee moved slowly, lacked expertise and members worked without coordination. *([gerb/stigma](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-02-09 - The Origin of the UFO Stigma.md>) 15:39-16:11)*
