Rachel Reeves is cited by Green & Pleasant as the most recent (2025) example in the video's
40-year "same room" narrative — a Labour Chancellor delivering a deregulatory speech from inside
the Lord Mayor's official residence, at the [[city-of-london-corporation]]'s invitation.

## Claims

- In July 2025, as Labour Chancellor, delivered the annual Mansion House speech (the Lord Mayor
  of the City of London's official residence, where the Chancellor speaks as the City's guest
  rather than at the parliamentary dispatch box) announcing a package she called the "Leeds
  Reforms," rolling back parts of the banking regulation introduced after the 2008 financial
  crash. She called regulation "a boot on the neck of business." Green & Pleasant's framing: a
  Labour chancellor, in the Lord Mayor's house, unwinding banking safeguards a Labour government
  had built after the last crisis — "the government changed, the party changed, and the city's
  position did not move an inch"
  *([gp/older-than-parliament](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-06-03 - The Government Inside Britain That's Older Than Parliament.md) 15:39, 15:47, 15:54, 16:02, 16:09, 16:14, 16:22)*.

## Relationships

- Cited as the most recent instance of a UK Chancellor acting, per Green & Pleasant, on behalf of
  the [[city-of-london-corporation]]'s interests, via the 2025 Mansion House "Leeds Reforms"
  speech.

## Speculation flag

The 2025 Mansion House speech, its "Leeds Reforms" name, and the "boot on the neck of business"
quote are matters of public record; the framing that this represents continuity with (rather than
a break from) the City's historical influence over UK financial regulation is Green & Pleasant's
interpretive claim.
