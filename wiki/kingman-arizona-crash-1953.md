Cited by Gerb as an example of "Southwest systems" used to intake recovered craft around this
period, associated with [[sandia-national-laboratories|Sandia]]. *([gerb/mitre](../raw/gerb/mitre.md) 2:04:13–2:04:30)*

## Claims

- Some of Gerb's sources state the materials [[lockheed-martin|Lockheed Martin]] attempted to
  divest via the [[operation-kona-blue|Kona Blue]] PAP (2008–2011) were the 1953 Kingman crash
  or part of it — craft hull and other pieces Lockheed allegedly had no success exploiting
  (chemistry, metallurgy, etc.) since the 1950s. Gerb holds this with "moderate to high"
  conviction. *([gerb/lockheed](../raw/gerb/lockheed.md) 1:47:45–1:48:12, 2:04:22–2:04:47,
  2:39:41–2:40:03)*

- Primary witness Arthur Stansel Jr. (pseudonym "Fritz Warner" per 1973 UFO researcher Raymond
  Fowler) disclosed in a signed affidavit that on 21 May 1953 he was summoned to a crashed,
  roughly 30-ft oval unknown object near Kingman, constructed of an unfamiliar brushed-aluminum-
  like metal, with reported occupant bodies nearby. Stansel, a mechanical-branch engineer under
  Air Research and Development Command (ARDC) working the Operation Upshot–Knothole nuclear
  tests, named [[edward-dahl|Dr. Ed Dahl]] as the man who summoned him ("go on a special job") and
  Dr. Eric Henry Wang as crash-retrieval/reverse-engineering team lead. Stansel's own diary
  (20 May 1953) records a "funny call from Dr. D" the night before. *([gerb/northrop_trw](../raw/gerb/northrop_trw.md) 1:12:52–1:16:24)*
