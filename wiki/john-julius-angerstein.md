John Julius Angerstein, "sometimes called the father of [[lloyds-of-london]]," is cited by
Green & Pleasant as a documented link between the City of London's marine-insurance business and
the transatlantic slave trade, and as the source of the founding collection of the National
Gallery in London.

## Claims

- Sometimes called the father of Lloyd's, for his role formalizing/reorganizing the London
  marine insurance market. Has now been formally identified as a trustee of a slave plantation in
  Grenada *([gp/older-than-parliament](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-06-03 - The Government Inside Britain That's Older Than Parliament.md) 11:53, 12:02)*.
- Was also an art collector; after his death the British government bought his collection, which
  became the founding collection of the National Gallery
  *([gp/older-than-parliament](../raw/gp/Green & Pleasant - 2026-06-03 - The Government Inside Britain That's Older Than Parliament.md) 12:02, 12:10)*.

## Relationships
