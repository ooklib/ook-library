The federal antitrust case that ended with the court-ordered breakup of [[standard-oil]] on 15 May 1911. In this account it began under [[theodore-roosevelt]] and was completed after his presidency.

## Claims

- Roosevelt had earlier passed a bill punishing railroad rebates, but the campaign vs Standard exploited [[ida-tarbell]]-driven public anger and his re-election promise to take down monopolies. *([mm/rockefeller](<../raw/mm/MagnatesMedia - 2023-11-19 - Rockefeller - The World’s First Billionaire.md>) 40:40, 40:46, 42:21, 42:26)*
- Charges: monopolization, predatory pricing, rebates, corporate espionage and secret ownership of competitors; plus 21 state suits, over a thousand exhibits, 444 witnesses and 12,000+ pages of evidence; Standard held 87% of refining, 100% in some places, and had bribed hundreds of politicians. *([mm/rockefeller](<../raw/mm/MagnatesMedia - 2023-11-19 - Rockefeller - The World’s First Billionaire.md>) 42:47, 43:02, 43:23, 43:39)*
- Ordered dissolved into 34 unaffiliated companies; the narrator says this made [[john-d-rockefeller]] richer, since his roughly quarter share in each firm nearly tripled in value once traded, nearing billionaire status by end 1911. *([mm/rockefeller](<../raw/mm/MagnatesMedia - 2023-11-19 - Rockefeller - The World’s First Billionaire.md>) 43:48, 44:51, 45:05)*
- Context given for why breakup suited him: new oil fields in Texas and California, foreign competition from the Middle East and Russia, and gasoline overtaking kerosene. *([mm/rockefeller](<../raw/mm/MagnatesMedia - 2023-11-19 - Rockefeller - The World’s First Billionaire.md>) 44:29, 44:37, 44:45)*
