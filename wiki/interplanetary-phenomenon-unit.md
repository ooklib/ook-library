The Interplanetary Phenomenon Unit (IPU) is a disputed but partially Army-corroborated 1940s–1950s
unit whose alleged internal reports are among the more discussed disputed documents in UAP Gerb's
catalog. The Army has confirmed to FOIA requesters that an IPU existed in some form; UAP Gerb
treats its documented existence, disestablishment, and disputed reports as one more layer of the
alleged "Manhattan Project 2.0" onion. *([gerb/secrecy_1](../raw/gerb/secrecy_1.md)
2:05:42–2:06:51)*

## Claims

- The Army's Department of Counter Intelligence has confirmed the IPU existed; it was
  disestablished in the late 1950s, and its records were surrendered to the Air Force's AFOSI in
  conjunction with [[project-blue-book]], confirming it dealt with the UFO phenomenon. Per FOIA
  responses, it was an "in-house project" — an interest item for an unnamed Army Assistant Chief
  of Staff for Intelligence (G2) — that lacked formal function, mission, or authority, and is
  known to the Army today only through "institutional memory." Debate continues over whether the
  unit was genuinely UFO-focused or simply the mail-processing "input processing unit" of the
  Army's mail room. *([gerb/secrecy_1](../raw/gerb/secrecy_1.md) 2:05:49–2:07:22)*
- Researchers Ryan S. Wood and the late Dr. Robert Wood argued the "unnamed G2" framing was a
  cover for real IPU leadership under General [[george-c-marshall|George C. Marshall]] (Army
  Chief of Staff 1939–1945, Secretary of State 1947–1949, Secretary of Defense 1950–1951). UAP
  Gerb finds this conceptually consistent with the Manhattan Project's [[top-policy-group|top
  policy group]] pattern of senior leaders hiding behind subordinate offices.
  *([gerb/secrecy_1](../raw/gerb/secrecy_1.md) 2:07:22–2:08:13)*
- A disputed IPU report dated 22 July 1947 states Marshall (then Secretary of State) was the only
  cabinet member briefed on the crashed UFOs, with Secretary of Defense James Forrestal to be
  briefed on certain aspects; it also places select Atomic Energy Commission scientists, including
  [[j-robert-oppenheimer|Dr. Robert Oppenheimer]], on the crash-retrieval team, and names a
  "Colonel Housebrook" (real Army Colonel Sherman V. Hasbrouck) of the
  [[armed-forces-special-weapons-project|Armed Forces Special Weapons Project]] as having ordered
  a special radiobiological team, a Manhattan Project-era Special Engineer Detachment (SED), and a
  Sandia National Labs security detail to secure the immediate Roswell crash area.
  *([gerb/secrecy_1](../raw/gerb/secrecy_1.md) 2:08:51–2:10:32)*
- UAP Gerb notes [[j-andrew-kner|J. Andrew Kner]]'s 1994 statement — describing AEC-lab custody of
  recovered discs — could not have derived from the IPU documents, which were not FOIA-released
  until the mid-1990s, raising (without resolving) the question of whether the two accounts
  independently corroborate each other. *([gerb/sandia](../raw/gerb/sandia.md) 39:36–39:57)*
- Special Engineer Detachments (SEDs) — the Manhattan Engineer District's 1943 designation for
  military personnel with scientific/technical backgrounds assigned alongside civilian
  scientists at Los Alamos, Sandia's Z Division, and Oak Ridge — are described in the IPU
  documents as an on-site technical unit at UFO crash sites, a detail UAP Gerb finds intriguing
  given SED personnel's later prominent physics/engineering/academic careers.
  *([gerb/secrecy_1](../raw/gerb/secrecy_1.md) 2:10:32–2:11:20)*
