William Howard Taft was the first civilian governor of the Philippines, later Secretary of War and president. [[eyes-wide-open]] presents him as one of a "triumvirate" (with [[elihu-root]] and Theodore Roosevelt) of Northeastern Wall Street patricians who in the host's account ran US foreign policy from roughly 1899 to 1913 and whose feud with the MacArthurs seeded [[douglas-macarthur]]'s lifelong resentment of the "Eastern elite". *(host's framing, opinion)*

## Claims

- Host: the triumvirate's "quasi oligarchic control" of the US foreign apparatus for about 20 years "shaped the US empire as we know it today" and laid the groundwork for the Wall Street-dominated neoliberal Cold War order. *([ewa/willoughby-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-12-25 - The MacArthur Legacy - 50 Years of Empire Building in the Pacific - WILLOUGHBY 2.md) 8:39-9:17)*
- His father Alfonso Taft (Grant's secretary of war and attorney general 1876-77, ambassador to Russia) co-founded the [[skull-and-bones]] society at Yale in 1832 with William Huntington Russell of the Russell family (owners of Russell and Co., "the most significant American opium distributor in China"); William Howard and his son Robert were also members. Host says Bones birthed many careers of the US elite "not to mention large parts of the early cadre of the CIA" and that East Asia links are "baked into" both Bones and the Tafts. *([ewa/willoughby-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-12-25 - The MacArthur Legacy - 50 Years of Empire Building in the Pacific - WILLOUGHBY 2.md) 9:37-10:38)*
- Headed the Philippine Commission (June 1900) and clashed with [[arthur-macarthur-jr]] over the strength of Filipino resistance; with Root's help had MacArthur recalled in July 1901 and ran the colony with civilian control from 1902; recalled February 1904 and rewarded with Root's old job as Secretary of War. *([ewa/willoughby-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-12-25 - The MacArthur Legacy - 50 Years of Empire Building in the Pacific - WILLOUGHBY 2.md) 8:19-8:45, 13:29-13:36, 17:34-18:44)*
- Host: Roosevelt convinced him to run in 1908 and he won; MacArthur viewed him as a lightweight. Roosevelt then split the Republican vote via the Bull Moose party in 1912 and Wilson won; Taft later taught law at Yale and was chief justice 1921-30. *([ewa/willoughby-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-12-25 - The MacArthur Legacy - 50 Years of Empire Building in the Pacific - WILLOUGHBY 2.md) 19:08-20:18)*
- Host names Assistant Secretary of War John J. McCloy ("chairman of the establishment") as a later member of "the very same circles Taft was prominent in", who called Douglas MacArthur "a man on horseback threatening democracy" over postwar Japan. *([ewa/willoughby-2](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2025-12-25 - The MacArthur Legacy - 50 Years of Empire Building in the Pacific - WILLOUGHBY 2.md) 16:45-17:08)*
