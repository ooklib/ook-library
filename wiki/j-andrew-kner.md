J. Andrew Kner, a former New Mexico State Representative (the video's ASR consistently renders
his name "Kner"/"Knisser"/"Kissner"), authored a 1994 written statement — "Peculiar Phenomenon:
Early United States Efforts to Collect and Analyze Flying Discs" — based on years of private
interviews with firsthand UFO legacy-program personnel, including employees of White Sands
Proving Ground, the Naval Research Laboratory, and AEC labs (Los Alamos, Lawrence Livermore, Oak
Ridge, [[sandia-national-laboratories|Sandia]]), former high-ranking military officers, defense
contractors, and Office of Naval Research staff. The statement was presented at the second annual
UFO Crash Retrieval Conference (12–14 November 2004) by Ryan and the late Dr. Robert Wood, read
into the record by Linda Moulton Howe — whom Gerb calls "an unreliable source at best" — but Gerb
says he separately contacted Kner directly and found him credible. *([sandia](../raw/mHkPHd_eOXI_
sandia.md) 22:18–24:06)*

## Claims

- Kner's sources describe a series of events beginning 15 May 1947 in which elements of the US
  Army offensively downed two to four "flying discs" (one being Roswell), contributing to the
  deaths of several US servicemen between 9 May and 31 July 1947; the "special weapons aboard
  flying discs" were immediately classified as "enemy weapons systems." *([sandia](../raw/
  mHkPHd_eOXI_sandia.md) 24:06–24:47)*
- The discs were processed and analyzed for Dr. [[vannevar-bush|Vannevar Bush]] (chairman of the
  Joint Chiefs' Joint Research and Development Board and director of the Office of Scientific
  Research and Development), assisted by Air Materiel Command and personnel of the
  [[armed-forces-special-weapons-project|Armed Forces Special Weapons Project]] at Sandia Base;
  further analysis was allegedly supported by the Department of Terrestrial Magnetism, MIT
  personnel, the National Advisory Committee on Aeronautics, and the
  [[rand-corporation|RAND Corporation]]. *([sandia](../raw/mHkPHd_eOXI_sandia.md)
  25:33–27:23)*
- Under a classified 1948 presidential executive order, custody of the discs was vested in
  civilian authorities — the [[atomic-energy-commission|AEC]]'s contractor-operated national labs
  (Los Alamos and the soon-to-be-separate Sandia), assisted by Air Force Systems Command, the
  Naval Research Lab, and the Research and Development Board. *([sandia](../raw/mHkPHd_eOXI_
  sandia.md) 27:39–28:49)*
- Elements of the "Lashup" Soviet-bomber early-warning radar network at White Sands, Los Alamos,
  Sandia Base, and the 767th ACNW radar site could, per Kner, have been used to track UFOs — and,
  per the [[aztec-new-mexico-crash-1948|Aztec, New Mexico]] case, may have interfered with and
  downed a manned non-human disc. *([sandia](../raw/mHkPHd_eOXI_sandia.md) 30:01–30:34)*
- Shared his findings with fellow NM Rep. Steven Schiff, whose resulting GAO probe (report
  released 28 July 1995) found Roswell Army Airfield's outgoing messages for the relevant period
  had been "destroyed without proper authority" by parties the GAO could not identify — an
  investigation that led to the Air Force's 1997 "Roswell Report: Case Closed" (Project Mogul
  explanation). *([sandia](../raw/mHkPHd_eOXI_sandia.md) 32:03–33:39)*
- Gerb: Kner's account "almost sounds like a 1994 precursor to David Grush," and the two are
  "kind of" alike; Gerb would like to get Kner on record for an interview.
  *([sandia](../raw/mHkPHd_eOXI_sandia.md) 23:42–23:50, 31:48–31:57)*
