The "sect crisis" of 1955 was the first year of [[ngo-dinh-diem]]'s presidency, when independent sectarian militias (the esoteric Cao Dai, ASR "Cai"/"kaai", and the organized-crime [[binh-xuyen]], put in charge of Saigon-area policing by Bao Dai in the war's last years) staged dozens of coup attempts and riots. Diem's regime answered with repression, the Binh Xuyen first, then the Cao Dai, and "much later" the Buddhists, which the host says would spell its downfall. *([ewa/laos-3](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-15 - The French Connection in South East Asia - LAOS 3.md) 32:28-33:36)*

## Claims

- Per the Pentagon Papers, from December 1954 [[edward-lansdale]] armed and trained Diem loyalists within the reformed army to counter the sects; he urged a unity government with the sects but Diem refused; by March 1955 the Binh Xuyen and the Army of the Republic of Vietnam (ARVN) clashed continuously, culminating in the week-long Battle of Saigon. *([ewa/laos-3](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-15 - The French Connection in South East Asia - LAOS 3.md) 33:36-34:14)*
- The Pentagon Papers reveal Lansdale imported a contingent of Filipino counter-guerrilla forces from his former client and then Philippine president Ramon Magsaysay and got him to open a CIA-financed "psychological warfare counter guerrilla school", the Security Training Center at Fort McKinley near Manila, where Vietnamese personnel were trained. *([ewa/laos-3](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-15 - The French Connection in South East Asia - LAOS 3.md) 34:14-34:47)*
- The host says this Filipino connection persisted through the Vietnam War, with Filipino, South Korean and Thai mercenaries doing much of the "dirty work" and shielding US personnel from atrocities, which he suggests illuminates Lansdale's earlier work for Magsaysay. *([ewa/laos-3](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-15 - The French Connection in South East Asia - LAOS 3.md) 34:47-35:19)*
- Lansdale then hired Filipino Colonel Napoleon Valeriano (ASR "valian") to head Diem's presidential guard, saying he chose him for his "fine record against the Communist Huks". *([ewa/laos-3](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-15 - The French Connection in South East Asia - LAOS 3.md) 35:24-35:49)*
- Aftermath: in the host's telling, the [[binh-xuyen]] opium dens were crushed and Laos opium exports "dropped dramatically" 1954-58 until the Nhu-[[air-laos-commerce]] alliance revived the trade. *([ewa/laos-3](../raw/ewa/Eyes Wide Open - 2024-11-15 - The French Connection in South East Asia - LAOS 3.md) 47:17-47:39)*
