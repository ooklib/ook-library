TRW is a defense contractor whose close relationship with the Air Force prompted the 1960 spinoff
of the [[aerospace-corporation]] as an independent FFRDC. *([gerb/mitre](../raw/gerb/mitre.md) 21:59–22:14, 1:03:39–1:03:46)*

TRW Inc., formally Thompson Ramo Wooldridge Inc., traces its roots to 1901 and existed under that
name from 1965 until its purchase by [[northrop-grumman|Northrop Grumman]] in 2002 — a deal Gerb
calls Northrop's "crown jewel" acquisition. TRW spanned aerospace, electronics, automotive,
defense, and credit reporting, but was renowned for defense/aerospace work: spacecraft (Pioneer 1,
Pioneer 10, Apollo), defense/communication satellites (including the Defense Support Program/DSP
missile-launch-detection satellites), and — through its Space Technology Laboratories (STL,
renamed TRW Systems Group) — the US's first ICBM and later Titan missile programs. Gerb considers
TRW the entity he finds "the most value in investigating" of Northrop's acquisitions, given its
"countless ties to UFO reverse engineering and crash retrieval programs." *([gerb/northrop_trw](
../raw/gerb/northrop_trw.md) 12:24–12:42, 32:29–34:11)*

## Claims (from the Northrop Grumman & TRW Vol.2 video)

- [[edward-dahl|Dr. Edward Bushnell Dahl]], named by Kingman, Arizona crash witness Arthur
  Stansel Jr. as a coordinator of the 1953 [[kingman-arizona-crash-1953|Kingman]] crash-retrieval
  effort, joined TRW's Space Technology Laboratories in 1955, became senior VP of TRW Systems
  Group by 1966, and retired as executive VP in 1977. TRW Systems Group comprised three units —
  space and electronics, systems (systems engineering/integration, C3 systems), and aeronautics
  (now owned by Raytheon) — with systems engineering and integration doing CIA contract work
  including on the "Safe Project," and special projects (led in 1965 by chief scientist Bill
  Carson) building NRO Program B's SIGINT satellites. *([gerb/northrop_trw](../raw/gerb/northrop_trw.md) 1:17:55–1:26:32)* See [[pine-gap-australia]] and
  [[everett-heinman]].
- Acquired BDM (Braddock, Dunn & McDonald Corporation) in 1997 — the contractor whose board
  included [[sumner-shapiro|Rear Admiral Sumner Shapiro]], former Director of Naval Intelligence.
  *([gerb/northrop_trw](../raw/gerb/northrop_trw.md) 44:42–45:05)*
- Alleged managing/critical entity to the [[zodiac-crash-retrieval-program|Zodiac]] crash-
  retrieval program per [[richard-dolan|Richard Dolan]]'s research and [[eric-davis|Eric Davis]]'s
  account (via Jacques Vallee's journals) of a TRW special study on Roswell-crash data.
  *([gerb/northrop_trw](../raw/gerb/northrop_trw.md) 45:57–49:19)*
- Conducted late-1960s/1970s studies on "state-of-the-art tube vehicle systems," detailing
  underground train systems down to 3,000 ft that could travel 450+ mph — material Gerb has
  previously connected to his deep-underground-military-base (DUMB) research.
  *([gerb/northrop_trw](../raw/gerb/northrop_trw.md) 49:19–49:44)*
- Leading contractor on SDI, with a $340.5M Brilliant Pebbles Initiative contract and a $200M Air
  Force compact space-qualifiable laser contract; Gerb speculates whether these contract vehicles
  were secretly backchanneled to TRW's compartmented legacy-program work.
  *([gerb/northrop_trw](../raw/gerb/northrop_trw.md) 1:09:11–1:09:51)*
- [[stephanie-osullivan|Stephanie O'Sullivan]] joined TRW around 1982, serving in a data-masked
  role overseeing "a series of sensitive projects for TRW" for the Navy, before moving to the
  Office of Naval Intelligence in 1985; [[dawn-meyerriecks|Dawn Meyerriecks]] (TRW technical staff, 1981–1993)
  overlapped with her there for roughly two years. [[richard-haver|Richard Haver]] likely crossed
  paths with O'Sullivan there or at ONI. *([gerb/northrop_trw](../raw/gerb/northrop_trw.md)
  1:43:52–1:44:47, 3:16:57–3:17:21)*
- [[richard-haver|Richard L. Haver]] joined TRW in 1999 as VP/director for intelligence programs,
  continuing at Northrop Grumman after the 2002 acquisition until 2010; a labeled Dick Cheney
  protege. *([gerb/northrop_trw](../raw/gerb/northrop_trw.md) 1:46:16–1:47:12)*
- [[donald-c-winter|Donald C. Winter]] joined TRW in 1972, later worked SDI's
  [[talon-gold|Talon Gold]] program under DARPA, and returned to TRW as CEO before leading
  Northrop's mission systems post-acquisition. *([gerb/northrop_trw](../raw/gerb/northrop_trw.md) 39:00–43:51)*
- TRW's 2001 annual report shows company-funded R&D (including IRAD) spending of $468M, $442M,
  and $442M in 1999–2001, lowering automotive R&D to increase spending in space/electronics and
  new aeronautical programs. TRW was found to have engaged in five separate IRAD-fraud schemes
  between 1990–1997 (mischarging, misclassifying private work, mischarging fabrication/prototyping
  costs to avoid IRAD ceilings), leading to Northrop's $111.2M 2003 False Claims Act settlement;
  the GAO separately investigated TRW for IRAD misclassification in 1989, and a VP departed TRW
  in 1986 amid a similar overcharging scandal. Gerb also cites a 5 April 1982 CIA DS&T memo
  ("CIA Interference with DoD Tri-Services Committee re: Industrial Contractors' Negotiation of
  Advance Agreement on Independent Research and Development") stating the CIA DS&T sought to
  channel contractor IRAD funds — including from TRW, [[e-systems|E-Systems]], Lockheed Missiles
  and Space Company, Rockwell, and Harris — into DS&T's specific areas of need, which Gerb reads
  as likely early evidence of TRW misappropriating IRAD funds for the CIA DS&T.
  *([gerb/northrop_trw](../raw/gerb/northrop_trw.md) 2:07:13–2:12:23)*
