An alleged UFO crash in May 1958 in a civilian cornfield near a Nike Ajax missile site by Olney, Maryland, told by [[harland-bentley]] and relayed by [[uap-gerb]] in [[gerb-whistleblowers-2-video]]. Gerb notes he has never heard of another UFO crash where the craft regained flight, and finds no supporting evidence for May 1958.

## Claims (gerb/whistleblowers-2)

- Bentley says at 6:00 a.m. in May 1958 he heard a sound like a pulsating transformer, looked out of his window and saw a disc-shaped object about 2,000 yards away in a cornfield collide with the ground and immediately take off again. Gerb compares the sound to the one [[michael-herrera]] described in 2009. *([gerb/whistleblowers-2](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-05-26 - UFO Whistleblowers [Vol.2].md>) 3:11-3:55)*
- He ran to a hill to see the debris: a washing-machine-sized metallic piece glowing white hot. Soon USAF personnel arrived in radiation protection suits, used long poles to load pieces onto a lead-lined truck, and he never learned where it went. Gerb notes this pattern recurs in crash-retrieval stories. *([gerb/whistleblowers-2](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-05-26 - UFO Whistleblowers [Vol.2].md>) 3:55-4:32)*
- The craft was about 30 ft in diameter, saucer-like, with ball-like lights on the rim cycling orange, red and white, pulsating, and a cake-like structure on top; it was "wobbling" before impact, which Gerb links to a 1950s Blue Book USO case with a wobbling cigar shape. *([gerb/whistleblowers-2](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-05-26 - UFO Whistleblowers [Vol.2].md>) 4:32-5:20)*
- When it took off again it sheared thick tree limbs like a sharp knife, which Gerb compares to [[jonathan-weygandt]]'s alleged 1997 Peru crash ([[weygandt-peru-crash-encounter-1997]]). *([gerb/whistleblowers-2](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-05-26 - UFO Whistleblowers [Vol.2].md>) 5:20-5:42)*
- Radar follow-up: the next evening (about 10-11 p.m.) the nearby Gaithersburg facility phoned to say 12 to 15 UFOs were hovering 50 to 100 ft off the ground; the radio operator heard the same pulsating sound through a headset, and Bentley's site saw the blips on its M33 radar sweep, after which all signatures left in one sweep. Gerb, citing the M33's 33.33 RPM sweep, says Bentley's analog computer calculation implied over 177,000 mph, and compares Hermann Oberth's 1954 claim of recorded speeds of 19 km/s (about 42,000 mph). A presiding general debriefed the men saying they were helicopters in Navy, Army and Air Force training. *([gerb/whistleblowers-2](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-05-26 - UFO Whistleblowers [Vol.2].md>) 5:42-7:01)*
- Corroboration attempts: Gerb finds only a passing mention in Baltimore's Evening Sun of 23 December 1958 of the US Air Force capturing at least two flying saucers. *([gerb/whistleblowers-2](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-05-26 - UFO Whistleblowers [Vol.2].md>) 7:01-7:25)*
- Gerb's context cases: the 1958 Lock Raven Reservoir Blue Book case (Alvin Cohan and Philip Small saw a roughly 100 ft egg-shaped object over the bridge, their car stalled, a white beam burned their skin), 3.5 miles from another Nike Ajax site (W-93, per Gerb); and 29 September 1958 at W-93, where Pvt. Jerome Scanland and Sgt. Ferris saw a teardrop-shaped, luminous green object land about 1.5 miles away, leaving broken branches and scorched earth, which the Air Force explained as repair trucks welding. Gerb cites J. Allen Hynek's The UFO Experience for the first. *([gerb/whistleblowers-2](<../raw/gerb/UAP Gerb - 2024-05-26 - UFO Whistleblowers [Vol.2].md>) 7:25-9:33)*
